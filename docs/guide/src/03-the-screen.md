# The screen

![The screen with its five parts numbered](img/screen-marked.jpg "The screen during the 1912 watch, three seconds after the bells. 1 the readouts; 2 the views; 3 the Bridge, on a watch only; 4 the damage diagram; 5 the panel and its tabs."){.stage}

## 1 · The readouts

The box at the top left names the ship and the night, gives the status, the ship's time, five readouts and the hull's
gauge. Under them one line carries the watch before the ice ("Bells 3 s ago, no order yet") and the forecast after it.
Two more kinds of line join them late in the night: the mark of where she would break, and, once she has broken (on a
damage preset, once she has foundered), a line for each of her pieces as it falls.

| Readout | What it shows |
|---|---|
| The time | Ship's time. On a watch it shows the seconds, with the time since the bells or the touch beside it. On a damage preset, the time since the collision. |
| {{:Trim}} | Degrees down by the head (the bow) or by the stern. |
| {{:List}} | Degrees down to port or to starboard. |
| {{:Water}} | Tons of sea water aboard. |
| {{:Inflow}} | Tons a minute coming in. |
| {{:Draft fwd/aft}} | Metres of hull under water at the bow and at the stern. |
| {{:Hull}} | The bending moment of her hull where it is greatest over boiler room 1, in GN m, on a bar that marks what the studies say her hull could bear, @@gauge.low@@ to @@gauge.high@@ GN m: teal below that range, amber within it, red past it. Chapter @@ch.after-the-ice@@. |
| {{:She would break here, now}} | A red line under the gauge, from the moment her bending and shear somewhere along her reach what her hull could bear there: the station, the space it lies in, and the ship's time. On a watch she breaks there and the watch ends; on a damage preset nothing breaks. Chapter @@ch.after-the-ice@@. |
| {{:Depth}} · {{:Speed}} · {{:Pressure}} · {{:Water}} · {{:Under}} | After she breaks (on a damage preset, after she founders), a line for each piece of her as it falls to the bed: its depth, its speed, the sea's pressure and temperature there, and the time since it went under. Chapter @@ch.after-the-ice@@. |

The status is one coloured word or two. On a watch: {{:On watch}}, {{:Ice ahead}}, {{:On the ice}}, then
{{:Clear}} if she passed it. Otherwise: {{:Intact}}, {{:Afloat}}, {{:Flooding}}, "Over bulkhead" and its letter when
water pours over a bulkhead's top, and at the end {{:Breaking}} on a watch, which ends where she breaks, or
{{:Foundered}} on a damage preset. From the touch on, a strip under the views counts the
boats: how many are away, the people in them, the places that went empty.

## 2 · The views

The buttons at the top right move the camera. Drag the 3D view to turn it, scroll or pinch to zoom, and drag with the
right button or with Shift held to move sideways. From the Bridge, the crow's nest and the boat, dragging looks about.

| Button | The view |
|---|---|
| {{Quarter}} | Off her starboard bow, from above. The damage presets open here. |
| {{Profile}} | Her starboard side, level with her. |
| {{Below}} | From under the sea, looking up at her bottom. |
| {{Plan}} | From straight above. |
| {{Astern}} | From astern and above, looking where she is going. A watch opens here. |
| {{Bridge}} | From her navigating bridge, looking ahead: what the officer of the watch saw. |
| {{Crow's nest}} | The lookouts' eye on the foremast, about @@eyes.nest@@ m above the water (a guess), carried by her pose. |
| {{Boat}} | The eye of someone in a lifeboat lying @@eyes.boat@@ m off her port beam, @@eyes.boatH@@ m above the sea. The boat stays where it is while she moves on or goes down; the wheel or a pinch brings it nearer or farther, and the button again lays it off her beam once more. |
| {{Screws}} | Under the sea off her starboard quarter: the rudder, the screws and their shafts. |
| {{Director}} | Hands the camera to the night: at the bells, the touch, the first boat, each rocket, her last minutes and the moment she breaks it chooses the view and holds her own pace for a few seconds, with a small caption of the ship's time. Any view button takes the camera back. |
| {{X-ray}} | A see-through hull, showing the water in each space (and in each pocket, its own surface while it fills), the bulkheads and their letters, and the engines. On at the start. |
| {{Night}} | The night as a person there would have seen it. Night and X-ray take turns: choosing one turns the other off. |
| {{Wake}} | On a watch, her wake as the model makes it: the waves her hull raises and the white water where they break and in her screws' races (below). On at the start, but on a phone. |
| {{Columns}} | The vertical columns the model adds up, one box each where it holds water, and a box of its own for each pocket. |
| {{Follow}} | Keeps the camera on her as she moves and settles. |
| {{Lights}} | Draws her lights. They are the model's: they fail when her dynamos do. |
| {{Walk}} | Puts you on her decks, an eye 1.7 m above E deck amidships, to walk her while she sinks, on the model's own water. The button again, or Esc, brings you out (chapter @@ch.after-the-ice@@). |

While she falls to the bed, four more views head a strip at the right of the stage; Quarter, Profile, Below, Plan and
Astern follow her bow, and Screws her stern; and the zoom reaches out to 6.5 km (chapter @@ch.after-the-ice@@).

## 3 · The Bridge

The wheel, the engine-order telegraph and the instruments, over the lower part of the view. It appears on a watch
only. Chapter @@ch.the-bridge@@ takes it in turn.

## 4 · The damage diagram

Her profile in the ship's own frame, from the after peak (AP, at the left) to the forepeak (FP, at the right): the
fifteen bulkheads lettered A to P, and the compartments between them. Brass tops stand to E deck, teal ones to D deck.
The cyan line is the sea's surface as she lies, so it tilts as she trims. Water fills each compartment as it comes in,
and the holes show as red marks. The two pockets inside her compartments, boiler room 5's bunker and the forepeak's
tank (chapter @@ch.how-it-works@@), fill on their own, each in its own place.

The {{Run}} tab reads from the top. While no watch is open it begins with {{:Start here}}: {{Take the watch}},
{{Watch the 1912 orders}}, and {{the Evidence tab}} for what the simulator is and what it is not. Under it stands
{{:Time}}, the clock's pace and its bar back to any minute (chapter @@ch.scenarios-and-what-ifs@@). The watch's own
section comes next, with everything a watch adds under it, and then the scenario cards.

At the foot of the panel, under whichever tab is open, a line names the main sources, and under it stands the mark
"Early access, a work in progress", with three links: the User Guide (this guide), Report a fault (the repository's
Issues on GitHub) and the source code. The site's version and its licence, MIT, close the line: site v@@site.version@@
in this build. In the desktop app the three links are left out, since its window opens no links: the mark, the version
and the licence remain.

## The night, and the eyes of people who were there

{{Night}} draws what a person there would have seen: a clear, moonless, flat-calm night, stars to the horizon and
mirrored in the glassy sea, a black sea, her own lights. The berg is a dark mass a little paler than the sky, with no
white water at its foot, which is why it was seen so late. Her lit ports shine in the water. A port stays lit while
its section's circuit holds and her dynamos feed it. Under the sea it burns on, drawn dimmer, until its section's fuses
blow or the dynamos stop (chapter @@ch.her-systems@@). All of it is appearance, chosen in the code and marked there;
X-ray is how the physics is read.

## Her wake

On a watch the sea round her is the model's, not a drawing. Her hull raises a field of waves as it moves, worked out
step by step from her speed, her turning and her drift; and white water is made where those waves are too steep to
stand, in a sheet off each bow, and in the races of her three screws, as much as each shaft's thrust makes. The white
water stays on the sea where it was made, spreads and dies away, so what she did is written on the water behind her.
Ring {{Stop}} and the white road astern fades as her screws' races die; ring {{Full}} astern and the wing screws throw
their races forward along her sides. Nothing of Kelvin's wedge is drawn by rule: the V of waves either side of her
track, the waves across it and the lopsided wake of a turn come out of the field.

![Her wake from above at 22½ knots](img/wake-plan.jpg "From above at 22½ knots, a few seconds after the bells and before any order: the V of her waves either side of her track, the white water off her bows and in her screws' races."){.half}
![Her wake after the 1912 turn, from above](img/wake-turn.jpg "From above, drawn back, when the encounter on the 1912 orders is over: her turn written in white water on the sea behind her, the berg just off her track."){.half}

{{Wake}}, with the display switches, turns it off and on. It is on from the start, but on a phone, where it waits to be
asked for. Off, the page draws what it drew before: a ribbon from her stern, a bow wave and Kelvin's lines. They stand
in as well for a watch's first seconds, while the field is being made, and whenever the clock runs at {{10×}} or
faster, quicker than the field can follow. A damage preset lies stopped and has no wake.

Where the waves are and how high, and where white water is made, are the model's. How much white water each cause
makes and how long it lasts are estimates, marked in the code (chapter @@ch.how-it-works@@). The drawing is appearance:
the sheen and shade on the slopes, the lines of equal height every quarter of a metre in the X-ray look, the broken
edges of the foam. In the Night look her own lights show the white water close about her; farther off it is a darker
road on the black sea.

## The ship heard

With {{Sound}} ticked, the ship has a sound of her own beside the bells, the telegraph's gong and the engines' beat. Each
sound's loudness follows a number in the model; only the timbres are the page's own.

- **Her own wind and the sea along her side** grow as the square of her speed. On a flat calm the only wind on her is
  her own, so when her engines stop the night goes quiet.
- **The engines** rumble with the revolutions of the wing shafts.
- **The ice** grinds with the force of each step of the contact, from the first blow to the end of the grind.
- **The water coming in** rushes with the tons a minute coming in.
- **Her structure** creaks as her trim changes.
- **The ship's bell** strikes the half hours by her clock.

Each view hears its own mix: the wind and the bell on the Bridge and in the crow's nest, the engines and the water below
the waterline, everything faint from the boat. {{The ship at sea: volume}} sets the level. The sound is silent when
switched off or paused, or once she has broken or foundered, and it fades as the clock runs faster than her own pace.
Nobody has tuned the levels by ear yet. The men's voices on the bridge are not part of it: they have a switch of their
own (chapter @@ch.the-bridge@@).

## On a phone

The same model and the same controls. The readouts keep {{:Trim}}, {{:List}}, {{:Water}} and the hull gauge; the
view buttons scroll sideways; the Bridge sits between the readouts and the damage diagram, and on a short screen gives
way to the end card while it is up and to the walk while you walk. The panel becomes a sheet
along the bottom: tap a tab to open it, and tap the tab again or {{⌄}} to close it. On the first screen the Run tab
reads "Start", and beckons until it is first opened. After she breaks or founders, the
strip of her fall stands at the right edge in the room the readouts and the Bridge leave it, with the boats' count to
its left, below the view buttons.

## The clock

The clock's controls stand under {{:Time}}, at the top of the {{Run}} tab: under {{:Start here}} while no watch is
open, and above the watch's own section on a watch, so that they are in reach without scrolling. {{Pause}} stops the
clock and {{Play}} starts it again; [[Space]] does the same. {{Restart from collision}}, or [[R]], starts the run
again with the damage it has now; on a watch the button reads {{Restart this watch}}. The speed buttons
run the model {{1×}}, {{10×}}, {{60×}}, {{240×}} or {{900×}} faster than real time. The tick box {{Stern light}}
shows the light at her stern: photographs suggest she had one, though the rules of 1910 did not ask it of a steamer.

Under the speed buttons, the bar {{:Back to}} goes back to any minute of her time already played. The page keeps the
whole run as it plays, every minute of her time (every fifth minute on a phone), which takes about 50 MB of memory
for a whole night, as the hint under the bar says. Drag the bar, or press [[,]] for a minute back and the full stop (.)
for a minute on, with Shift for ten. Beside {{:Back to}} stands the minute, with the ship's time on a watch and on the
1912 damage. The run is paused there, with her lights and the reports to the bridge put back at once as they were at
that minute, without a notice: no report is said again. Press {{Play}} and it goes on exactly as it went: on a watch
the orders given after that minute are given again on their steps, and the watch comes to the same end with the same
fingerprint. On a watch you can give an order
there instead, and the night goes your new way: the orders and the minutes kept after that moment are dropped, and
the page says so once. A hole made there with the brush drops only the minutes kept after it: no man on the bridge
gave it, so the night's own later orders still come. A damage preset keeps no log, so what you change there by hand
drops the minutes kept after it in the same way: a hole, an opening closed, a door or the fires. That change's own
notice says so, once. Only the minutes before she is gone are kept: her fall to the bed is not.

A damage preset has no watch, so the boats, the reports and help do not appear on it; the hull gauge, the mark of where
she would break, the card at the end and her fall to the bed do. When a preset founders the card says when, and when
she lies steady with water aboard it says she floats; {{This run again}} starts it afresh. The 1912 damage founders at
@@preset.founderClock@@, @@preset.founderMin@@ minutes after the collision. After the flag the clock runs on through
her fall, and once both pieces lie on the bed the play button reads {{On the bed}} (chapter @@ch.after-the-ice@@).

## The Damage tab

- **{{:Tool}}.** {{Look}} leaves the hull alone. With {{Punch hole}}, tap the hull below the waterline to open a
  hole there; dragging still turns the view. With {{Scrape}}, drag along the hull to open a line of holes, as the ice
  did. Picking a tool turns the X-ray hull on. The tools work on a watch too (below).
- **{{:Hole size}}.** The slider runs from 0.02 m² to 5 m², and names the size: smaller than a sheet of paper, about
  one A4 sheet, a few sheets, a torn plate, a ram or a mine.
- **{{:Openings}}.** The holes, by compartment and side, with their depth, their area in square feet and the tons a
  minute coming in: on a watch, the ice's and yours alike. On a damage preset the × at a row's end closes those holes,
  and {{Remove all openings}} closes them all; the water already aboard stays. On a watch the holes are part of the
  night: {{Remove all openings}} closes none, and to have none you start the watch again.
- **Holes on a watch.** A hole you make on a watch is an order of the watch's own. It stands in the watch's log at its
  step, the forecast after the ice takes it in, and a link to the watch replays it. No man on the bridge gave it, so it
  does not take the watch over: the night's own orders, the 1912 orders or a replay's, go on. Nobody on the bridge is
  told of it: the men learn of the water only as the reports bring it (chapter @@ch.after-the-ice@@), and so do the
  crew's minds. On a night holed before any touch, the bridge's reports begin when water is first noticed in
  a room, and that room's report comes by its own route. Each hole of a scrape is an order of its own, and a link
  carries at most 600 orders: past that, it opens the night without them. Once she is gone, nothing more can be holed.
- **{{:Before the collision}}.** The tick box gives her the list to port of the night, from her emptied starboard
  coal bunkers (on the 1912 damage about 1.6°, as calibrated; Halpern estimated 2°), and with it the one bunker the model holds as a space of its own: the empty starboard half of boiler
  room 5's forward bunker, which takes the sea in first and gives way later (chapter @@ch.how-it-works@@). It applies
  when the run restarts; a restart of the 1912 damage keeps her pumps and her list as built.
- **{{:Her rooms, and when the water came}}.** Type a room's number as the plans print it ("C 102", "#139"), or a
  room by its name ("the Turkish bath"), and pick it from the list: her deck plans' rooms, read into sourced tables
  that load the first time the tab opens, under a line crediting Bruce Beveridge's deck plans as Encyclopedia Titanica
  publishes them. A room picked folds the list away and brings its card into view. For the room picked, the page marks
  it on the damage diagram. It says when
  the water first came to each of the room's pieces, in words such as "water on C deck between bulkheads K and L, port
  side, at 2:17 am". It also says how deep the water stands over the deck there now. "First came" is the first time
  that piece's water stands a centimetre deep over its floor, a guess at what counts. {{Walk from here}} puts you on her
  deck at that room, to walk from it (chapter @@ch.after-the-ice@@); it is not offered on the orlop, where the walk does
  not go, nor once she has gone. {{Clear}} lets it go. A room's water is its piece of the model's own: a deck's space
  in one compartment, on one side, never the water at one cabin's door. So a stateroom in a block has its block's
  pieces and times. The times are taken on her whole seconds, at most three quarters of a second after the water came,
  on her own hull from the start of any run (not on Olympic's or Britannic's).
- **Who was booked there.** Where the Cave list of 1912 booked first-class passengers into a stateroom, the room's card
  names them as the list prints them, each line with its staterooms: the lines under the number you picked come
  first, at most eight, then how many more. The Cave list is a proof of the White Star Line's printed list of
  first-class passengers, with the staterooms set against the names, found on the body of one of her saloon stewards.
  It was printed before she sailed, so it gives the staterooms as they were booked: late bookings and some who sailed
  are missing, some it names did not sail, and it cannot say who was in a room that night. The card says as much
  under the names, in the list's own caveat. Second and third class stay unnamed: no list of their staterooms is used.
  A name finds a room too. Type "Allison", say: where no room has that name, the page lists the people of that name
  the Cave list books, each with the room it gives, and picking one picks that room at its first number.

## The Bulkheads tab

![The Bulkheads tab at 2:09 am](img/bulkheads.png "The Bulkheads tab at 2:09 am on the 1912 damage: the time water first flowed over each bulkhead."){.side}

- **{{:Watertight doors}}.** {{Close all from the bridge}} drops the doors at the tank top from the bridge. The switch
  works by electromagnets, so it needs current from the dynamos; a door falls shut in about @@doors.fall@@ seconds.
  Without current the switch does nothing. A float under each room's floor still drops the doors into it when water
  comes in, and a lever at each door works it by hand. {{Open all}} raises the doors by hand. A scenario's own open
  doors are held open. On the 1912 watch her engineers raise the doors aft of boiler room 4 themselves, as the record
  has it (chapter @@ch.her-systems@@).
- **{{:Bulkhead height}}.** {{As built}}, {{All to D deck}} or {{All to B deck}}: raising the bulkheads is the
  classic what-if.
- **{{:The fifteen bulkheads}}.** For each bulkhead: its letter, the compartments it stands between, the deck its top
  reaches, its doors, and the time water first flowed over it. A door reads {{Open}}, {{Closed}} or {{Falling}}; click
  it to work it by hand. On a watch every door order goes in the log, like the helm's.

## What-ifs, and what the model makes of them

Each of these changes the 1912 damage. The page makes the first four: the × beside an opening's row on the {{Damage}}
tab, and the bulkhead height. The last is in the note below. The model's answers come from the repository's validation
run, the same run the {{Evidence}} tab shows:

:::gen validation
titanicNoBR5 | No damage in boiler room 5 | Wilding: she still sinks, slower
titanicFour | The damage stopping at hold 3 | Four compartments: she floats
titanicD | Every bulkhead to D deck | Wilding: it would not have saved her
titanicB | Every bulkhead to B deck | Wilding: she would still go down
titanicDoors | The watertight doors left open | Debated
:::

:::note Doors left open: the model and the page
The validation run holds every door open from the collision on, as a scenario holds its own open doors, with their
floats unarmed. Held open, she sinks later in this model, not sooner, because the water spreads aft low down instead
of tipping the bow: *@@val.titanicDoors@@*, against *@@val.titanic@@* with the doors shut. In the page, {{Open all}}
raises the doors by hand, but each door's float drops it again once water stands @@doors.float@@ m deep beside it. On the
1912 damage the doors of bulkheads D, E and F fall shut within minutes, and she founders as if they had never been
opened. The page cannot repeat the validation's case.
:::

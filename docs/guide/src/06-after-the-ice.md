# After the ice

:::lede
The encounter takes a minute or two; what follows took two and a half hours, and most of it could only be watched.
Four readers of the watch follow the rest of the night. Each reads the model and keeps an account of its own, and none
changes the night: a watch has the same fingerprint with all of them as with none.
:::

They appear on a watch only, as sections of the {{Run}} tab and as marks on the stage. Their orders go in the watch's
log with the helm's, so {{Replay my watch}} and a link from the card replay them too. Each takes the watch over as the
wheel does: on the 1912 orders it drops those still to come, and the watch is yours from then on.

## Reports to the bridge

![The reports to the bridge on the Run tab](img/reports.png "The Run tab forty minutes after the touch on the 1912 orders: the orders to send a man to look, and the reports, newest first."){.side}

notice it and brought to the bridge in the time that took, in his words: the carpenter after sounding, the mail clerks,
the chief engineer by messenger, the officer of the watch by eye, the commutator read on the bridge. The section
{{:Reports to the bridge}} lists them, newest first, and a notice shows each as it comes.

engineer. Each man takes the time his errand took; a button stays grey while he is out.

## The boats

![The boats on the Run tab](img/boats.png "The section on the boats eighty-five minutes after the touch on the 1912 orders: what the two parties of officers are doing, and the three orders."){.side}

Twenty boats with @@boats.places@@ places for the @@boats.aboard@@ people aboard. The model moves places and minutes, not
persons: the boats are uncovered, swung out, loaded and lowered by two parties of officers and hands, one to a side, at
the rates the record gives, and people come forward to them the more readily the more she is down by the head. It names
nobody and draws no person.

From the touch on, a strip on the stage counts {{:Boats}} away, the people in them, the places that went empty and, at
the end, the people in the water. The section {{:The boats}} on the {{Run}} tab gives the same counts ({{:Boats away}},
{{:In them}}, {{:Places empty}}, {{:Still aboard}}, {{:In the water}}), a line on what each party is doing, the table
{{:Boat by boat}}, and three orders:

- **{{Uncover the boats now}}.** Otherwise the officers order it themselves, @@boats.uncoverAfter@@ minutes after a touch
  that lets the sea in, and call the people to the boats @@boats.call@@ minutes later.
- **{{:Who may board, port side}}** and **{{:Who may board, starboard side}}**: {{Women and children only}},
  {{Women and children first}} or {{Fill her}}. A watch starts with the night's own: @@boats.rulePort@@ to port,
  @@boats.ruleStarboard@@ to starboard.
- **{{:Lower a boat once she holds}}**: {{30 % (the model's rule for the night)}}, {{Half}}, {{Three-quarters}} or {{Full}}.

A boat is lowered by hand down the falls, more slowly when she is down by the head, when she lists against the boat, in
the dark, and at half the pace with more than @@boats.wayFull@@ knots of way on her; not at all above @@boats.wayNone@@
knots, nor from a station within @@boats.lowStation@@ m of the sea. An order that cannot be carried out says why.

![The port boats at night, seen from a boat off her beam](img/boats-night.jpg "Eighty-five minutes after the touch, at night, from a boat lying off her port beam: the boats already away lie off her side."){.stage}

On the 1912 orders, @@boats.within@@ of the @@boats.timed@@ boats lowered from the davits go within ten minutes of the
record's time, and @@boats.modelIn@@ people are in them against the record's @@boats.recordIn@@. Outside the ten minutes
(minutes early or late): @@boats.outside@@. At her end the strip reads @@boats.away@@ of @@boats.count@@ boats away,
@@boats.inBoats@@ in them, @@boats.empty@@ places empty and @@boats.water@@ in the water.

:::gen boats
:::

The model's fitted numbers are @@boats.fitted@@: how readily people came forward, below and above a trim of about
@@boats.jumpTrim@@ degrees, and the minutes a party gives a boat before it lowers her. The rest are the record's rates or
named guesses.

## Help: the wireless, the rockets, the other ships

The section {{:Help: the wireless, the rockets, the other ships}} gives the wireless (calling or silent, and why), the
ships that have answered and when each expects to be with her, and the rockets left. Its three orders are
{{Send the distress call}}, {{Fire a rocket}} and {{Work the Morse lamp}}.

With no order of yours the bridge does as it did in 1912: the captain orders the call @@help.orderMin@@ minutes after
the touch (@@help.orderClock@@), the operators send it at @@help.callClock@@ once they have her position, and
@@help.rockets@@ rockets go up from @@help.firstRocketClock@@ to @@help.lastRocketClock@@, of the @@help.stock@@ in the
locker aft. The wireless runs on her dynamos; when they stop, the operator throws in the battery, whose reach is
about @@help.battery@@ miles against some @@help.reach@@ on the main set at night.

## Walking her decks

{{Walk}}, at the end of the row of display buttons, puts you on her decks: an eye 1.7 m above E deck amidships, in her
own frame, so that her heave, her trim and her list carry you while down stays the world's. As she goes down by the
head the deck ahead becomes a hill and the horizon tilts. The W, A, S and D keys walk, the arrow keys turn and walk, Q
and E take you down and up a deck where there is one, Shift runs and Esc leaves; drag to look about. On a phone a pad
of buttons, each named for what it does, does the same. While you walk the page holds her own pace, and gives yours back when you leave. When she
breaks (on a damage preset, when she founders) the walk ends.

What you meet is the model's: her side where her hull is; her watertight bulkheads on every deck below their tops, with
their doors open to you only while the model has them open; and the water of each of her 64 spaces at its own level,
the levels the X-ray hull draws, with the bunker's and the peak tank's water where they stand. A pad at the lower right of the stage says where you are: the deck, the compartment
and the side, and the water at your feet, or that you are under it, or that her lights are out. Its buttons walk,
turn, go down and up a deck and leave. Water to the knees slows you.

![Walking E deck, looking forward](img/walk.jpg "Her E deck at @@at145.clock@@ on the 1912 orders, walked from amidships, looking forward. The pad at the lower right reads: @@walk.where@@."){.stage}

This is a first stage. Each deck is open floor from side to side, without its rooms. Her rooms are read from the deck
plans into sourced tables, all nine decks from the orlop to the Boat deck, each room checked against a 1912 original
where one could be reached (chapter @@ch.how-it-works@@), but the walk does not draw them yet. A key takes you up or
down a deck where you stand, without stairs. Where the model gives a bulkhead's doors no place, they stand on her
centreline, or a third of her half-breadth out to either side. The lines, the colours, the water's look and the dark
are appearance.

## The hull girder's gauge

![The readouts with the hull gauge inside the range](img/gauge.png "The readouts at @@gaugeIn.clock@@ on the 1912 orders: the hull gauge reads @@gaugeIn.hull@@, @@gaugeIn.hullWhere@@, and its bar has entered the marked range."){.side}

As her bow fills and her stern rises out of the water, her hull bends: her middle carries the weight of both ends. The
{{:Hull}} line in the readouts gives the bending moment where it is greatest over boiler room 1, where she broke, in
GN m, and says where it is. Its bar marks the range the studies give for what her hull could bear there, @@gauge.low@@ to
@@gauge.high@@ GN m: teal below it, amber within it, red past it. In the X-ray hull a band round her shows the same
station in the same colour. The gauge itself only reads her: it shows where the bending stands against what she
could bear. Where it reaches that, a watch breaks and ends, and on a damage preset the line only marks the moment
(below).

On the 1912 orders the gauge enters the range at @@gaugeIn.clock@@. On the 1912 damage (the preset, the model's
calibrated run), read on every step of the page's own run from 2:10 am:

:::gen gauge
:::

So the record's break, between @@gauge.recordLow@@ and @@gauge.recordHigh@@ degrees of trim, falls at @@gauge.at11@@ to
@@gauge.at17@@ GN m on this model's curve: past the top of the range, in the bar's red. The gauge enters the range at
about 5 degrees of trim and passes its top at about 10, so on this model the bending of her hull had outrun what the
studies say it could bear before she reached the record's attitude. Intact she bends at @@gauge.intact@@ GN m.

### Where she would break

![The readouts at the onset](img/onset.png "The readouts at @@onset.clock@@ on the 1912 orders, the first step her own loads reach her strength somewhere along her, where she breaks. The status reads “@@onset.status@@”, and under the hull gauge the red line reads: @@onset.line@@."){.side}

The studies' range is a figure for one place. The model also works out what her hull could bear at every station along
her, in bending and in shear, from her plates and the riveted joints between them, and holds the gauge's bending and
shear against it as she settles. The first time the two meet anywhere along her, a red line appears under the gauge:
{{:She would break here, now}}, then the station in metres forward (+) or aft (−) of amidships, the space it lies in,
and the ship's time. In the X-ray hull a red ring marks the place. Hold the pointer over the line for the figures: the
bending moment against what the hull could bear there, the shear against what it could carry, her trim and her list.

On a watch she breaks there. The watch ends on that step: the status reads {{:Breaking}}, the card on the stage
{{:She broke}}, and her fall begins from there (below). The forecast after the ice runs to the same step, so it gives
the time she breaks. In this build the onset comes over boiler room 2, about 5 metres forward of boiler room 1, where
the wreck parted: at @@onset.clock@@ on the 1912 orders and at @@presetOnset.clock@@ on the 1912 damage. On the
night itself, by the witnesses' accounts, she broke between about 2:15 and 2:19 am, and was gone at 2:20. But the onset comes
at about 7 degrees of trim, flatter than every published analysis of the break (five put it at 10 to 17 degrees, one at
23, the older picture at 45 or more; no witness gave an angle), and six to seven minutes before she founders on the
1912 damage, or on a watch carried on to its flag, where the record's own times leave from under a minute to about
five between her break and her going. Nothing in her strength or her weight was tuned to put it there. On a damage
preset nothing breaks there: the line marks the moment, the model carries on to her flag, and her last minute is a
plunge.

## Her fall to the bed

When she breaks the page does not stop. From that step the model takes her as two pieces, her bow and her stern, cut
where her own loads reached her strength, and carries each 3,790 m down to the bed: each moved by its weight, by the
water and the air in it, by the water it must push aside and the water's drag on it, and at the last by the mud. The
status stays {{:Breaking}}, the ship's time runs on, and the stage follows the fall. A damage preset runs on to her
flag instead and is cut there, over boiler room 1, its status {{:Foundered}}; from there it falls the same way.

- **The clock.** The fall runs at the speed you chose: at {{60×}} it is over in under ten seconds, at {{1×}} it takes
  its own time, some eight minutes for the stern. {{Pause}} stops it and {{Play}} goes on; when both pieces lie still
  that button reads {{On the bed}}. The ship's sounds stop as she breaks (on a damage preset, as she founders). On a
  watch the night goes on beside her fall: the wireless is silent from the break, the reports are done and the boats
  stay as they were left (their hours after are not yet in the model), but the other ships keep their courses, so the
  section on help still moves.
- **The strip.** Down the right edge of the stage, the sea from the surface to the bed, to scale: marks at 200 m, where
  daylight ends by day, and at each kilometre, the bed's depth at its foot, and each piece a mark at its own depth with
  its speed in metres a second. Tap a piece's mark to go with it.
- **The views.** Four buttons head the strip. {{Bow}} and {{Stern}} go with their piece from 400 m off: drag to turn
  the view, scroll or pinch to zoom. {{Bed}} is an eye on the bed below her, looking up as she comes down. {{Column}}
  shows the whole sea, surface to bed, in one picture from several kilometres off, each piece named beside it. Drag to look about
  from the bed, or to turn the column.
- **The readouts.** Under the gauge, a line for each piece: {{:Depth}}, the depth of its lowest point, its keel while
  it lies upright; {{:Speed}},
  through the water, in metres a second and in knots; {{:Pressure}}, the sea's at that depth, in atmospheres;
  {{:Water}}, here the water's temperature there; {{:Under}}, the time since the piece went under, or "afloat". The
  pressure and the temperature come from the model's own column of an April sea at the wreck.
- **The bed.** A net of 50 m squares about her and 500 m squares beyond, laid on the bed so that its slope, and her
  height above it, can be seen.
- **At rest.** Once both pieces are down, the wreck as surveyed is laid on the bed in brass outlines, with the boiler
  field as a ring round the point under the break. They are placed about the model's own point under the break, so
  that the model's answer and the wreck are seen together. The labels give the gap between the two bows in its two
  parts, how far on from the break each lies and which way her head was: "the model's bow: @@rest.bowOn@@ m on, her
  head where she broke @@rest.bowHdg@@ deg", and "the bow, as surveyed: about 0.8 km on, her head about north" (the
  bow's surveyed place names no source of its own, so it is given rounded).
- **The card.** As each piece comes to rest, a line on the card says when, how deep, and how far from where she broke:
  "The bow is on the bed at …", then "The stern followed at …". On the night of 1912, as recorded, each line sets the
  wreck beside it in brackets: the wreck's bow about 0.8 km from that point, the fall not fitted to it, and its stern
  about 150 m from it. Where the bow rests upside down after a hand-over at
  the flag (a damage preset, or a watch with `handover=flag`), a line in italics under the bow's says so, and that
  handed over where she breaks, as a watch is, both pieces land upright.

Each piece's place and attitude, its depth and speed, the sea's pressure and temperature, the bed and its slope, and the
moments each piece goes under, strikes the bed and comes to rest are the model's. The rest is appearance. In the X-ray
look the fall is drawn in the page's own colours. In the Night look, where nothing could have been seen at that depth,
a lamp hangs 150 m off the piece you are with ("a lamp that was not there") and the water darkens as she goes down. In
either look specks stand in the water so that her speed shows. Her torn ends are drawn flat, and the water drawn in her
is what the model held where she broke (on a damage preset, at the flag). No funnel rides down on either piece, as
none stands on the wreck. Her first funnel falls at the surface if the model's own sea reaches its seat before her end,
as survivors saw it fall under its own weight; the second also fell at the surface and the third at the break, so the
page draws none from the fall's first frame (when the fourth went is a bet). The foremast is laid back across the bow
when the bow strikes the bed, its crow's nest on it, as the wreck has it (Ballard, 1987), and the mainmast, which no
part of the wreck has, leaves the stern when the stern strikes. Those moments are the drawing's bets; the record has
the end state only.

:::note Read the fall as a first model
A watch lets her pieces go where she breaks: on the first step her own loads reach her strength, about 7 degrees by
the head and nearly upright, cut clean in two at that place. Each piece's air is one pocket, held where it lay then,
high in her dry spaces, and on the 1912 orders both pieces land upright, as the wreck's do. A damage preset, and a
watch with `handover=flag` in its address (a what-if), let her go at the flag instead, as the model leaves her there:
far down by the head, on her beam ends, and in one piece until that moment. There each piece's air rises through the
part of each slice the sea can fill, a stand-in until the air is followed space by space (held where it lay at the
flag, as in edition 1.5, it turned them over). Handed over at the flag, the stern lands upright, but on the 1912
damage and orders the bow comes to rest upside down, where the wreck's bow lies upright, and the card at her end says
so under the bow's line. Her bow lands farther from the point under the break than the surveyed bow lies, about 1.6 km
against the wreck's about 0.8 km on the 1912 orders, and part of that gap is her heading where she broke (the labels at rest give both).
Nothing in the fall is fitted to close it: the page shows what the model computes (chapter @@ch.how-it-works@@).

Her speeds are near the record's estimates. On the 1912 orders the bow strikes the bed at @@rest.bowStrike@@ m/s,
where Best and others (2000) put it between 5 and 10 m/s, and falls at @@rest.bowMph@@ mph on average on the way down,
against Ballard's estimate of 25 to 30. In the repository's trials (`out/fall_trials.txt`) the same pieces let go wholly
under and flooded, the bow at the record's trim and the stern standing steeply, land upright with nothing tuned: the
stern within @@trials.sternWithin@@ m of where the survey found it, the bow @@trials.bowAgainstPlace@@. The page cuts
her clean where she breaks: the engine's joint at the break, which bends and tears before it lets go, is a what-if that
no default run uses (chapter @@ch.how-it-works@@).
A damage preset with the 1912 sea under it, the 1912 damage or the five forward compartments, goes down from its flag
the same way, cut in two, 3,790 m to the bed. Britannic, which sank whole in 119 m of water, has no sea of her own
here: she keeps the page's old ending, and has no fall.
:::

## The Carpathia question

Could any order given after the ice have kept her afloat, and in one piece, until Carpathia was alongside? A search put the question to
this model (its note is `@@carpathia.note@@`): from a minute after the touch on the 1912 orders, it tried
@@carpathia.tried@@ nights of orders to the doors, the engines, the fires and the pumps, and kept the best. The note
answers in its own words: *@@carpathia.answer@@* Nothing here says she could have been saved: the answer is its
table, past the target on k of N models. The search is the model's answer as it stood on 23 November 2026, each night
ending where the watch hands her over to her fall, on the calibrated model and on @@carpathia.models@@ models in the
kept set's band: those whose own watch, run on to her foundering, founders 150 to 170 minutes after the touch.

- **What it buys.** The best orders found keep her @@carpathia.keeps@@ @@carpathia.gain@@ minutes longer: she
  @@carpathia.ends@@ @@carpathia.best@@ minutes after the touch, against @@carpathia.base@@. Carpathia is alongside
  @@carpathia.t1912@@ minutes after the touch as the night went, so she needed @@carpathia.need1912@@ minutes more.
  She needed @@carpathia.needCall@@ more had the distress call been made at once, and @@carpathia.needNear@@, the
  nearest of the three, had she also made five miles toward Carpathia. The best night falls @@carpathia.short@@
  minutes short of that. On none of the @@carpathia.models@@ models does it reach any of the three: past the target
  on 0 of @@carpathia.models@@, whichever target.
- **What it is made of.** Pumps, the engines full astern for half an hour, every fire drawn, and one door raised by
  hand, as the table below lists them. By the search's own count it pumps @@carpathia.pumped@@ tons overboard, against
  @@carpathia.pumpedWatch@@ on the 1912 watch, whose engineers' own rules now do much of what an order could. Its pump
  orders are ones the {{:Pumps}} section gives, and its fires order is {{Draw every fire}} (both in chapter
  @@ch.her-systems@@); its engine orders are the telegraph's (chapter @@ch.the-bridge@@); its door order is a click on
  bulkhead F's door on the Bulkheads tab (chapter @@ch.scenarios-and-what-ifs@@).
- **How firm.** With the model's guesses moved, at least half of the gain holds on @@carpathia.firmHalf@@ of the
  @@carpathia.models@@ models, and some of it on @@carpathia.firmAny@@.

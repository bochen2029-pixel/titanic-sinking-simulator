# The living crew: bring your own key. A brainstorm, v1.2

2026-11-23 CST, fork 2 ("the BYOK lane"). It adds to v1 and v1.1, which stay as written. This version records a
principle Bo set after reading them, and what it changes.

## 19. Bo's principle: the minds are the crew's, never the ship's

Bo, 2026-11-23 (his words as typed):

> "so my take is that titanic ship itself shouldn't be made into a "smartship" with its own "ship ai"... it should
> still be steered, commanded like back in 1912
>
> but its the officers, the captain, the crew etc that should be embued with LLM enfused intelligence and that sort
> of persona and roleplaying etc etc, and it is they, in-character, etc that drive/command/sail the ship and perform
> its operations"

## 20. What it means

1. **Nothing speaks as the ship.** There is no ship's assistant, no command terminal, no voice of the vessel. She is
   1912 machinery: a wheel, telegraphs, voice pipes, telephones, bells, a switch for the doors, a Marconi set.
2. **The player talks to people.** An order goes to the man who would carry it out, and he answers. A question goes
   to the man who would know. If nobody on board could know, nobody answers.
3. **Orders travel as they did.** The wheel takes seconds to go over. The engine room answers the telegraph. A man
   sent forward takes minutes to come back. Most of this is already the model's own: the rudder's rate, the engines'
   reversal, the errands' times in the reports reader.
4. **Each mind has a seat, and 1912 means.** An officer knows what he has seen, heard or been told, by the channels
   of the night. He can be wrong, late or unsure. That was already v1's third rule; it is now the first.
5. **Jev and an LLM are a man's judgement and a man's speech.** The page says "what Mr Murdoch was asked" and shows
   his doubt. It never says the ship decided.
6. **The player's own instruments stay the player's.** The X-ray look, the nervous-system view, the forecast and the
   kept set's bands are the modern viewer's, outside the fiction. "As the bridge knew it" is the fiction. No officer
   is ever given what only those views show.
7. **The optimizer is a research tool.** The Carpathia search knows the whole model. It is never a character, and no
   officer is handed its answers.

## 21. What it changes in v1

- **Mode G, "the docent", is no longer "ask the ship".** Two honest forms remain, and Bo chooses:
  - a guide outside the fiction, plainly modern, off by default: the User Guide that can answer back; or
  - nothing: a question is always asked of a person on board.
- **Mode A's wording.** The order line is an order given to the bridge. Its replies are the quartermaster's, the
  officer of the watch's, the engine room's. The first build already follows this.
- **A new mode J: take a station.** The roles turn round: the officers, in character, give the orders, and the player
  carries them out. At the wheel: "Hard a-starboard!", and the player must turn it the right way, by the 1912
  convention. As the fourth officer: "Go forward and see what damage there is", and the player walks there (the walk
  exists) and reports what he saw.
- **Mode C, the AI watch, is a watch of named men.** Each decision belongs to an officer within his own remit, and
  shows as his: Murdoch on the bridge at the collision, Lightoller and Murdoch at their boats, the chief engineer at
  his pumps, the captain for what only a captain orders.
- **The initiative the model already has stays where it is.** Boxhall going forward unasked, the carpenter sent for,
  the engineers' rules for the pumps: these are doctrine in the model today. The officers' judgement is added where
  doctrine is silent or the reports disagree, not in place of it.

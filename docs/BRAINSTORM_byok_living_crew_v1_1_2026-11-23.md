## 15. What Bo's own demos have already measured

[read: the public pages of TypeSafe, the A320 simulator and Jev Park, 2026-11-23]

**The flare.** On the approach, from 150 ft, Jev is asked about ten times a second which of four height readings to
believe.
- Its answer comes in about 0.1 s. If it is silent for 2 s, the simulator's own formula starts the flare.
- The site's server holds the key and asks only from fixed templates. The player brings no key.
- With instruments broken, Jev landed 4 to 8 of 10 approaches stable, and the formula none.
- The page says so itself: a twenty-line code voter did as well or better.

**Jev Park.** A car parks itself on a lot the player draws.
- Eyes about 4 times a second (a frame and up to seven close-ups); a planner about once a second; a referee when two
  cars block each other. Code steers and brakes.
- A run of about 65 s is about 260 calls and five cents. A vision call is about 5,000 tokens. <!-- leak-scan: allow-amount -->
- About 170 ms round trip from Dallas.
- Against the simulator's own truth: 83 % right on whether a space is empty, 99 % on its kind.
- The app shows the frame Jev was sent, and logs each request and answer.
- A small server holds the key under a daily budget. When the budget is spent, the page says so.

**TypeSafe.**
- Jev is in early access: developers come in from a waitlist.
- A choice takes up to 255 options; a score up to 10 levels.
- Verification and guardrails are among the uses it names.
- Clef is not TypeSafe's: it is the open-weight family that speaks the same API.

## 16. What that changes in v1

1. **Speed is proven for the reflex tier** (v1 sections 8 and 13). A tenth to a fifth of a second from Texas is
   measured in two of his own projects, several times a second. Deepgram and the LLMs are still unmeasured.
2. **Eyes are the cost** (v1 section 8). Text beats stay under cents a night. Four frames a second at about 5,000
   tokens each is about five cents a minute. So the lookout looks only on the approach, a few minutes, not all night:
   a whole night of eyes would be about $8 [derived]. <!-- leak-scan: allow-amount -->
3. **The floor is a rival, not only a fallback** (v1 section 10). The flare's own result says a short piece of code
   matched Jev. So every Jev decision earns its place on the bench first, against the plain doctrine.
   - Expect Jev to win where the input is perception or language: the lookout's frame, free speech to an order,
     reports that disagree.
   - Expect a rule to do as well where the input is one number crossing a line: "is it time to uncover the boats?"
4. **The public page has a proven path** (v1 section 9). Both demos use a small server that holds the key, asks only
   fixed questions and stops at a daily budget. So:
   - the app is where the player's own keys go;
   - the public page gets one or two modes as a budgeted demo with no key asked, on the page's own origin.
5. **Few players will hold a Jev key** (v1 sections 3 and 12), since it is early access. An LLM key and a Deepgram
   key are the common ones. Clef, or the budgeted demo, covers judgement for everyone else.
6. **Show the judgement from the first day** (v1 section 6). Both demos show what was asked and what was seen. The
   crew's panel should show each officer's last question, his probabilities, and his doubt as a bar.
7. **An aside for the app** (NH13). The flight simulator's 1.8 MB download runs in Edge's app mode, with no runtime
   shipped. With the model's own maths it would compute the same night. But it has no main process to hold a key, so
   the player's keys belong in the Electron app. A tiny keyless download could exist beside it [bet].

## 0. Summary

- **Main plant (high).** Five ballast-and-bilge pumps at 250 LT/h and three bilge pumps at 150 LT/h: 1,700 LT/h = 1,727 t/h = 0.468 m3/s = 28.3 LT/min [read, derived]. All steam-driven [read]; direct-acting reciprocating is my assumption, the usual kind in 1911 [bet]; the makers and type are not in any primary source I could open ("duplex" only in a forum post [search summary]).
- **Extra bilge capacity, usually missed (medium).** Each of the five large boiler rooms (BR6 to BR2; none in BR1) had a large duplex ash-ejector pump of about 150 LT/h (Wilding's estimate for those in BR4 and BR3 [read]; the other three taken as alike [bet]). Those in BR6 and BR4 were on the bilges; those in BR5, BR3 and BR2 shared a pump room with a 250 and reached the bilges only through a portable pipe [read + derived]. Bilge-connected total 2,000 LT/h (0.551 m3/s); 2,450 LT/h (0.675 m3/s) with the portable pipes [derived]. The four condenser circulating pumps (centrifugal) also had 18-in bilge injections, but only in the two engine rooms; their capacity was not found.
- **Where (medium-high).** 250s: pump rooms in BR5, BR3, BR2, and two in the reciprocating engine room. 150s: two in the reciprocating engine room, one in the turbine room. No pump in BR1, the holds, the electric engine room or the shaft tunnels, which have suctions only, nor in the peaks, which have not even those [read].
- **Reach (high for the arrangement, medium for its use).** Two independent 10-in bilge mains with cross-connections, valves worked by rods and wheels from the bulkhead deck; in principle any compartment could be pumped [read]. In practice holds 1 to 3 had only 3 to 3.5-in suctions (about 110 to 200 LT/h each at 2.5 m/s [derived]), their valves stood in the firemen's passage, which the iceberg opened within about 5 minutes [read], and the forepeak and after peak have no listed suction [read].
- **The night (high that pumping was tried; how many pumps ran is unknown).** BR5's pumps were on soon after the collision and its plates stayed dry until the bunker door burst at about 12:40 (T 60). BR4 had its own 150 and, by Wilding's account, BR3's 250 through the permanent pipes; its water still rose slowly from under the floor, about a foot by 1:20 (T 100). A 10-in leather suction pipe was carried forward from the shaft tunnel at about 12:45 (T 65) to add more pumping (for No. 5 in the Court's view, No. 4 in Wilding's). Nothing shows pumping in BR6 or the holds [read].
- **Pumps against the inflow (high).** Wilding: about 16,000 LT in about 40 minutes through about 12 sq ft, an average 400 LT/min; the pumps were 7 % of it [read + derived]. Small leaks were another matter: BR5's leak (a 3-in deck hose) was about 120 to 200 LT/h, under one 250 [derived]; BR4's seam about 380 LT/h (Halpern), about equal to the 400 put on it [read].
- **Effect (low).** The Court: with every pump working the engineers could not have prolonged her life appreciably [read]. My estimate for what was done: about +5 minutes (2 to 11) on the 160 [derived]. What-if, the whole plant led to BR5 and BR4 from the start: about 6 to 28 minutes, and never enough to stop her [derived].
- **Supply (read + bet).** Steam only; no pump depended on the dynamos [read]. Steam was kept up to the end (the lights burned to about 2:17, T 157), so in 1912 the pumps were limited by where they could draw, not by steam [derived]. For other runs: delivery = rated × f(p), f = 0 below about 30 psi and 1 above about 80 psi at the auxiliary main, linear between, and independent of the overboard head until stall [bet, on the physics of direct-acting pumps].

### 3.2 Compartment by compartment

- **Big holes (holds 1 to 3, BR6).** Once such a compartment is near the sea level outside, pumping it lowers its level only by dh = (Q / (Cd A))^2 / 2g. For one 250 (0.0688 m3/s) through 0.2 m2 at Cd 0.6, dh = 0.017 m [derived; the 0.2 m2 is a bet]. Pumping a big-hole compartment moves sea water through the ship and buys almost nothing; while it fills, it trims the fill rate by the pump rate, a few percent [derived].
- **BR5 (small hole).** The Court: the leak was only about the flow of a 3-in deck hose [read]. A 3-in hole 23.5 ft under the surface (counsel's figure, BI Day 4) passes v = sqrt(2 x 9.81 x 7.16) = 11.9 m/s through 0.00456 m2: 0.054 m3/s (196 LT/h) at Cd 1, 0.032 m3/s (118 LT/h) at Cd 0.6 [derived], under BR5's one 250. Barrett's dry plates fit. Mark Chirnside: BR5's ingress was at first "kept under control by the pumps" (https://markchirnside.co.uk/faq-would-higher-watertight-bulkheads-have-saved-titanic/) [read]. With the bunker door down, most of the leak went into the bunker, not to the pumps, until the door burst [derived from the Report].
- **BR4 (tiny seam).** Halpern's reconstruction assumes BR3's 250 and BR4's 150 on BR4 from 12:10; they "would have been about 467 tons" out by 1:20, leaving about 170 LT in the room; total inflow 637 LT in 100 minutes = 3.7 cu ft/s (0.105 m3/s, 380 LT/h) through about 0.079 sq ft (11.3 sq in, 0.0073 m2; a 5/16-in seam split over one frame space) at a 35 ft mean head [read]. On those numbers the 400 LT/h roughly held the inflow after 12:10 and the 170 LT is the first half hour's water [derived].
- **The Court** ("Observations"): the inflow into the five forward compartments, about 14 ft above the keel in ten minutes, was beyond anything the pumps could have handled; the leaks in BR4 and BR5 did not appreciably hasten the sinking; and even had all the pumps been got to work they "could not have saved the ship or prolonged her life to any appreciable extent" [read]. Wilding agreed in substance (BI 20374) [read].
- **Hackett and Bedford (1996), the Marine Forensic Panel (1997-98), Stettler and Thomas, TUHH (Dankowski 2013):** whether and how they modelled the pumps: NOT FOUND (papers not open; the summaries say nothing of pumps).

### 3.3 Minutes the pumps added (my estimate) [derived, low confidence]

- The pumps' only lasting effect is water removed where the holes were small (BR4, BR5); removing M early shifts the rest of the sinking by about M / W', where W' is the rate at which the ship was taking water when the pumping stopped mattering (the state is mostly the floodwater's mass).
- M: BR4, 467 LT by 1:20 (Halpern); plus 0 to 200 LT after 1:20 if the pumps ran on unattended [bet]; plus 0 to 150 LT in BR5 [bet]. So about 470 to 820 LT.
- W' between 1:20 and 1:50: from the forum's 75 LT/min (at T 40) up to about 200 LT/min [bet], rising as the bow went down.
- Shift: 470 / 200 = 2.4 min to 820 / 75 = 10.9 min. **About 5 minutes, range 2 to 11.** Consistent with the Court's finding that the pumps could not have prolonged her life appreciably.
- What-if, the whole plant (2,000 LT/h) led to BR5 and BR4 from T 5: in the first 90 minutes their small leaks cap what can be removed (500 to 580 LT/h together, about 800 LT); in about 40 minutes of overflow from forward, the rooms' own suctions (about 710 LT/h at 2.5 m/s) or the whole plant (33 LT/min) cap it at about 475 to 1,330 LT. M about 1,300 to 2,100 LT; with the same W' (75 to 200 LT/min), shift about 6 to 28 minutes. Never enough to stop her: the overflow ran at hundreds of tons a minute at the end.

## 4. Supply: steam, electricity, head

- **Steam pressure.** Boilers 215 psi; the dynamo engines took 185 psi; the pumps had steam from the auxiliary pipe (single-ended boilers and BR2, BR4, any boiler through the cross-connection) and, for the engine-room bilge pumps, also from the emergency pipe from BR2, 3 and 5 above E deck [read]. On the night: BR5's boilers blew their water off and its fires were drawn by about 12:30; BR4's fires were partly drawn by 1:20; the orders passed below were to keep steam up, then to draw the fires as far as possible [read]. The single-ended boilers were cold (plant sheet). The lights burned practically to the end and the emergency engine was running late [read], so BR2 and BR3 (and what was left) held the auxiliary lines near dynamo pressure until about 2:15 [derived]. In 1912 the pumps were never steam-starved; their limit was where they could draw.
- **How delivery depends on steam (direct-acting steam pumps; general, not Titanic-specific).** A direct-acting pump's water pressure is at most the steam pressure times the ratio of steam-piston to water-piston area, less friction; the pump slows as its load nears that limit and stalls at the balance; its delivery is swept volume times strokes a minute times a volumetric efficiency [search summary: Firgelli's Worthington-duplex and Dean pages, https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/mechanisms/worthington-duplex-pump and https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/mechanisms/dean-steam-pump; C&B Sales bulletin, https://www.candbsales.com/wp-content/uploads/AA-AA.pdf; Hydraulic Institute FAQ, https://pumpsandsystems.com/hydraulic-institute-pump-faqs-may-2007]. Discharging overboard against perhaps 5 to 15 m of water (7 to 22 psi) [bet], a bilge pump needs only a few tens of psi of steam: full speed down to perhaps 80 psi, stalling near 30 [bet].
- **Head.** Positive displacement: the reciprocating pumps' delivery does not depend on the overboard head until stall. If the forward discharges were above the load waterline (period practice [search summary]) and went under as the bow sank, the extra back-pressure is about 10 kPa a metre, small against about 1 MPa of steam [derived]. The centrifugal circulating pumps are different: their delivery falls with head along a curve (no curve found).
- **Suction.** All suctions are at the bottom, under the plates (strum boxes) [read], so a pump lowers the water only to the strum. The pumps stood at the floor of the rooms they drained, so suction lift was no limit for them [bet].
- **Electricity.** No pump needed the dynamos [read, as an absence]. What did: the stokehold fans (forced draught), stopped by order from about 11:55 [read]; the lights in the pump rooms (out for minutes in BR5 and BR4) [read]; the telephones and the bridge's door release (plant sheet). With the engines stopped, natural draught was enough for the small auxiliary load [bet].
- **When rooms flooded.** A steam pump has no electrics and can run submerged while steam reaches it [bet]; but the men left BR5 at about T 60 and BR4 at T 100, BR6's pump was abandoned at once, and no engineer survived to say whether the pumps ran on.

Rules [bet]:
- A zone's pumping = min(sum of the pumps assigned to it x f(p); the zone's suction limit; the main limit for pumps standing elsewhere). Take it from layer 0 (below E deck), from both sides in proportion to their water, and stop at the strum (leave about 0.1 m over the tank top).
- A pump in a room whose water stands more than about 1.5 m above the plates (about 2.3 m above the tank top; the plates are about 0.76 m up, Halpern) stops, unless a "ran on unattended" switch is set.
- Each pump needs a start time (a person opened its steam valve) and a suction assignment: the night's choices, not the plant's.

### 5.2 Steam, electricity, head

- f(p) = clamp((p - 30) / (80 - 30), 0, 1) with p in psi gauge on the auxiliary main (the best boiler room still connected and lit); for the ash-ejector pumps, built for a 150 lb jet, clamp((p - 20) / (60 - 20), 0, 1) [bet].
- In the 1912 run p stays at least 185 psi to the end (the lights), so f = 1; the law matters only when a run loses steam early.
- No pump on the dynamos. Keep their link to steam only.
- Ignore the overboard head for the reciprocating pumps. If ever needed: raise the stall pressure by the head's pressure (area ratio taken as 1) [bet].

### 5.3 What to expect, and tests

- 1912 as operated against no pumps: she founders about 5 minutes later (2 to 11) [derived]. A difference over about 15 minutes means the pumps reach too much (big-hole zones drained, or the suction or main limits skipped).
- Zone 5 stays below its plates (0.76 m above the tank top) until its bunker door bursts or the overflow from zone 4 arrives; zone 6 has about 0.3 m over the plates forward at T 100 (Cavell's foot; Halpern's 170 LT); zones 7 to 12 are dry at T 100.
- The model's 158.6 minutes was fitted without pumps: with them on, check the 1912 founder time against the record (157 to 160 minutes) and refit if it drifts out.

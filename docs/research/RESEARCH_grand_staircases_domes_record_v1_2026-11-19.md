## 0. Summary

- **The forward staircase** ran from the boat deck to F deck ("from the middle to the boat decks", The Shipbuilder 1911), "over 60ft. in height and 16ft. wide", between the first and second funnels, with three lifts in one trunk immediately forward of it from E to A deck [read]. Its plan size per deck is not in any source I could open; Wikipedia gives the boat-deck hall as 56 ft by 33 ft (citing Beveridge) and the flights as 20 ft wide projecting 17 ft (31.6 m2, unsourced) [read]. **It stands just aft of bulkhead F, over boiler room 4 (zone 6)**, centre about x +32 (range +27 to +40), its lift trunk at about bulkhead F: Halpern and Weeks put the E-to-F flight "just aft of WTB-F", Halpern puts lifeboat 8 (davits at 39F and 28F) alongside its boat-deck entrance, and on the wreck the swimming pool off the F-deck landing is behind a shut watertight door in the bulkhead [read; derived]. The plan's "+37 m, over bulkhead F" is close; the well itself feeds zone 6.
- **The after staircase** served A to C decks only, between the third and fourth funnels, immediately forward of the smoke room, just aft of where she broke [read]; about x -27 to -37, over the reciprocating engine room (zone 10) [bet].
- **Each dome had a second, outer glazing:** a glazed "skylight weather cover" on the roof, rectangular panes with a round scuttle at the centre, hinged sidelights in its faces (12 on Olympic's forward cover, fewer on Titanic's), and a duct into a fan-driven trunk (Bob Read, from plans and photographs) [read in his articles]. The forward cover sat on the roof of the boat-deck house; the after one on the boat deck. The dome was "of iron and glass" [read]; its plan size (20 ft diameter, one unsourced page), rise, glass and the cover's height (6 ft on the rigging plan, a forum lead I could not open) are not established.
- **Before the dome, the sea had five other ways into the forward stair hall:** D-deck gangway doors, two close together on each side, steel, 73 1/2 x 56 1/8 in, "closed by lever handles but had no rubber" (Report; the conservators of one door say it had a gasket); doors on C deck ("Exit doors through the ship's side ... abreast of this entrance", Report) and B deck (entry foyers with gangway doors, Wikipedia), both missing from the Report's own list of side doors; the A-deck vestibules behind the 192 ft screen; the boat-deck vestibules and arched windows [read]. On the record's trim and list, the port D-deck doors were under water from about T 110-130 (1:30-1:50), the port C and B levels about T 135 and T 148, the starboard D-deck doors about T 148, the boat deck at the stair about T 156-157 and the cover and dome last, about T 156-158, in the break-up's minute [derived, rough].
- **No witness saw or heard a dome break.** I read the testimony of every survivor who was on the forward boat deck at the end (Lightoller, Brown, Bride, Gracie, Woolner, Bright, Lucas, and Thayer through Gracie) and searched the whole of Gracie's 1913 compilation for dome, skylight, glass, staircase and lift: nobody mentions the dome. The stairway Lightoller saw the water "coming up" at 2:05 was "a little stairway ... just abaft the bridge ... for the use of the crew only" (BI 14024), not the grand staircase [read]. "The dome implodes and the sea pours down the staircase" is a reconstruction (Marschall, Lynch and Marschall 2003, Cameron's 1997 set, which really did flood that way), not testimony.
- **What the record does say about the stair:** water was running down the forward stair's E-to-F flight at about 12:45-12:55 (Wheat: "more than trickling ... not exactly a stream", BI 10965) [read]; the stair hall was a way up for people (Hart's parties of third-class women, about 12:30-1:10 [read in Gracie]), and probably until the end: Gracie's "mass of humanity" that "arose before us from the decks below" just as the water reached the bridge, between him (starboard, x about +43) and the stern, where the entrance's starboard vestibule stood 6-16 m aft of him [read; the route is my bet]; the bridge went under about 2:15 (T 155), the forward funnel fell about 2:17, she was gone at 2:20 [read].
- **The wreck:** the forward stair is an empty well whose dome void is the ROVs' way in; the woodwork is gone (floated out, eaten, or blown out at the bottom: all theories); the halls round it, the reception room's leaded windows and much panelling survive; ROVs reached D deck by it and later F deck; a pile of wreckage lies at D deck; fragments of a dome's iron frame lie in the debris field, said to be the after dome's [read, secondary].

**The noises.** Survivors heard crashing as she rose, and each said what he thought it was: Woolner, "sounded like the crashing of millions of dishes of crockery"; Beesley, as if things "had been thrown downstairs from the top of a house"; Bath Steward Rule, "the noise we heard was that of the boilers and engines breaking away" [read in Gracie, ch. III]. None names the dome, a skylight or glass.

**The lifts.** No testimony about the lifts after the collision was found, and no lift attendant testified. NOT FOUND.

**Did anyone see or hear a dome break?** No. I read the inquiry testimony of the survivors who were on the forward boat deck in the last ten minutes (Lightoller BI and, through Gracie, US; Brown; Bride; Gracie, US Day 11 and his book; Woolner; Bright and Lucas through Gracie; Thayer through Gracie) and had the whole of Gracie's 1913 book, with its reprinted statements of about forty survivors, searched for "dome", "skylight", "glass", "staircase", "stairway", "companion", "elevator" and "lift". Nobody mentions the dome or its cover. The stairway that Lightoller watched the water come up was a crew stair "just abaft the bridge". The water "gurgling up the hatchway forward" that Gracie heard was at the bridge. The picture of the dome bursting and the sea pouring down the grand staircase comes from reconstructions (section 6).

**Times of the sea at the forward boat deck, the bridge and the first funnel, by witness:** A deck at its forward end awash at about 2:05 (Lightoller, Woolner); the water up the crew stair onto the boat deck "almost immediately afterwards" (Lightoller); the boat deck forward awash and the water in the Marconi room by about 2:10 (Bride, Lightoller); the bridge under, the crow's nest at the water, about 2:15 (Brown, Gracie, Lightoller); the forward funnel's base and gratings under as Lightoller went in, and the funnel down about 2:17 (Lightoller, Gracie, Thayer); gone 2:20. **How fast:** Halpern and Weeks read the whole record as saying that everything happened very fast only in the last ten to fifteen minutes; A deck forward went from "at our feet" to nearly "boxed in against the ceiling" in about a minute (Woolner); Thayer's "fairly fast at an angle of about thirty degrees"; Lightoller's "very slowly until the end" for the stern.

### 10.5 What is fact, what is inference, and what is cinema in "the dome implodes and the sea pours down the staircase"

**Fact:** each staircase was an open well through several decks, lit by an iron-and-glass dome that sat under an outer glazed weather cover; the forward well stood just aft of bulkhead F over boiler room 4 and the after one near the break over the reciprocating engine room; by the end both wells were wrecked, the forward one now an empty shaft with the dome and its cover gone, and fragments of a dome's iron frame lie in the debris field; the forward boat deck and the bridge went under at about 2:15, the forward funnel fell at about 2:17 and she was gone at 2:20. **Inference:** that the forward dome failed when the sea reached it, a minute or two after the bridge went under, because the boat-deck house round it, its doors and its arched windows were already under, and glass that thin cannot hold a sea above an air space; that some of the staircase's woodwork floated out of the well, as the 1912 floating wreckage and the Halifax pieces suggest for the after staircase; that the crowd Gracie saw came up the forward stair. The record also says the sea had other, lower ways into the stair hall first: the D-deck gangway doors, which had "no rubber" and on the port side were under from about 1:30-1:50 on the record's list, the B- and C-deck side doors if they existed, the A-deck screen and vestibules, and the boat-deck vestibules and windows; so by the time the sea stood on the roof the hall below was probably already flooding, and the dome may have failed with little head across it, or been carried away by the wave or the falling funnel. **Cinema:** a dry, lit staircase full of people that the sea enters only through a bursting dome, the water falling as a column from the top. No survivor saw or heard the dome go; the image is Marschall's painting and Cameron's 1997 set, which was built strong and flooded through its dome for the camera, and broke free and floated up, as he believes the real one did.

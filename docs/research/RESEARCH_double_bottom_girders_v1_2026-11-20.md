  report of a research subagent it briefed at 13:42 CST (Claude Opus 5.5); the last section is the lane's arithmetic.
- **Why:** at the 1912 onset's station (x -10.93 m, boiler room 2, hogging), once the side has torn the double bottom is

  tear's top turns on it. The model's double bottom there rests on the panel's model (four side girders a side, 1 in

## Summary

1. **The count: both sources were right, about different things.** Four intercostal side girders a side lie between the
   centre keelson and the watertight longitudinal 30 ft out (which Harland and Wolff called the margin plate); a fifth
   intercostal lies outboard of it, in the wing tank. A side has six webs outboard of the keelson. The Shipbuilder's
   "five intercostal tank girders amidships on each side of the centre keelson" counts the four and the wing one;
   *Anatomy of the Titanic*'s four counts those inside the 30 ft member. The model has five webs a side: four side
   girders (its outermost standing for the 30 ft member) and a short margin plate at the tank top's edge.
2. **Depth:** 63 in at the centre line; Britannic's 30 ft member is 54 in deep, so the webs get shallower outboard.
3. **Thickness: nothing for Olympic or Titanic.** Britannic (Engineering, 1914): the vertical keel 1 in (as the Olympic
   class's); the double bottom's other vertical members 5/8 or 3/4 in (the scan's fraction is blurred); the margin
   plate and the wing intercostal 3/4 in. The model has the panel's 1 in throughout.
4. **The intercostals' connections: nothing for the ship.** Period practice joins side girders to the floors by lugs or
   angles (Holms 1904: double lugs with four rivets, after single lugs with two had failed); double angles are named for
   the centre girder (Lloyd's 1909-10). The model's double riveting stays a bet; the subagent leaned to single (about
   60 %, low confidence).
5. **Holes:** none in the centre keelson amidships (watertight, caulked) nor in the 30 ft member (watertight); the
   intercostals' unknown (Lloyd's allows manholes in side girders; Nicol 1909 has one lightening hole a cell).
6. **Floors** on every frame, 36 in apart; web frames 30 in deep on every third frame in the boiler rooms.
7. **For the ligament** (the last section): on the sources' layout with Britannic's 3/4 in plate the double bottom
   carries 19.93 MN, within 2 % of the onset's shear (above it list free, 0.2 % below it held). The coin's edge
   survives the research; 5/8 in plate or a weaker riveting of the 30 ft member tips it to letting go.

## Period practice (not the ship's own)

- **Lloyd's Register, Rules 1909-10** (archive.org, HECRAR1910) [read, digest]: cellular double bottoms have intercostal
  side girders well connected to the floors and to both bottoms (no riveting rule in that paragraph; Table 8 not
  reached); manholes are allowed in side girders, none in the centre girder over the midship three-quarters of the
  length; middle-line intercostals take double angles to the floors.
- **Holms, *Practical Shipbuilding*, 1904** (archive.org) [read, digest]: side girders join the floors by double lugs
  with four rivets, the older single lug with two having failed (a single vertical lug is sometimes used); girders'
  end joints often single-riveted; the tank top thicker under the boilers.
- **Nicol, 1909** (archive.org) [read, digest]: one lightening hole through each intercostal plate a cell.
- **Walton, *Steel Ships*, 1901** (archive.org) [read, digest]: intercostals fixed by angles or flanges, with manholes
  at intervals.

## What it does to the ligament (the lane's arithmetic) [derived]

The rule is `S.hinge`'s for a double-bottom web: the least of its shear yield (151 MPa), its joint's tension strength
over sqrt 3, and its buckling as a panel between floors 36 in apart. Inputs: the webs at Fig. 14's places [derived];
depths tapering linearly from 63 in at the keel to 54 in at the 30 ft member, the taper extended to the wing
intercostal at 38 ft (51.7 in) [derived]; the keelson 1 in at the quadruple-riveted strength (as the model's); the 30
ft member at the quadruple strength, as the model's margin plate [bet]; the intercostals double-riveted [bet], unless a
row says otherwise. Demand: the joint's shear at the onset from the gauge, 19.61 MN list free, 19.98 held.

| Layout | Vs (MN) | V / Vs at the onset, list free (held) |
|---|---|---|
| V0, the model as built: four side girders a side and two margin plates, 1 in | 20.34 | 0.964 (0.982) |
| V1, the sources' layout at the panel's 1 in | 25.37 | 0.773 (0.787) |
| V4, V1 with the 30 ft member double-riveted | 22.15 | 0.885 (0.902) |
| V2, the sources' layout at Britannic's 3/4 in (keelson 1 in) | 19.93 | 0.984 (**1.002**) |
| V3, V2 with the four inboard intercostals 5/8 in | 18.33 | **1.070 (1.090): lets go** |
| V5, V2 with the 30 ft member double-riveted | 17.51 | **1.120 (1.141): lets go** |
| V6, V2 with the intercostals triple-riveted | 25.78 | 0.761 (0.775) |

- **The riveting governs in every row.** Buckling between floors (elastic 388 MPa at 5/8 in, 559 at 3/4) and shear
  yield are far above it. Holes would govern only if they took more than 72 % of a double-riveted web (41 % of a
  quadruple one), so the intercostals' unknown manholes matter less than their unknown riveting.
- **The joint strength at which the ligament just holds the onset's 19.61 MN** (the 30 ft member quadruple): with 1 in
  webs a tension efficiency of 0.19; with 3/4 in, 0.29 (double riveting is 0.30); with 5/8 in inboard, 0.34, more
  than double riveting gives.
- **Reading.** The sources add a web a side to the model's layout and, if Titanic's plates were Britannic's, take a
  quarter off each one's thickness: the two nearly cancel. On the sources' own layout at 3/4 in the double bottom holds
  the onset's shear by 1.6 % with her list free and fails by 0.2 % with it held. The coin's edge survives the research;
  5/8 in plate, a single-riveted intercostal or a double-riveted 30 ft member would tip it to letting go in shear at
  the tear's top, and the panel's 1 in would put it 23 % clear.
- **Nothing in the model changes on this note.** The lane does not tune the onset, and where sources differ the
  panel's figures stand (the panel's section is the elastic check's reference; the rule was set before any result).
  For stage B4 the rows above are what-ifs, run beside the as-built ligament, with the margin and the sense printed at
  release and never chosen by the record (the coordinator's ledger, 2026-11-20).

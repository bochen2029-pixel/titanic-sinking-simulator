## 0. Summary

- **The sign of the record is solid; the size is not.** A starboard list from the first minutes is attested by five people (Hichens, reporting the captain's reading of the wheelhouse inclinometer, which he called the commutator, and again from boat 6; the passengers Chambers and Astor; Symons and Duff-Gordon at boat 1). It was gone by about 1:10-1:25 (T 90-105). A port list grew from about 1:20 and was called heavy or great in the last 15-20 minutes [read]. The only measured angle is Hichens's 5 degrees to starboard on the inclinometer, 5 to 10 minutes after the impact (US Day 5) [read]. The only number for the port list from a witness is Quartermaster Rowe's "about six degrees" when collapsible C left at about 1:40 (BI 17678) [read]. Halpern's reconstruction in the repo (+5 at T 10, +2 at T 85, 0 at T 100, -10 at T 130, -15 at T 145) fits these; his -10 and -15 rest on qualitative witnesses I could not check, because his papers are PDFs.
- **Before the collision she listed to port** (Chambers: nearly all afternoon; Beesley noticed it at dinner) [read; read in a secondary]. The coal from the starboard side of the bunker that had been on fire (the forward bunker of BR5, against bulkhead E, emptied by Saturday) explains it: removing about 200 to 330 tons 6.5 m to starboard gives 1.9 to 3.1 degrees at GM 0.80 m [derived]. The model's 2.0 degrees is in that range.
- **Mechanisms, with authors.** (1) Wilding (Harland & Wolff, BI 20242-20251, 1912): starboard first because the post office and baggage room in hold 3 flooded early and G deck's port side was cut up by third-class cabin partitions; port later because, once water was above E deck, the port working passage ("Scotland Road", about 9 ft wide) was a much easier road, while the starboard first-class passage was reached by one stair only and closed forward by a heavy non-watertight steel door; then the list fed itself as stability fell [read]. (2) Halpern and Weeks (2011): asymmetric flooding of the starboard side through the firemen's passage at the bottom of the ship; the list corrected when the passage was full; later up to 10 degrees to port [read in Wikipedia]. (3) The empty starboard bunker of BR5 (Barrett; the Report) [read]. (4) The forward port gangway door: counsel (BI, Day 19) suggested it let in a rush of water and a sudden list to port; Lightoller rejected it (BI 14695); National Geographic (2019) says crewmen opened a port gangway door, could not shut it against the list, and the sea reached it by about 1:50 [read]. (5) Open ports: seen open and shipping water from boat 4 [read in Gracie]; how many is NOT FOUND. (6) Passengers: Wilding computed that 800 people moved 50 ft would right her about 2 degrees (BI 20934) [read], which implies GM about 0.5 m [derived].
- **Bunkers.** The firing bunkers lay across the ship at the boiler-room bulkheads, on both sides of each bulkhead, one per stokehold (11), with 'tween-deck bunkers beside the casings on G deck and coal ports just above F deck [read]. The door in each of bulkheads E to J was at the end of a watertight passage about 9 ft (2.74 m) long through the bunker [read], which gives the bunkers' length fore and aft. A full-width bunker 2.74 m long from tank top to G deck holds about 500 to 540 m3, about 380 to 450 tons at 42 to 47 cubic feet a ton [derived]; this matches the 307 and 365 tons that the HANDOFF gives for the two bunkers either side of bulkhead E [repo] and the 307-543 tons a forum post gives for the firing bunkers [search summary]. Total 6,611 tons, plus about 1,092 in hold 3 [read in secondaries]. Permeability of coal in bulk: 0.40, the Board of Trade Bulkheads Committee's own figure, at 47 cubic feet a ton [read]. 5,892 tons aboard at Southampton [read in secondaries]; perhaps 2,500 to 3,400 tons on the night [derived; read in a secondary]; BR5's forward starboard bunker was empty [read]; where the rest lay is NOT FOUND.
- **E deck.** Scotland Road ran on the port side from the forward cross passage right through to the after end of the ship, about 8 1/2 ft (2.59 m) wide by the Report, about 9 ft by Wilding and 8 to 9 ft by Joughin [read]; stewards' and crew rooms lined its outboard side for 475 ft [read]; a 20 ft ladderway at mid-length led down to the third-class dining rooms [read]. Bulkheads C to J stop at E deck, so nothing crossed the passage from about bulkhead C to bulkhead K; K to O carried hand-worked doors at E deck [read]. The forward cross passage, 9 ft wide at the sides and 15 ft at the centre, ended in a gangway door on each side [read].
- **Gangway doors.** Lightoller sent the boatswain and about six men to open the gangway doors while he worked at boat 6 (BI 13896-13914; boat 6 left at 12:55); none of them was seen again [read]. In 1912 he named E deck (BI 13903a, 13916); in 1935 he wrote of the port lower-deck gangway door abreast of No. 2 hatch [read]. The wreck: the port gangway door of D deck's first-class reception room, 73 1/2 x 56 1/8 in (1.87 x 1.43 m, 2.66 m2), was seen attached to the hull in 1993 and was recovered from the sea floor in 1998 [read]; whether it stood open in 1912 is not shown. My geometry puts the forward E-deck door (x about 84-90 m, sill 15.5 m above the keel) at the sea about T 70-95 and the D-deck door (x about 45-55 m, sill about 17.1 m) about T 110-130 [derived, rough].
- **Stability.** At 49,000 t and GM 0.80 m (Hackett and Bedford, as the model uses) a list of 5 degrees needs about 3,400 to 3,500 t·m of heeling moment and 10 degrees about 6,800 to 7,700 t·m [derived]. 300 t of water 8 m off the centreline (2,400 t·m) gives 3.5 degrees at GM 0.80 m, 5.3 to 5.6 at 0.50 m, about 10 at 0.20 m and about 14 at zero [derived]. The late list needs both a moment to port and a loss of GM.

**The gaps at the boats as an inclinometer** [derived, low]. A boat hangs plumb from its davit head, so a list phi opens or closes the gap at the deck edge by about L sin phi, L being the fall from davit head to gunwale. With L about 3.5 m [bet] and Hichens's 2 ft (0.61 m) as the gap when level [read], Evans's 2 1/2 ft at boat 10 gives about 2.5 degrees, Lowe's 3 ft at boat 14 about 5 degrees, Joughin's 4 1/2 ft about 12.5 degrees and Shelley's 4-5 ft 10 to 14 degrees. The scatter is wider than any target; at most, the boats say several degrees to port by 1:20-1:30. Boats lowered on the high side scrape the hull (boat 6 at 12:55 on the port side, boat 15 and collapsible C on the starboard side after 1:30), which dates the change of side to between 12:55 and 1:35.

## 6. Transverse stability on the night

Figures: displacement 49,000 t (HANDOFF §4a [repo]); GM 2 ft 7 1/2 in = 0.800 m (Hackett and Bedford, `GM0` in `core/core.js` [repo]); BM about 6.4 m [derived: I = 0.053 x 259 x 28.19^3 = 307,500 m4 for a waterplane coefficient near 0.78, over V = 49,000 / 1.025 = 47,800 m3; it is also the BM that HANDOFF §4b's loll figures imply; the model holds the Harland & Wolff value].

**300 tons of water 8 m off the centreline** = 2,400 t·m, sin(phi) (GM + 3.2 tan^2 phi) = 2,400 / 49,000 = 0.0490 [derived]:
- GM 0.80 m: 3.46 degrees (3.51 small-angle). The added weight is low and helps GM a little; the free surface of a 2.74 m x 13 m bunker half costs 1.025 x 2.74 x 13^3 / 12 / 49,000 = 0.010 m: both negligible.
- GM 0.50 m: 5.3 degrees (5.6 small-angle). GM 0.20 m: about 9.7 degrees. GM zero: about 14 degrees.

**The bunkers at bulkhead E as a heeling moment** [derived; geometry from section 3.2; water to G deck, z 9.42 m]: one half (port or starboard) of W or Y is about 257 m3; empty and flooded, with 0.95 for the framing, 244 m3 = 250 t at about 6.0 m off the centreline, 1,500 t·m. If the port half holds coal (0.40) and is also flooded through the coal, it holds 105 t and the net is 870 t·m.
- W alone (BR5's empty starboard half; Barrett's hole), port half full and flooding: 1.3 degrees; port half dry (a division, or the water not yet through): 2.2 degrees.
- W and Y both holed on the starboard side with dry port halves: 3,000 t·m, 4.4 degrees; with flooded coal to port: 1,740 t·m, 2.5 degrees. HANDOFF's 300 t at 8 m (3.5 degrees) is inside this range.

**The coal list before the collision** [derived]: 336 t (half of 672) taken out of the starboard halves of W and Y at y = 6.5 m is 2,184 t·m, 3.2 degrees; 200 t, 1.9 degrees; the same 330 t moved across to port (13 m) would be 6.3 degrees, more than anyone reported. The model's 2.0 degrees equals 1,368 t·m, about 210 t taken from 6.5 m to starboard.

**What the passengers tell us** [derived]: Wilding's 2 degrees for 800 people moved 50 ft (BI 20934): 800 x 70 to 75 kg = 56 to 60 t, x 15.24 m = 853 to 914 t·m; GM = M / (W tan 2) = 853 to 914 / (49,000 x 0.0349) = 0.50 to 0.53 m. Either Wilding used a smaller GM than Hackett and Bedford's 0.80 m, or he allowed for the flooding. The model holds GM near 0.5 m through 2:00 (HANDOFF §4b [repo]), which is what Wilding's figure implies.

**Loll** [derived, after HANDOFF §4b]: with GM negative, a wall-sided hull lolls to tan(phi) = sqrt(-2 GM / BM): BM 6.4 m and GM -0.1 m give 10.0 degrees, -0.3 m give 17.0 degrees.

## 7. Conflicts

1. **Boat 6, 12:55: starboard or port?** Hichens, in boat 6, says the starboard list held the boat against the side (BI 1372); Lightoller, loading it, says that is where he first saw the list, to port, and heard Wilde's order to go to starboard (BI 13852-13868). Symons and Duff-Gordon put a slight starboard list at boat 1 ten to fifteen minutes later. I would take starboard at 12:55-1:10: three witnesses, two of them handling boats on the side the list favoured, against one; Lightoller's memory of the order may belong to a later moment, and Gracie puts an identical order at the last boat (about 2:00-2:05). Lightoller's own very little at boat 4 (1:55) shows he judged the list small throughout.
2. **Rowe's first sight of the port list.** He says he first noticed it when he left the after bridge for the fore bridge (BI 17676), about 12:25-12:45, which would put a port list before boat 1's starboard list. His "about six degrees" is stated for when he left the ship, at 1:40 (BI 17678-17680). I would use only the 1:40 figure; his accounts are known to be inconsistent (Wormstedt).
3. **Hichens's timing.** US: about 5 to 10 minutes after the impact. BI: after 12, after the order to get the boats out. Halpern takes T 10. The two are compatible if the captain read the instrument twice; I would take +5 at T 10 with a time range of T 5 to T 25.
4. **Lightoller's even keel at about 12:10** against Hichens's starboard list after 12:23 and Chambers's noticeable list. A small list (1 to 3 degrees) is easy to miss on deck; I would read Lightoller as small, not zero.
5. **How large at the end.** Rowe 6 degrees at 1:40; Halpern 10 at 1:50 and 15 at 2:05; Lightoller very little at 1:55; Wilding (from the evidence) quite small while boats remained; the Commissioner very great; Bride, Hardy, Brown, Joughin and Thayer heavy or great. I would fit 6 degrees at T 120 (medium) and treat 10 and 15 as Halpern's estimates (low-medium), not testimony, until his Part II is read.
6. **Which gangway door.** 1912: the only port gangway door is on E deck (Lightoller); 1935: the port lower-deck gangway door abreast of No. 2 hatch; the wreck's door is D deck's, at the reception room, and nobody saw it opened. Two readings: (a) Nichols's party opened the forward E-deck door at x 84-90 m, where Wilding puts the forward end of Scotland Road and the memoir puts No. 2 hatch; (b) the D-deck door was the one opened. I would test (a) as the order's target and (b) as what the wreck may show.
7. **When the order was given.** BI: at boat 6 (12:55). Memoir: at boat 4, with the forecastle head within about ten feet of the water. On the model's geometry the forecastle head (C deck at the stem, z = 23.6 m) stood 14.3 m above the first waterline, so with ten feet left the stem had sunk about 11.2 m. The well deck at x = 84 m, awash at about T 130, needed a rise of 11.8 m there; the stem, on a longer lever, rises faster and reaches 11.2 m somewhat earlier, roughly T 100-120 [derived, rough]. Either the memoir's ten feet is too small or the order came after 1:20. I would keep the BI time (T 70-80) for the order and T 80-95 for the earliest opening.
8. **BR5's bunker door.** The Report: it burst about an hour after the collision (T 60). Barrett: the rush came at about 1:10 (T 90). Half an hour apart (plant sheet, conflict 4). Use T 60-90.
9. **Bunker capacities.** 365 and 307 (repo), 307-543 (forum), 380-450 (my geometry, full width). All agree if the lettered figures are full-width bunkers; Science Insights' "starboard bunkers ... 672 tons" then means both sides. The Harland & Wolff table (in Halpern's "Prime Mover" as an image) would settle it.
10. **Coal aboard.** 5,892 certified against 4,427 to 6,000 elsewhere. Use 5,892.
11. **Wheat's stairway and the model's event.** The model labels the T 75 event "water pouring down from E deck at the Turkish baths (bulkhead F)" [repo]. In the transcript as I read it, Wheat's stairway lay on the starboard side of the racquet court, with bulkhead D in the discussion, and counsel summed up that the water rose forward of D to E deck and ran aft along E deck (BI 11036-11064). I did not settle which stairway the Report's "bulkhead F" refers to; worth a check before the event is used as a side-specific target.
12. **Hold 2 at T 40.** The Report has the water about 3 ft above E deck in No. 2 hold at 40 minutes. At a trim near 2 degrees the sea outside at x = 94 m is about 13.8 m above the keel against E deck at 15.8 m there [derived], so the hold cannot stand 3 ft above E deck unless the time is later or the trim larger. Treat it as the seamen's quarters' water, not a hold level.

### 9.5 (e) What is well supported, and what is a bet

- **Well supported:** the sign history (port before, starboard for the first hour, upright about T 90-105, port after and growing); the 5 degrees on the inclinometer near T 10 (one witness, an instrument); Rowe's about 6 degrees at T 120; Scotland Road's width (2.6-2.7 m) and its run over the bulkhead tops; Wilding's mechanism as the builder's own reading; BR5's empty starboard bunker; the 9 ft bunker length; the 0.40 permeability of coal at 47 cubic feet a ton; the recovered D-deck door's size; Lightoller's order and the party's loss.
- **Medium:** the bunker capacities and volumes (geometry and secondary tonnages agree); Halpern's T 85 and T 100 points; the gaps at the boats; GM about 0.5 m from Wilding's 2 degrees.
- **Bets:** Halpern's -10 and -15 (his evidence unread); every bunker's fill on 14 April except BR5's; whether a bunker's halves were divided; the bunker doors' size; the starboard passage's width; which gangway door was opened, when, and whether at all; the position of the reception-room door along the ship; the number of open ports.
- **Tests to run first** [bet]: (1) bunker pairs at E (W and Y) with W's starboard half empty and holed, a division closed, holds 2-3 split as now: does T 10 reach +3 to +5? (2) Scotland Road at 2.7 m with the starboard passage choked by a 1 m door, no gangway door: does the sign change between T 90 and T 105, and how far port by T 120? (3) Add case (a) of 9.4 from T 85: does T 120 reach -6 without sinking her before 2:15? If (2) alone gets the late list, the door is not needed; if neither (2) nor (3) gets past -3, the loss of GM (HANDOFF §4b) is the missing piece.

# The ship's plant (boilers, coal, steam, electricity, doors, pumps): a sourced fact sheet (v1)

- **Written:** 2026-11-16 by a research scout (Opus 5.5) for the lead session that is building `core/systems.js`. Research 18:36 to 19:18 CST; file written 19:18 to 19:25 CST. Web research only; nothing in the repository was changed except this file.
- **Status:** COMPLETE for v1 (the ten items of the brief, plus a compartment map in section 11), with the gaps listed at the end.
- **Read first:** `RESEARCH_olympic_class_steering_propulsion_trials_v1_2026-11-16.md`, cited below as "sheet §n". Its §1 to §3 already hold the steering gear (two three-crank steam engines, 100 psi), the screws and the engines, and these plant facts: 24 double-ended and 5 single-ended boilers at 215 psi, about 12 h to raise steam, the turbine running on the reciprocating engines' exhaust with no throttle, the change-over valves, and the steering engines exhausting to the main condensers. They are not repeated here; where this sheet relies on them it cites the sheet, and where it found a primary source for the same fact it says so. Sheet §7 holds the engine orders after the collision, §9 Britannic, §10 Lusitania.
- **Method:** primary sources first: the British Wreck Commissioner's Report's description of the ship (Machinery, Electrical Installation, Pumping Arrangements, Watertight Compartments, Detailed Description, General) and its damage pages; the British and US inquiry transcripts (engine-room crew Barrett, Beauchamp, Hendrickson, Dillon, Cavell, Ranger, Scott; deck and other crew Lightoller, Lee, Pearcey, Rule, Pitman; Edward Wilding of Harland & Wolff; Guglielmo Marconi; J. Bruce Ismay); The Shipbuilder (1911) as reproduced by GG Archives. Then researchers: Samuel Halpern, Mark Chirnside, Parks Stephenson, the TRMA, Ralph Currell. About 25 web searches and about 50 pages opened.
- **Read or search summary:** "(read)" = a page I opened today; "(search summary)" = only the search tool's digest of a page I did not open, so lower confidence; "(ET key points)" = an Encyclopedia Titanica reference page that summarises an article hosted elsewhere, so the words are ET's, not the author's.
- **Tool caveat:** most pages were read through a fetch tool that extracts the text with a small model. I asked it for verbatim quotes and asked twice for the key ones; punctuation may be normalised. Encyclopedia Titanica refuses that tool, so its pages were read in the app's built-in browser. Nothing was downloaded; for that reason Halpern's PDF papers were not opened.
- **Quotes:** verbatim and under 15 words. From the 1911-13 sources (the inquiries and their reports, The Shipbuilder, Beesley, Gracie) they are given freely; from modern work still in copyright (Halpern, Chirnside, the TRMA, Currell, Encyclopedia Titanica and the like) at most one short quote per work, and the other rows taken from it say "(paraphrase)".
- **Conventions:** 1 ft = 0.3048 m; 1 sq ft = 0.0929 m2; 1 lb = 0.4536 kg; 1 long ton = 1,016.05 kg; 1 psi = 6.895 kPa (pressures are gauge, as the sources give them); sea water 1,025 kg/m3; pump "tons" taken as long tons. Times are ship's time on 14-15 April 1912 unless marked; the British Report puts ship's time 1 h 50 min ahead of New York time (see Conflicts 18). "BI nnnn" is a British Inquiry question number.
- **Numbering:** the boiler rooms (the firemen's "sections") are numbered 1 to 6 from aft: No. 1 is next to the engine room, No. 6 is foremost. The simulator's order from forward is therefore 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. The eleven stokeholds (firing aisles) are numbered from aft too: Nos. 10 and 11 are in No. 6, Nos. 8 and 9 in No. 5 (Barrett, BI 1846), so No. 1 is the single-ended room's.

## At a glance (for the simulator)

1. **Boilers:** 29 Scotch boilers, 24 double-ended (6 furnaces) and 5 single-ended (3 furnaces), 159 furnaces, 215 psi. From forward: No. 6 has 4 DE (24 furnaces), Nos. 5 to 2 have 5 DE each (30 furnaces each), No. 1 has 5 SE (15 furnaces).
2. **Lit on 14 April:** the 24 DE boilers (two or three of them in No. 2 only from about 8 a.m. that Sunday, 12 h before they could be connected); the 5 SE boilers in No. 1 were cold. 75 rpm was the order; about 22.5 kn by log 8-10 p.m. without the SE boilers.
3. **Steam:** about 6,260 lb/min (47 kg/s, about 2.0 kg/s per DE boiler) to the main engines at 75 rpm by Halpern's calculation; the feed heater was rated for 700,000 lb/h (88 kg/s), a ceiling.
4. **Coal:** 5,892 tons aboard leaving Southampton; bunkers about 6,611 tons; Olympic burned 629 tons a day at 21.43 kn; Titanic at 22+ kn perhaps 690-710 tons a day (my scaling, no record found).
5. **Flooding of the plant:** No. 6 had 8 ft of water in about 10 min and was full to E deck in about an hour; No. 5 was kept dry by pumping until a rush through the pass (bunker door) at about 12:40 to 1:10; No. 4 took water from under the plates at about 1:20, about a foot when abandoned; Nos. 3, 2, 1 and the engine rooms were dry at 1:20.
6. **Fires:** dampers shut at the "Stop"; fires drawn in No. 5 (30 furnaces, 15-20 men, about 20 min), partly in No. 4, and in the after stokehold of No. 6 (about 15 min, by one account); the boilers blew off steam through the first hour; the orders passed below were "Keep steam up", then "draw fires as much as possible" (Dillon).
7. **Electricity:** four 400 kW, 100 V DC steam sets (W. H. Allen, 325 rpm, 185 psi) at the tank top in the electric engine room (aft of the turbine room), steam from Nos. 1, 2 and 4 by their own pipes; two 30 kW emergency sets on D deck in the turbine casing about 20 ft above the waterline, steam from Nos. 2, 3 and 5 by a pipe above E deck, run every night.
8. **Lights:** burned until about 2:17-2:18, two or three minutes before she sank; how they went out is disputed (gradually as the stern went under, or suddenly with one flash). The stokehold lights in Nos. 5 and 4 went out for some minutes early on.
9. **Wireless:** 5 kW motor generator off the mains, then the emergency dynamos, then a battery; last signal 2:17, "blurred, and ending abruptly".
10. **Steering:** steam steering engines on the auxiliary steam line; hydraulic telemotor; only the rudder indicator is electric.
11. **Watertight doors:** 12 at the tank top, held by friction clutches, released from the bridge by electromagnets (needs current), closed by gravity through oil cataracts (25-30 s, unsourced); floats and a lever at each door work without current; reopened by hand after the bridge switch is off; the doors aft were reopened about 12:45 and never closed again.
12. **Pumps:** 5 x 250 + 3 x 150 = 1,700 tons an hour (0.47 m3/s), all steam; three in pump rooms in Nos. 2, 3 and 5; the Court found they "could not possibly have coped".
13. **Fans:** the 12 stokehold fans were electric; all 45 running fans were stopped by order, over about three quarters of an hour from roughly 11:55 p.m. (my arithmetic from Ranger's evidence).
14. **Screws:** clear of the water before the final plunge (three witnesses), but the engines had stopped for good about midnight, so there was no thrust to lose.

## Conflicts and doubts

1. **How many boilers were lit.** 24 lit and 5 not (Barrett, US) against "either eight or five" out on Sunday (Barrett, BI 2205), after 9 out the first two days and 8 the next two (BI 2203). Read together: 20 or 21 boilers lit until Sunday, 24 from Sunday evening, the 5 SE never.
2. **When the Sunday boilers came on line.** Barrett: lit about 8 a.m., 12 h to connect, "may have been connected that same night" (BI 2232-2233). Chirnside and Halpern: on line at 7 p.m., citing Fireman Shiers, whose British testimony I read and which has nothing on boilers; the source of the 7 p.m. is not found.
3. **Was No. 6 abandoned at once?** The Report and Barrett: yes, the men went through into No. 5 as the door came down. Beauchamp, in the same stokehold (No. 10): fires were drawn for about a quarter of an hour, with water over the plates, and he left by the escape. Possibly different groups in the same room.
4. **When No. 5 flooded.** Barrett: the rush came at "about ten minutes past one" (BI 2349). The Report: the bunker door burst about an hour after the collision, that is about 12:40. Half an hour apart.
5. **What gave way in No. 5.** The Report and Halpern: the bunker door, not the bulkhead. Barrett: "something had given way", he did not look.
6. **Where the bunker fire was.** The starboard bunker at the forward end of No. 5 against bulkhead E (Barrett, the Report, TRMA) against the after starboard bunker of No. 6, or a reserve bunker forward of No. 6 (Garzke's papers of 1996-97, as reported by the TRMA). Since the bulkhead ran through the cross bunker and coal was taken out of both halves (Halpern, ET key points), "No. 5 forward" and "No. 6 aft" may be the same bunker's two halves.
7. **When the fire began.** At Belfast (Hendrickson, "I heard") against soon after leaving port (Barrett).
8. **Coal aboard.** 5,892 tons (certified, per ET and Maltin) against "about 6,000" (Ismay, a round number).
9. **Coal consumption.** No Titanic record found. 825-826 tons a day is bunker capacity over "8 days at full speed"; Olympic measured 629 tons a day at 21.43 kn; other secondary sources give 600-650 (search summaries).
10. **Height of the stokehold plates.** Barrett: "supposed to be six feet above the tank tops". The Report puts the damage about 10 ft above the keel and the tank top about 5 ft above it, which with water entering 2 ft above the plates would put the plates about 3 ft above the tank top; but the US Report puts the tear "about twenty feet below the waterline", about 14 ft above the keel at her draught, which fits Barrett's 6 ft. Unresolved.
11. **The wireless set's power.** 5 kW (British Report; Marconi under oath) against a 1.5 kW standard set (The Shipbuilder, 1911, written for Olympic before Titanic's set was fitted). Stephenson (ET key points): Titanic's set was "more advanced than initially planned". Use 5 kW for Titanic.
12. **Height of the emergency dynamos.** 20 ft above the waterline (Electrical Installation page) against 21 ft above the load waterline (Watertight Compartments page). Different reference lines, perhaps.
13. **Number of motors.** 153 (British Report) against 150 (The Shipbuilder).
14. **The door bell.** Scott heard none (BI 5547); Rule heard the electric bells (BI 6389-6391); the bridge notice says to sound it for 10 s before closing.
15. **How fast the doors closed after the impact.** "Almost immediately", "instantly", "about three minutes", "in less than five minutes".
16. **How the doors were reopened.** By hand with a pump handle (Dillon), but counsel and Scott held that the bridge had first to release the clutches; the notice ("cannot ... be operated mechanically whilst switch is on") supports them. So the bridge switch was probably put off again some time before 12:45.
17. **How the lights went out.** Gradually, as the after end went under (Ranger); suddenly, one flash, then out (Beesley); "practically until she disappeared" (Lee, Pearcey, the Report). The two failure modes (steam pressure falling, so the dynamos slow and the lamps dim; circuits flooding, so fuses blow section by section) may both have happened.
18. **The clock for the last minutes.** The Virginian's 12:27 New York time is 2:17 ship's time on the British Report's offset (1 h 50 min). The US Report used 1 h 33 min (2:00), and Chirnside and Halpern compute 2 h 02 min for 14 April, which would make it 2:29, after the 2:20 foundering. Treat 2:17 as approximate.
19. **The 1:45 message.** "Engine room full up to boilers" (British Report) against "Engine room getting flooded" (US Report); and the engine rooms were dry at 1:20 (Dillon, Scott).
20. **Steam for the main dynamos.** Wilding: from Nos. 1, 2 and 4; but No. 1 was cold on the night, so they must have run on Nos. 2 and 4 or through the cross-connection.
21. **Halpern's steam figure.** A calculation with an assumed 42% cut-off, not a measurement; the feed heater's 700,000 lb/h is a rating, not an evaporation. Neither is a measured boiler output.
22. **The firing crew.** Halpern: 5 leading firemen a watch; Barrett: one per section, which would make six with No. 1 (cold on the night).

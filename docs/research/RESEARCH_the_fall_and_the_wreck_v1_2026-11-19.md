## 0. Summary

- **Where the pieces lie (measured, medium-high).** Ballard's centre of the boiler field is 41 deg 43' 32" N, 49 deg 56' 49" W (Ballard 1987, p. 199, via Wikipedia) [read in Wikipedia]. The positions published with it put the bow **772 m due north** of the boiler field and the stern **148 m north-west** of it (the stern's position is the one the UK's 1992 MAIB reappraisal took from Ballard) [read; derived]. That makes bow and stern **689 m apart, the bow bearing 010 deg from the stern**, against the "about 600 m" every account prints (Ryan 1986: the stern "some 600 meters behind the bow") [derived; read]. The bow points north to north-north-east; the stern lies reversed, "twisted about 180 degrees out of phase" (Oceanus, 1986) [read].
- **The depth (medium).** 3,795 m (Uchupi, Ballard and Lange 1986, as cited), about 12,500 ft (3,810 m) in WHOI's 1986 account, 3,800 m (NOAA), and a bed of about 3,772 m just north-east of the bow by the 2004 current meter [read; read in Wikipedia; read in the brainstorm]. **The brainstorm's 3,784 m (12,415 ft) is NOT FOUND in any source I could read.** Use 3,790 m, 3,770-3,810. No separate depths for bow and stern, no slope in degrees ("gently sloping").
- **The bow (observed).** Upright, about 5 deg to port; the prow buried **12 m** (Best et al. 2000) to **15-20 m** (Ballard; Mengot and Halpern and Weeks: "60 feet at the anchors") with both anchors visible (the brainstorm's "starboard anchor" should read "the anchors"), its after end on the mud; bent down 6-10 deg under the well-deck cranes and 4 deg under the forward expansion joint; the decks at its after end collapsed; a furrow and ejecta in the mud; the No. 1 hatch cover blown off some 60-100 m ahead [read; read in Wikipedia].
- **The stern (observed).** Upright, reversed; the rudder buried about 15 m; decks pancaked to 3.7-4.6 m in all; 15 m of the 32 m poop deck peeled back on itself; bent 10 deg to starboard; "decisive implosion damage from being dragged under before the interior could completely flood" (Mengot) [read]. It turned in the fall: the 2010 sonar map shows marks west of it and nearly all the debris east of it, read as rotation "like a helicopter blade" (AP 2012) [read].
- **The middle and the debris (measured maps).** About 70 ft (21 m) of the ship disintegrated and is the source of nearly all the debris (NOAA) [read]. The five single-ended boilers of boiler room 1 lie together, about 150-180 m south-east to east of the stern, and every author who says so takes them for the point under the break: they "dropped straight down" [read; read in Wikipedia]. The field begins about 600 m south of the bow and runs 600 m on (1986), or two trails of 600-800 m south-west (1987), about 1,700 by 875 m on the 1988 map (the brainstorm's reading); the 2010 survey box was 3 by 5 miles [read].
- **The fall (reconstructions only).** 5-6 minutes to the bed for both (Uchupi, Ballard and Lange 1986) [read in Wikipedia]; the bow struck at an estimated 25-30 mph (Ballard 1987; Mengot) or about 20 knots after gliding about 1 ft forward for every 6 ft down (Halpern and Weeks 2011) [read in Wikipedia; read]. **The only computed impact** is Best, Powrie, Hayward and Barton (2000): from the embedment and a measured undrained shear strength of about 25 kPa at 10-16 m, the bow most likely struck at **5-10 m/s at under 40 deg**; steep and fast is ruled out [read: abstract]. The positions give the bow a glide of 1 in 4.9 and the stern a nearly vertical fall [derived]. The stern's air spaces failed somewhere between about 60 ft and 1,000 ft down (Mengot; the panel's 1998 analysis; Ballard 1986); no one computed it [read; read in repo note].
- **What was heard (1912).** Pitman, in boat 5 about 300-500 yards off: "four reports", "like the reports of a big gun in the distance", which he took for "bulkheads going", heard "Not until she was submerged" (US Day 4) [read: transcript]. Scarrott also gives four explosions as she went "with a rush" (BI 426) [read: transcript]. The stern, after settling back and rising again, stood from about 20 seconds (Etches) to about five minutes (Buley, Evans) and went under at 2:20 by Pitman's watch; no suction (Gracie) [read; read in Wormstedt].
- **The sea and the bed.** Surface current that night about 1.1 knots (0.56 m/s) toward 197 deg (Halpern; the MAIB's 1.2-1.3 knots at 196 deg; the 1985 team's "at least a 1.1 knot southerly current") [read in Halpern; read]. Bottom current 1/4 to 3/4 knot (0.13-0.39 m/s) on the 1986 dives, direction uncertain [read]. Silt over thick clay, su about 25 kPa at 10-16 m below the bed (one 1998 core) [read]. Sea surface about -2 deg C [read in Wikipedia and repo notes]; about 1 deg C at the bed [search summary]; no profile found.
- **Other wrecks** (section 7) give hull-collapse depths only for pressure hulls (Thresher 730 m in 0.1 s; Scorpion 470-610 m, about 91 s of acoustic events), and the landing's work on flooded hulls (Bismarck's 2 km slide and compressed decks; Hood's midship piece in an impact crater; Scorpion's skid trench; El Faro's bridge half a mile away). None gives a measured fall speed.
- **For the model (section 10):** a site frame with the boiler field at the origin; acceptance bands: separation 550-800 m; the bow 540-950 m from the break point toward her heading, the stern within 300 m; the prow buried 12-20 m; the bow's impact 5-10 m/s at under 40 deg (computed) with 10-13 m/s as the outer band; the stern reversed (180 +/- 40 deg) and its tight spaces failed by four discrete events between about 18 and 305 m; a fall of 4.5-7 minutes as a reconstruction to check, not to fit.

What the positions add, as checks any computed fall must pass [derived from section 1.1]:
- **The bow planed.** If the boiler field marks the point under the break (every reconstruction above that says where), the bow travelled 772 m horizontally while falling about 3,800 m: a glide of 1 in 4.9, a path 11.5 deg from the vertical. Halpern and Weeks' 1 in 6 would put it 630 m off. Allow 1 in 4 to 1 in 7 (540-950 m); the bow section's own half-length (about 70 m) is inside that band.
- **The stern fell nearly straight down.** It lies 148 m from the boiler field: 1 in 26, within about 2 deg of the vertical, and turned end for end on the way.
- **Path length and mean speed of the bow.** A path of sqrt(3,795^2 + 772^2) = 3,873 m in 5-6 minutes is a mean of 10.8-12.9 m/s along the path; the brainstorm's pairing of "13 m/s" with "six minutes" does not close (13 m/s for 360 s is 4,700 m); "about 11 m/s for about 6 minutes" or "13 m/s for about 5 minutes" would.
- **The computed impact (5-10 m/s, under 40 deg) is slower than the asserted ones (10-13 m/s).** Either the bow was still slowing in its last hundreds of metres (planing flatter as it neared the bed), or the asserted speeds are means of the whole fall carried over to the landing. A model that reaches a terminal speed of about 11 m/s and keeps it to the bed will overshoot Best's band.

## 8. Conflicts

1. **The depth.** 3,795 m (Uchupi, Ballard and Lange 1986, as cited); about 12,500 ft, 3,810 m (Ryan 1986; WHOI); 3,800 m (NOAA, Wikipedia); the bed about 3,772 m just north-east of the bow (the 2004 current meter, as the brainstorm read it); outliers 13,000 ft (Ballard on the first day), 3,996 m (Sellers), 12,400 ft (Professional Mariner, 2007). The brainstorm's 3,784 m (12,415 ft) is NOT FOUND in a source I could read. **Use 3,790 m, band 3,770-3,810 m.**
2. **The separation.** About 600 m (Ryan 1986; Wikipedia; National Geographic 2012, "1,970 feet"), against 689 m from the published positions, "over 650 m" (Maltin) and "about half a mile", 800 m (AP 2012). The positions are rounded to one second and the point each refers to (stem, centre, after end) is not stated. **Use 650 m, band 550-800 m.**
3. **The bow's burial.** 12 m at the prow (Best et al. 2000, the only paper that measured the sediment), 15 m ("50 feet of mud", Ballard 1986), 18 m ("60 feet at the anchors", Mengot; Halpern and Weeks 2011), up to 20 m (Ballard 1987). They may measure to different points (the stem's foot, the keel, the anchors' level). **Use 15 m, band 12-20 m.**
4. **The bow's impact speed and angle.** Computed: 5-10 m/s at a fairly shallow angle, under 40 deg (Best et al. 2000). Estimated: about 20 knots, 10.3 m/s (Halpern and Weeks); 25-30 mph, 11.2-13.4 m/s (Ballard 1987; Mengot). Angle: "shallow" (Ballard 1987), "about 45 deg" (Ballard 1986; Halpern and Weeks, of its descent), "steep" (Mengot; the 2012 programme). The computed figure is the one with a method; the others may be the fall's mean speed carried to the landing.
5. **The bow's forward bend.** About 6 deg (Ballard, as Mengot gives it) or about 10 deg (Marschall; Halpern and Weeks); the after bend, 4 deg, is agreed.
6. **The axis of the debris.** South of the bow (Ryan 1986: 600-1,200 m south); trailing south-west from bow and stern (Ballard 1987, as Wikipedia gives it); nearly all of it east of the stern (AP 2012, from the 2010 map). Not necessarily contradictory: the field runs north-south from the bow to beyond the stern, and round the stern it lies to the east.
7. **One trail or several.** Ballard (1987, 2002): a comet trail, light pieces farthest. The 2010 team (Lange; Delgado): several fields in a non-random pattern that do not fit the comet model, nearly all from the 70-ft middle.
8. **The size of the whole site.** AP (2012): "3-by-5-mile (5-by-8-kilometer)", statute miles by its own conversion; Wikipedia: "3 by 5 nautical miles (6 x 9 km)". AP's conversion is the press's own; take statute miles, 4.8 x 8.0 km, as the survey's target box, not the debris itself.
9. **Where the stern's air spaces failed.** About 60 ft (18 m, Mengot), about 200 ft (61 m, Mengot in Gleicher's account; the panel's 1998 analysis, with the keel parting about 500 ft, 152 m), about 1,000 ft (305 m, Ballard's second possibility in 1986, when he still thought she might have broken under water). No one computed it.
10. **When the four reports came.** Pitman: after she was under ("Not until she was submerged"). Scarrott: as she went "with a rush", order unclear. Symons, Crowe, Stengel, Olliver: explosions before she went. Both can be true: the break's reports at the surface (repo break-up note, 5.2) and the stern's after it.
11. **The bottom current's direction.** From bow to stern (south-south-west), as I read Ryan's 1986 line about working "bow-to-stern" with the current like a wind; from south to north in a forum diver's impression (search digest).
12. **The bow's heading on the bed.** North (1985) or north-north-east (1986), words, not a measured heading. At the surface her head was "facing north" by Rowe, magnetic, which Halpern takes for about north-north-west true.
13. **The time she went.** 2:20 by Pitman's watch; Gracie's watch stopped at 2:22 when he went into the water, before the end; watches aboard were not set alike.
14. **The fall times.** "5-6 minutes" for both sections (Uchupi, Ballard and Lange 1986); "about five minutes" for the bow (National Geographic 2012); "about six minutes" (Britannica). The brainstorm's "about 30 mph (13 m/s)" with "about six minutes" does not close (section 4).

### 10d. The bets that remain, with the range a model should allow

1. **The break point is over the boiler field.** The five single-ended boilers are compact and heavy and fell "straight down"; a boiler falling at a few metres a second for some minutes through water that moves 0.1-0.4 m/s relative to the ship could still land 100-200 m off. Allow the break point anywhere within 200 m of the boiler field [bet].
2. **The bow's glide.** 1 in 4 to 1 in 7 (path 8-14 deg from the vertical). A strip model that produces 1 in 2 (planing like a wing) or 1 in 20 (falling like a stone) is wrong.
3. **The bow's attitude in the fall.** 15-45 deg bow down; it must arrive at under 40 deg from the bed at 5-10 m/s (Best) or, at worst, at 10-13 m/s; something must slow or flatten it in the last few hundred metres for the computed band.
4. **The stern's failure depth.** 18-305 m; central 60 m. The failure should be a set of discrete events (four reports), not a smooth squeeze.
5. **The stern's turn.** 180 +/- 40 deg net, sense free; Pellegrino's mechanism (the rudder hard over at 30-45 deg) is one way to get it and is itself a bet. A stern that stood near vertical has no clear heading when it goes: which way it tipped over under water sets its heading as much as any spin, and Thayer's and Beesley's turning at the surface feeds that. So the target is the heading on the bed, not a spin rate.
6. **The "down-blast".** One author's hypothesis; appearance at most, as the brainstorm says.
7. **Sediment.** su0 2-5 kPa, gradient 1.5-2 kPa/m, density 1,400-1,700 kg/m3; calibrate so that the bow, at its own impact speed and angle, buries 12-20 m at the prow and its after end stays on the surface.
8. **The bed.** 3,790 m, 0-2 deg slope, no relief needed at the scale of the ship (the canyon is to the west).
9. **Bottom current.** 0.1-0.4 m/s, direction drawn from the seed (the two readings disagree); it matters only for light debris.
10. **Fall time.** 4.5-7 min. Treat it as a reconstruction: let the physics give it, and check that it lands in the band.

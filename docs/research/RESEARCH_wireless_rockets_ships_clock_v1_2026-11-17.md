## 0. Summary

- **The set (high).** Marconi 5 kW with a rotary (disc) discharger and magnetic detector, in a "silent room" and an operating room on the boat deck; two operators keeping a constant watch [read]. Guaranteed range 250 nm (TRMA, quoting The Marconigraph) or about 350 (Marconi under oath); about 400 nm by day in practice (Bride); about 1,500 to 2,000 nm at night (secondary sources) [read]. On the night it ran on the ship's dynamos nearly to the end; the emergency battery apparatus was never used; in the last minutes the power was impaired and the spark weak (Bride); the Virginian's operator heard a last signal that ended as if the power had been switched off [read].
- **The clock (high that it is disputed).** Titanic's clocks were not changed at midnight (Pitman) [read]. Her ATS was that of the noon longitude of 14 April, 2 h 02 min ahead of New York by Halpern's computation, the modern reading (Halpern; Wormstedt, Fitch and Behe; Maltin; Wikipedia) [read]. The US Senate used 1 h 33 min (Boxhall's 11:46 = 10:13 NY; Lightoller's 2:20 = 5:47 GMT) [read]; the British Report's times imply 1 h 50 min [derived]. Use 2 h 02 min; keep 1 h 50 min as a switch; it moves every wireless event 12 minutes.
- **The call (high in NY time, medium in T).** First CQD at 10:25 p.m. NY with the captain's position 41°44'N 50°24'W; Boxhall's corrected 41°46'N 50°14'W at 10:35; the first SOS at 10:55 [read]. On 2 h 02 min the first CQD is 12:27 a.m. ATS, **T 47**; on the British Report's reading 12:15, **T 35**; on the Senate's 11:58, T 18 [derived]. The captain's order came about T 25 (Bride) [derived]; the first call waited for a position.
- **Carpathia (high on the record, medium on her speed).** Heard at 10:35 NY (T 57) by her one operator, who was undressing for bed [read]; turned at 12:35 a.m. her time (T 60), course N 52 W, 58 nm to the CQD position [read]; stopped at 4:00 and had boat No. 2 alongside at 4:10 her time (**T 265 and T 275**) [read, derived]. The real run was about 46 to 47 nm, because the wreck lies 13 nm ESE of the CQD position [derived], so her mean speed was about **13.6 kn** (range 12 to 15), not the 17.5 kn claimed [read, derived].
- **Californian (high that she saw the rockets; low on the distance).** Stopped at 10:21 p.m. her time in field ice at 42°05'N 50°07'W by her own reckoning [read]: 19.7 nm N 15 E of the CQD position and **22.8 nm N 19 W of the wreck** [derived]. Her one operator turned in at 11:30-11:35 her time, which is **T 2 to T 7** on Halpern's 12-minute offset [derived]. Her officers saw eight white rockets between about 12:45 and 1:40 her time and did not act [read]. Distance in the record: 5 (Lord, to the steamer he saw), 8 to 10 (British Court), 10 to 12 (Groves), 17 to 20, most likely about 18 (MAIB 1992) [read, search summary].
- **Rockets (high).** About eight socket signals, white stars with a report, fired by Boxhall with QM Rowe from the starboard bridge socket by emergency boat No. 1, from about 12:45-12:47 (**T 65-67**) to about 1:45-1:52 (**T 125-132**), roughly 5 minutes apart by the firers' estimate and 8.6 to 9.3 minutes on average [read, derived]. She carried 36 socket signals [search summary]. They burst "several hundred feet" up (Lightoller) [read]; about 600 ft is a secondary figure [read]. The rule of the day: rockets or shells throwing stars of any colour, fired one at a time at short intervals [read].
- **Visibility [derived].** A burst at 600 ft is above the horizon of a 45 ft bridge out to about 36 nm, so on a clear night the limit is brightness, not the horizon; Titanic's masthead light (about 145 ft) is above that bridge's horizon out to about 22 nm. A Morse lamp reaches about 10 nm (Lord's claim) [read].
- **The light (medium).** White masthead lights, then a red side light, half a point to two points on Titanic's port bow, put at 4 to 6 nm by Boxhall, Rowe and Lightoller; the Morse lamp got no answer; lifeboats were sent toward it [read]. The British Court said it was the Californian; the MAIB's deputy chief inspector thought another, unidentified vessel [read].
- **The other ships (high to medium).** Mount Temple 49 nm W, stopped by ice about 14 nm short; Frankfurt about 150 nm SW; Birma 70 to 107 nm; Virginian about 170 nm N; Baltic 243 nm E; Olympic about 505 nm W; Caronia about 800 nm E [read]. Only Carpathia reached the boats; Californian came at 8:30 her time.
- **What-ifs [derived].** A call at T 5 instead of T 47 brings Carpathia to Titanic at about **T 213**, 53 minutes after the sinking at T 160; at the legendary 17.5 kn, T 167. Titanic steaming 5 nm toward her (10 kn for 30 min) saves about 22 minutes of Carpathia's run; with an immediate call too, about T 191; only both together at 17.5 kn beat T 160 (T 150). The one ship that could have come in time was the Californian: at 8 to 10 nm on the first rocket, or at any of the distances on an immediate CQD heard before her operator took off his phones.

## 2. The distress traffic

### 2.1 The clock problem, plainly

The other ships' operators logged in New York time: Lord said all wireless messages are dated in NY time; Cottam's clock was on NY time throughout; the Mount Temple's log puts the first CQD at 10.25, the same minute as the Cape Race log's 10:25 EST [read: Lord, US Day 8, https://www.titanicinquiry.org/USInq/AmInq08Lord01.php; Cottam, BI 17182, 17199-17201; derived]. Titanic's own witnesses gave ATS. To put a logged message on Titanic's T axis one needs her offset from New York, and the record gives three:

- **1 h 33 min:** the US Senate; Boxhall gave 11:46 p.m. ship's time as 10:13 NY [read, via titanicofficers.com], and Lightoller gave the sinking, 2:20 ATS, as 5:47 GMT, which is the same offset [read: US Day 4, https://www.titanicinquiry.org/USInq/AmInq04Pitman05.php].
- **1 h 50 min:** implied by the British Report, whose 12:15 first CQD and 2:17 last "C.Q." are Cape Race's 10:25 and the Virginian's 12:27 NY plus 1:50 [derived]; used by the Nova Scotia archives' Cape Race page [read]. It is also Carpathia's and Californian's own offset (Rostron, Lord) [read], and Halpern says the Court took Titanic's clock as Californian's [search summary].
- **2 h 02 min:** Halpern, from her noon longitude of 14 April (44°31'W, so ATS = GMT minus 2 h 58 min) and Pitman's evidence that the clocks were not changed that night; used by Wormstedt, Fitch and Behe (with Halpern), Maltin (2019), Wikipedia's "Sinking of the Titanic", and this repository's lifeboats note [read]. Bride's answer of about two hours (to a senator's 1 h 55 min) points the same way [read].

I use 2 h 02 min. It moves each wireless event 12 minutes later than the British Report's reading and 29 minutes later than the Senate's; it does not change the order of events. Its one casualty is the Virginian's 12:27 NY, which on 2 h 02 min falls nine minutes after she sank (§2.2, last rows).

### 5.1 The geometry of seeing a light [derived]

- Distance to the horizon: d = sqrt(2 R h). With R = 3,440 nm and h in feet this is 1.064 √h nm with no refraction, and 1.15 to 1.17 √h nm with standard refraction (the effective earth radius 7/6 R gives 1.15; the navigators' tables use 1.17). A light at height H is just on the horizon of an eye at h when D = 1.17 (√H + √h).
- Rocket burst, 600 ft, seen from Californian's bridge, 45 ft (Halpern's eye height): 1.17 x (24.5 + 6.7) = **36.5 nm**. At 400 ft: 31.2 nm; at 800 ft: 40.9 nm. From a liner's bridge at about 55 ft [bet]: 37.3 nm for 600 ft.
- Titanic's masthead light, about 145 ft (Halpern): 1.17 x (12.0 + 6.7) = 21.9 nm to a 45 ft eye, Halpern's 22 [read: Halpern, "Titanic's Masthead Light" (2007), https://glts.org/articles/halpern/masthead_light.html]. The 1910 rules asked only that a masthead light be visible at 5 miles and side lights at 2 [read: same].
- So on that clear night the horizon was not the limit for any distance in dispute (5 to 20 nm); brightness, attention and recognition were.
- Apparent height (effective radius 7,433 km, eye 13.7 m, dip 1.92 mrad). At 19 nm (35.2 km) the curvature drop is D/2R = 2.37 mrad: a 600 ft (183 m) burst stands (183 - 13.7)/35,190 - 2.37 + 1.92 = 4.4 mrad above the visible horizon, Titanic's masthead light (44 m) 0.42 mrad, a ratio of about 10. At 10 nm the ratio is 4.2; at 5 nm 4.3. A rocket fired by the ship whose masthead light one is watching therefore bursts several times higher than that light, never at half its height. Stone's observation fits a nearer steamer with the rockets coming from beyond her, or abnormal refraction (Maltin's explanation) [derived; the two readings are in the sources]. The model need not decide; it should not take Stone's comparison of the bursts with the masthead light as a distance cue.

## 8. Conflicts

1. **Titanic's offset from New York:** 1:33, 1:50, 2:02 (§2.1, §7). Use 2:02 (it rests on the sun at her noon longitude and on Pitman); keep 1:50 as a switch. Every NY-logged event moves 12 minutes between them.
2. **The first position and when it was corrected:** Cape Race logs 41°44'N 50°24'W at 10:25 and 41°46'N 50°14'W at 10:35, and Captain Moore received the first pair first (US Day 9); the Mount Temple's log already has 41.46/50.14 in its 10.25 entry, and Moore's British answer scrambles the pairs (BI 9235); Eaton's Frankfurt page prints 41.44N 50.14W. 3. **Who answered first:** Bride says Frankfurt, then Carpathia (BI 16513-16517; US Day 2); the logs have Mount Temple at 10:25, Carpathia at 10:35, Frankfurt at 10:48. The logs are better [bet].
4. **When Carpathia turned:** 12:35 Carpathia time (Rostron), stated by him as 10:45 NY; 12:25 in a log read at BI 25386; 10:38 NY by Halpern. Use 10:38 NY (T 60).

### 10.4 Rocket schedule and visibility rule

- **Schedule (1912):** eight rockets, the first at T 66, then every 8.9 minutes: T 66, 75, 84, 93, 102, 111, 119, 128 [derived from the first at T 65-67 and the last at T 125-132]. They need a man at the starboard socket (Boxhall, Rowe) and nothing from the plant. Stock 36, so a ship could keep one every five minutes for three hours [derived]. In a what-if the earliest first rocket is about T 10-15: the time to fetch the box from aft [bet].
- **Visibility:** seen by a ship's watch if D ≤ min(1.17 (√H + √h), D_lum), with H = 600 ft (burst; [bet], "several hundred feet" [read]), h the observer's eye (45 ft for Californian [read]; 55 ft for a liner [bet]), and D_lum = 25 nm on a clear dark night [bet: Titanic's were seen at 17-20 nm on the MAIB's reading and Carpathia's seen on the horizon from Californian at about 20-25 nm on Lord's position]. The horizon term is 36-37 nm, so D_lum decides.
- **Recognition is a separate switch:** in 1912 Californian saw all eight and did not act; seeing must not imply responding [read].
- **Report heard:** within 5 nm [bet; NOT FOUND].
- **Morse lamp:** read within 10 nm (Lord's claim) if the other ship is watching for it [read, bet].
- **Ship's lights:** a masthead light at height Hm is above the horizon to 1.17 (√Hm + √h); Titanic's 145 ft light to a 45 ft eye, 22 nm [read, derived]; luminous range at least 5 nm by rule, about 17 nm by Halpern's estimate for her light [read].
- **Boat flares:** a green light held about 6 ft up is above a 55 ft bridge's horizon to about 11.5 nm [derived], unless the model allows the night's abnormal refraction (Maltin) [read].

### 10.5 Two what-ifs, with the arithmetic [derived]

Inputs: founder at T 160 (the 1912 run; the model's 157); Carpathia d = 46.5 nm, v = 13.6 kn, delay 3 min, so a run of 46.5 / 13.6 x 60 = 205 min.

**(a) The call made at once.** CQD at T 5 instead of T 47 (the British Report's reading: T 35). T 5 is 9:43 NY; Cottam's station was open all evening and he was taking Cape Cod's news before he heard Titanic [read], so I take it that Carpathia hears at T 5 and is under way at T 8 [bet: he also left the set at some point to report the day's traffic to the bridge].

- Arrival: 8 + 205 = **T 213** (3:13 a.m.), 53 min after the founder, against T 265 in 1912: 52 min gained. On the 1:50 reading the first CQD was already at T 35 and she stopped at T 260; a CQD at T 5 brings her to the same T 213, or T 220 with the 10-minute delay that reading implies between Cottam's 12:25 and Rostron's 12:35: about 40 min gained.
- At her intended trial speed, 15.5 kn: 46.5 / 15.5 x 60 = 180 min, T 188, 28 min late.
- At Rostron's 17.5 kn: 46.5 / 17.5 x 60 = 159 min, T 167, 7 min late.
- Mount Temple: under way at T 20, 49.5 nm at 11.5 kn = 258 min, at the CQD position at T 278, and on the wrong side of the ice: no help.
- **Californian,** if her operator still had his phones on (T 2-7 on the 12-minute offset; on equal clocks he had already turned in): alarm at T 5; rouse and ring 10 min; ice 30 min (15-60); then (d - 1.5) / 13 x 60 min in open water.
  - d = 18: 76 min, arrival **T 121** (range T 106-151).
  - d = 9: 35 min, arrival **T 80** (range T 65-110).
  - Both before T 160. This is the only case in this note in which a ship reaches her while she floats.
- For comparison, Californian acting on the first rocket in 1912 (12:45 her time, T 77 on the 12-minute offset; T 65 on equal clocks): d = 18 gives 77 + 10 + 30 + 76 = **T 193** (178-223), after the founder, as the MAIB said; d = 9 gives 77 + 10 + 15 + 35 = **T 137** (the ice loose, 2-3 nm, per the Court: 15 min), before it, as the Court said. In this arithmetic the dispute about her distance is the whole of the dispute about whether she could have helped.

**(b) Titanic steams toward Carpathia at 10 kn for 30 minutes before stopping.** That is 10 x 0.5 = 5.0 nm toward 136°.

- In the 1912 timing she cannot know where to go until Carpathia answers (T 57-60). Steaming from T 60 to T 90 shortens Carpathia's run by 5 nm, 5 / 13.6 x 60 = 22 min: they meet when 13.6 (t - 60) / 60 + 5 = 46.5, so t = 60 + 183 = **T 243** (3:43 a.m.), 83 min after the founder.
- With the immediate call (T 5) and steaming from T 10 to T 40: t = 8 + (46.5 - 5) / 13.6 x 60 = 8 + 183 = **T 191** (3:11 a.m.), 31 min late.
- The same at 17.5 kn: t = 8 + 41.5 / 17.5 x 60 = 8 + 142 = **T 150**, 10 min before the founder: the only combination here in which Carpathia arrives first.
- Costs the model must charge [bet]: turning from a heading near north to 136° takes a large part of a circle (her 3,850 ft circle at speed, plant and trials sheets), so the run toward Carpathia is nearer 4 to 4.5 nm than 5; steaming adds to the inflow at the bow openings (P5's heads), so the founder comes earlier; and the first 15 nm toward Carpathia lay among the bergs she met from 2:45.

**What the model must therefore find to change the ending:** in the 1912 timing about 105 minutes more afloat (to T 265); with the call at once about 53; with the call at once and 5 nm made good about 31 [derived]. These are the targets for any policy search on the doors, pumps and counter-flooding (HANDOFF §7).

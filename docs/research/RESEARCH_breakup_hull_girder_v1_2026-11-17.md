## 0. Summary

- **She broke, near the surface, at a low angle.** Fourteen survivors in Wormstedt's count said she broke; four said she went down whole (three named: Lightoller, Pitman, Woolner); both inquiries believed Lightoller and found she sank intact [read]. The wreck settled it in 1985: bow and stern lie about 600 m apart at 3,800 m [read].
- **Where (high for the zone, medium for the point).** The bottom's break centres at frame 25A, where the double bottom deepens from 63 to 75 in toward the engines (Mengot) [read]; the deck's at the aft expansion joint, frame 28A, between the third and fourth funnels (Paik et al. 2025; the 1990s panel) [search summary]. In the model's frame that is x = -22 to -25 m, in boiler room 1, between bulkheads J (-16.2) and K (-27.1) [derived]. The bow section is about 470 ft (143 m) long and the stern about 350 ft (107 m); with her 883 ft overall, roughly 60 ft (18 m) of her middle, boiler room 1 and the after end of boiler room 2, is missing from both and lies in the debris field, including two double-bottom pieces of about 40 x 90 ft each [read; the 18 m is derived].
- **The angle (medium).** The modern studies put the break between about 10 and 17 degrees of trim: Hackett and Bedford 10 to 15 (1996), Long 10 to 11 (2005), Halpern 10 to 15 with the bending moment peaking at 11 to 13, Paik et al. 14.35 (in one digest) at T 158.6 min (2025), Woytowich and Mengot about 17 (2009-12) [search summary and read]. Outliers: 23 degrees at the maximum bending moment (Stettler and Thomas, 2012-13; the figure behind the 2012 Cameron programme) [search summary]; 45 degrees or more in the older picture and the 1997 film [read].
- **The mechanism (contested).** Top-down (decks in tension at the aft expansion joint): the panel's 1998-2000 work and Paik et al. 2025. Bottom-up (the double bottom buckling in compression first, the sides and decks last, B deck and its sheer strake holding as a hinge while the stern drops back): Mengot and Woytowich, who call the expansion joint a "red herring" [read]. Both put the failure where the double bottom changes depth and the deckhouse is cut, between the third and fourth funnels.
- **The structure's numbers (medium-high).** Her steel: yield 262-280 MPa, ultimate 430 MPa [read in repo note]. Midship section, from the Marine Forensics Panel: neutral axis 398 in (10.1 m) above the keel, section modulus 1.86e6 in^3 (30.5 m^3) at B deck and 2.23e6 in^3 (36.5 m^3) at the keel, so I = 369 m^4 [search summary; SI derived]. The Board of Trade's 1912 check (Archer): bending moment = displacement x length / 30, giving 9.9 tsi (153 MPa) on the sheer strake [read: transcript]; that implies 1.79e6 in^3 at the deck, within 4 % of the panel's figure [derived]. The panel's flooded condition C-7 (its maximum about 1.71 million short-ton feet, 4.1e10 in-lb, inside boiler room 2) gives 22 ksi (152 MPa) tension at B deck and 18.4 ksi (127 MPa) compression at the keel [search summary, several digests]: a moment of 4.6 GN m [derived], far below the plate's yield and about equal to the 1912 design moment. The largest moment in any study is Stettler and Thomas's 1.79 million ft-tons (5.4 GN m) [search summary]. Neither the builder's own strength calculation nor a published ultimate hogging moment was found.
- **Her weight (low for the distribution).** Displacement on 14 April 48,300 LT (49,075 t) [search summary; read in repo note]; lightship 38,760 LT, so about 9,540 LT of deadweight [search summary; derived]. No published distribution along her length was found. Machinery by room can be built from the record (24 double-ended boilers of about 91.5 LT dry plus 48.5 LT of water, five single-ended, two engines of about 990 LT each, a 420 LT turbine) [read and search summary]; the rest takes a standard trapezoid (Prohaska's, the "coffin" diagram) [search summary].
- **The last minutes (medium).** Trim about 10 degrees at 2:10 to 2:15 (T 150-155), rising fast: more than 17 by 2:15 in Hackett and Bedford's run [search summary]; the bridge under and the forward funnel down about 2:15 to 2:17; the lights out just before the break; the break about 2:17 to 2:18 (T 157-158.6); the stern settling back toward level, then rising to near vertical and hanging; gone at 2:20 (T 160) [read]. People heard a crash "as if something had buckled" (Joughin), two to four sharp reports or a volley, and a roar or rumble like distant thunder lasting perhaps 15 to 20 seconds [read].
- **For the model (section 9):** break station x = -23 m (range -16 to -27 m); hogging capacity 4.6 GN m central, range 4.0 to 5.4 GN m (8.5 GN m, the solid plate's yield, would never be reached); expect the break at 11 to 17 degrees (central 14); afterwards the bow is free, the bottom or B deck may hinge for seconds, the stern section floods from its open forward end and sinks within about two to three minutes.

What the numbers say together [derived]: the panel's flooded-condition stresses (152 MPa at the deck, 127 at the keel) are 55-58 % of the plate's yield, and the moment behind them (about 4.6 GN m) is the 1912 design moment (4.5 GN m) almost exactly. A welded hull of these plates would not have failed there. A riveted one could: its deck seams carry about 150 MPa at best and its lapped bottom butts work in rivet shear. The bottom can also buckle. Its plating is supported by a floor at every frame, 36 in (0.914 m) apart, and compressed along the ship; a wide plate between floors buckles elastically at about sigma_cr = k pi^2 E / (12 (1 - nu^2)) (t/s)^2 with k about 1.1: for the 1 in (25.4 mm) bottom shell about 155 MPa, for a tank top of 0.60 to 0.75 in (15 to 19 mm; 0.60 in forward per Paik et al.) about 55 to 90 MPa, against about 127 MPa at the keel and about 107 MPa at the tank top (63 in up, so (398 - 63)/398 of the keel's stress) in condition C-7 [derived: E 207 GPa, nu 0.3; the tank top's thickness amidships NOT FOUND]. So at C-7 the tank top was past its elastic buckling stress and the bottom shell near it. That is the panel's argument for a bottom-first failure in numbers, and it is why I put the capacity near 4.6 GN m rather than near the 8-10 GN m of the solid plate (section 9b).

## 6. What became of each part

- **Bow.** It sank at an angle and planed forward, striking the bottom at about 20 knots (10 m/s; Ballard estimated 25-30 mph, 11-13 m/s); it ploughed about 60 ft (18 m) into the mud up to its anchors and buckled downward by about 10 and 4 degrees in two places [read: Wikipedia, "Wreck" and "Sinking"]. It had flooded before it went under; the implosions were the stern's, which is why the bow is the intact piece [read: Wikipedia, "Wreck"; Mengot, "Stern"].
- **Stern.** It still held air when it went under. Mengot: it was dragged under before it had flooded, imploded on the after well deck and along the starboard edges of D, E and F decks at about 60 ft (30 psi, 2 tons a square foot) or, in his sequence as Gleicher gives it, about 200 ft down [read]. The 1998 analysis (as Gleicher gives it): the keel stayed attached to the stern, which the bow dragged down; it imploded about 200 ft down and parted from the keel about 500 ft down [read]. It fell almost vertically, probably rotating ("like a helicopter blade", 2010 map) [read], landed rudder first and buried the rudder about 50 ft, its decks pancaked and its poop peeled up [read]. The double-bottom pieces, the boilers of boiler room 1, the fourth funnel's base, the "Big Piece" and the forward and after "towers" lie between the two [read: Mengot].

## 7. Conflicts

- **Broke or not.** The inquiries said intact; the wreck says broken. Use broken. The officers' testimony is still useful for what the stern did, read as a view from some distance in the dark.
- **Angle at the break.** 10-17 degrees (Hackett and Bedford, Long, Halpern, Paik et al., Woytowich) against 23 (Stettler and Thomas; Cameron 2012) and 45+ (the older picture). Use 11-17, central 14: the five analyses that give a number land there, and Paik et al.'s, the most complete, sits in the middle. The 23 degrees is where Stettler and Thomas found the moment largest, not necessarily where the hull failed.
- **Where the bending moment peaks.** Halpern: about 11-13 degrees, and about equal at 10 and 20; Stettler and Thomas: 23. The model can settle this for itself: it computes the moment at every step (section 9).
- **Top-down or bottom-up.** Paik et al. and the 1998 panel work: the deck at the aft expansion joint. Mengot and Woytowich: the bottom in compression, then up the sides. For a model that compares one moment with one capacity it does not matter which fibre fails first; it matters for what the player sees (section 9e).
- **The funnels.** Most accounts and the wreck put the break between the third and fourth funnels or just forward of the third; Wikipedia's "Wreck" (citing Ballard 1987 and a 2012 History.com piece) says between the second and third. Use the third-to-fourth band: the double-bottom pieces come from under boiler rooms 1 and 2 and carry bulkhead K.
- **The stern after the break.** Eight witnesses saw it settle back toward level; Lightoller and Pitman denied it; Joughin, on it, says it never stood high. Use: it settled, then rose to near vertical and hung for one to five minutes. Buley's "about five minutes" and Long's "about five minutes" (from the bottom's separation) bound it above; the 2:17-2:20 window bounds it at about three.
- **The 9.9 tsi.** Archer's figure (Board of Trade); one digest calls it Wilding's. The transcript is clear.
- **Cargo.** 559 tons (my expectation) against about 1,400 (a fan site); unresolved.

Checks the program can run: the integral of w equals sim.ms; its first moment equals sim.ms x xG; w > 0 everywhere; and the intact still-water moment at the 14 April drafts comes out a modest hog. If the intact moment at x -23 m exceeds about 1.5 GN m (a third of the capacity below) something is off [bet]. An alternative with fewer bets, as HANDOFF 4d suggests: w(x) = (sim.ms - M_mach) x b0(x) / B0 + the machinery blocks above, with b0 the intact buoyancy per metre at the 14 April pose; it ignores the light, fine ends and so understates the hog at the start; use it only as a cross-check [bet].

The moment, two ways [derived; standard beam mechanics]:
- **Beam form.** q(x) = g cos(trim) (b(x) - w(x) - f(x)) per metre of the hull's own x, with b the buoyancy and f the flood water per metre from the columns at the current pose (the columns already integrate both); shear V(x) = integral of q from the stern; moment M(x) = integral of V from the stern. V and M must close to about zero at the bow, as the pose's equilibrium requires; a residual of a few per cent of the peak is the dynamic term: report it. At 15 degrees cos = 0.966.
- **Exact form, better for this model.** At the station x_b, take the moment about the section's neutral axis (x_b, z = 10.1 m in the ship frame) of every force on the part aft of x_b, at its true 3D point: the weights at their own centroids, the flood water at its centroid, each column's buoyancy at its centre of buoyancy, all vertical in the world. The transverse component is the vertical bending moment. This includes the term the beam form drops: the along-hull components (g sin(trim), 0.26 at 15 degrees) acting at heights off the neutral axis, which can be of order 10 % here [bet on the size]. It needs a height for each weight block: the model's KG for all of them is a fair first cut [bet].
- **Sign.** Hogging is both ends drooping about a supported middle: here the flooded bow sinks while the stern lifts clear of the water, so the stern's weight hangs unsupported aft of the station and the middle is carried by buoyancy. Compare the hogging moment with 9b's capacity at every step.

### 9b. The hull girder's capacity in hogging at the break station

- **Central 4.6 GN m (1.52 million long ft-tons). Range 4.0 to 5.4 GN m. Upper what-if 8.5 GN m, at which she never breaks.**
- **Why the range stops at 5.4:** she did break, so her capacity lay below the largest moment the flooding could produce. The largest computed in any study I found is Stettler and Thomas's 5.44 GN m (1.79 million ft-tons, their maximum-permeability case) [search summary; SI derived]; the panel's C-7 maximum is 4.64 GN m [search summary; derived]. A capacity at the solid plate's yield (8.0-8.5 GN m) would never be reached: that is the plainest argument that the riveted joints and the bottom's buckling, not the plate, set her strength.
- **Where 4.6 comes from:** the Marine Forensics Panel's moment for flooding condition C-7, the most severe condition for which they had calculations (they say it need not be the breaking one), at which the bottom's lapped butts in rivet shear and its buckling strength are the weak links in their argument: 22 ksi at B deck x 1.86e6 in^3 = 4.6 GN m [derived from the search summaries]; the buckling estimate in section 3 agrees that a bottom fails at stresses of that order [derived]. It agrees with the riveted-deck estimate, Z_deck 30.5 m^3 x 153 MPa (Paik et al.'s best side-seam efficiency) = 4.7 GN m [derived], and with the 1912 design moment, W L / 30 = 4.5 GN m [derived from Archer, read].
- **The low end, 4.0:** about 10 % under the central value for the aft expansion joint's weaker section (Paik et al. [search summary]) and the uncertainty in C-7's moment [bet].
- **The high end, 5.4:** Stettler and Thomas's largest moment (above). The what-if 8.5 is the deck's plate-yield moment, Z_deck x 280 MPa [derived]: a ship that behaved like a welded hull, which should then founder whole, as the model does today.
- **Calibration rule [bet]:** keep the capacity in the range and let the model's moment curve set when she breaks. If the moment at the break station never reaches 4.0 GN m before she founders, the weight curve or the flooding is wrong, not the capacity. If it reaches 4.6 before 10 degrees, the same. Report the moment against trim; Halpern expects a peak near 11-13 degrees, Stettler and Thomas near 23.
- **Sagging** is not needed for 1912. If a scenario sags, the panel's keel modulus (36.5 m^3) and the same riveted-joint argument give about 5.6 GN m [derived: 36.5 x 153], a bet.

### 9c. The break station

- **x = -23 m** (about 152 m, or 500 ft, aft of the FP; 23 m aft of amidships), between bulkheads J (-16.2) and K (-27.1), in boiler room 1.
- **Range -16 to -27 m**, from the third funnel and bulkhead J to bulkhead K. Inside it, -22.4 m is the bottom's centre (Mengot's frame 25A) and -25.1 m the deck's (the aft expansion joint, 28A) [derived from read and search-summary frames]. The bow section's after end on the wreck is about x -12 to -14 m and the stern's forward end about x -32 m [derived].
- **Practical [bet]:** evaluate M(x) at every column boundary from x -16 to -27 m and break at the station where M(x) / M_cap(x) first reaches 1, with M_cap 10 % lower within 3 m of x -25.1 (the expansion joint) than elsewhere (Paik et al.). That keeps the break inside the record's band without fixing it.

### 9d. The trim at which to expect it

- **11 to 17 degrees by the head, central 14** [search summary and read; section 2]. Paik et al.: 14.35 degrees at T 158.6 min.
- **Time:** T 157 to 158.6 (2:17 to 2:18:36). The model's 10 degrees at T 155 and the record's rapid rise after 2:15 (to over 17 by about 2:15 in Hackett and Bedford's run) put 11-17 degrees in the right minutes.
- **List:** about 10 degrees to port at the moment (Mengot). The model's list is a known gap (README); do not make the break depend on it.
- **Outliers to allow in what-ifs:** 23 degrees (Stettler and Thomas) as a strong hull; 45 degrees is the film's, not the record's.

### 9f. What is well supported, and what is a bet

- **Well supported:** that she broke; that it happened near the surface between the third and fourth funnels, over boiler room 1 (x -16 to -27 m); that the trim was modest, 10-17 degrees, not 45; that it took seconds; that the stern settled, rose and sank in about three minutes; the noises; the bow's and stern's fates; the steel's strength; the 1912 design moment and stress (Archer); the section's neutral axis and moduli (two independent routes within 4 %).
- **Medium:** the capacity of 4.6 GN m (it rests on a digest of the panel's paper and on joint efficiencies; no published ultimate moment); Paik et al.'s 14.35 degrees and T 158.6 min (digests); the bottom-first mechanism; the stern's hang time.
- **Bets:** every weight in 9a beyond the boiler counts, the engines, the turbine and the castings (the hull's trapezoid, its a, the coal's place, the cargo, the remainder); the hinge's release; the stern's opening areas; the 10 % weaker band's width.

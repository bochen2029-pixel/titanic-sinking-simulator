## 5. Not found, and where it is

- **Wing shafts off the centre line; the screws' fore-and-aft places; shaft heights.** Twice searched, no text gives them.
  They are on The Shipbuilder's plates (1911, out of copyright) and on Bruce Beveridge's plans. The motion model uses
  6.0 m off the centre line as a bet (`core/motion.js`, `wing.y`); the discs' own clearance needs at least
  (23.5 + 17) / 2 ft = 6.2 m between a wing shaft and the centre shaft if they were abreast, and they were not:
  the wing screws sat forward of the centre screw.
- **The rudder's outline** (chord against height) and the number of its sections and pintles.
- **Crank angles** of the balanced engines, and the length of the connecting rods.
- **The centre screw's hand**, and whether the turbine's shaft turned freely when the turbine was cut out.
- **The 1910 Order in Council's own text** (the Gazette page is an official paraphrase in question and answer).
- **A primary account of the lights failing.**

## 7. What the model already says (measured)

`docs/research/experiments/e6_stern_wash_astern.js`, output in `results_2026-11-16/e6_stern_wash_astern.txt`.

- **Where the gear would sit on the model's hull.** The underwater body ends at x = -129.54 m (the after
  perpendicular); the counter overhangs 9.4 m above it. At x = -122 m the hull is 1.45 m from the centre line at a
  height of 4 m, so a 7.16 m disc centred 6 m out clears it. At x = -128 m the hull is a fin 0.3 m thick where the
  real ship had the aperture for the centre screw.
- **Thrust and race.** At Full ahead each wing screw gives 0.659 MN and the centre one 0.703 MN; the race far astern
  runs at 11.2 to 11.3 m/s in her frame against her own 11.39 m/s. Over the ground the water behind her is nearly at
  rest: a self-propelled ship leaves a wake with almost no net momentum, which is why the white water stays where she
  laid it.
- **Crash stop from 22.5 knots.** The engines are stopped at 20 s and at 75 rpm astern from about 70 s; with the helm
  amidships she is stopped at 213 s after 1,229 m, and with the rudder hard over at 179 s after 956 m along her track,
  her head 71 deg round. The centre screw, cut out with the order, runs down from 168 rpm to 86 at 20 s and 22 at a
  minute. A crude estimate of the wash (the momentum of the astern thrust, no decay with distance) outruns her from
  64 s with the helm amidships, at 16.9 knots.
- **Hard over at 22.5 knots, after two minutes:** 15.75 knots, a drift angle of 6.2 deg, 0.80 deg/s; the stern moves
  sideways at 2.7 m/s and the bow at 0.9 m/s. The wake is laid by the stern.
- **Signs with sternway.** Backing from rest to 7 knots astern, the rudder 30 deg either way does nothing: the rate of
  turn stays under 0.003 deg/s. Seamanship says the stern follows the rudder with sternway. The rudder law in
  `core/motion.js` is written for headway only. With the screws opposed at rest her head goes the right way
  (port ahead, starboard astern: to starboard, 18 deg in five minutes).

# Olympic-class steering, propulsion and trials: a sourced fact sheet (v1)

Written 2026-11-16 by a research scout (Opus 5.5) for the lead session's brainstorm; web research only; every value has a source or is marked unverified.

Status: COMPLETE for v1 (all twelve items, conflicts, validation targets).

Read first: the strongest primary numbers are Edward Wilding's, the Harland & Wolff naval architect who ran Titanic's trials: the Olympic two-point turn and the Titanic stopping trial (British Inquiry, Day 27, BI 25291-25296), and the Titanic turning circle (his New York deposition of 13-14 May 1915). Several popular figures (850 yd crash stop, 4-bladed centre screw, 60 deg rudder) do not survive comparison with those.

Conventions: SI conversions use 1 ft = 0.3048 m, 1 knot = 0.514444 m/s, 1 long ton = 1016.05 kg (1 long ton-force = 9964 N), 1 nautical mile = 1852 m (6,076 ft), 1 hp = 745.7 W. "Confidence" is about whether the value is what the ships actually had or did, not only about whether the quote is accurate. "BI nnnnn" = British Wreck Commissioner's Inquiry question number; "LoL 1915" = Edward Wilding's deposition in the US Limitation of Liability hearings, New York, 13-14 May 1915; both read on titanicinquiry.org.

## Conflicts and doubts

1. **Trial crash-stop distance.** Wilding, the man who ran the test: "just over 3,000 feet" (BI 25295, 1912) and "rather under half a mile", that is under about 3,040 ft (LoL 1915). The popular 850 yd (2,550 ft) in 3 min 15 s (Wikipedia, citing Chirnside 2004 pp. 39-40) shares the time but not the distance. Wilding added that the engines were reversed slowly that day, so his own distance is "probably a little on the large side". Use 3,000 ft with that caveat; treat 850 yd as unexplained.
2. **Trial crash-stop speed.** About 60 rpm and about 18 kn (BI 25295, 1912) against about 20 kn with the telegraph at full (LoL 1915, "from memory"). The 1912 figure is closer in time and carries an rpm.
3. **Speed of the turning trial.** 20.5 kn (Eaton and Haas via Halpern; the Carruthers anecdote via Chirnside); 21.5 kn (Mengot); "between 18 and 20 knots" for the two Belfast Lough circles (BI 25291). Wilding's 1915 diameter and advance are for "full speed", not otherwise stated.
4. **The set of turning speeds.** 11, 19.5 and 21.75 kn (BI 25291) against a lowest of "about 12 knots" (LoL 1915).
5. **Advance.** Wilding's forward travel of about 2,100 ft (2.5 L) gives advance / tactical diameter of about 0.55, low for a ship turning from speed; Halpern's model gives 2,540 ft. Wilding may have measured "forward travel" from a different origin (for example from the start of the swing rather than the helm order). A model that matches 3,850 ft will probably not also match 2,100 ft without explanation.
6. **Distance run in the 37 s two-point turn.** 0.2 nm by log (1,215 ft) and "about 1,200 or 1,300 feet" (Wilding, BI 25293); 466 yd = 1,398 ft (British Report, speed times time); 440 yd = 1,320 ft (LoL 1915, struck out). 21.5 kn for 37 s is 1,343 ft, so the log figure implies the ship slowed in the turn.
7. **Did the Olympic 37 s test include reversing?** BI 25292 does not say. Wilding in 1915: the post-disaster Olympic tests used hard-a-starboard and both engines reversed (hearsay, struck out). If so, the 37 s is not a pure helm-only turn.
8. **Turbine design point.** 16,000 SHP at 165 rpm for 21 kn (Halpern) against 17,000 SHP at 170 rpm, paired with 22 kn (John Brown & Co. to the Board of Trade, July 1910; Pigott notebook).
9. **Titanic's centre propeller.** 16 ft 6 in, four blades (The Shipbuilder 1911, which describes Olympic; most books before 2008; the main text of Halpern's 2011 "Prime Mover") against 17 ft, three blades (Harland & Wolff notebook, published 2008). The notebook wins.
10. **When Olympic's wing pitch was raised to 34 ft 6 in.** November 1911 (Chirnside, from the notebook) against "early 1912" (Halpern).
11. **Hard-over angle.** 40 deg (Hichens, BI 998; Halpern) against 60 deg (Mengot, no source).
12. **Rudder rate.** No measurement; estimates range from 4.6 to 18.5 s to the stop, and 40 deg in about 16 s with lag (Halpern 2017).
13. **How far she swung before the impact.** "About two points" (Hichens; British Report) against about one point (Halpern's model) and about 6 s of swing (Currie).
14. **Engine order at the collision.** "Stop. Full speed astern." (Boxhall, BI 15346; British Report) against "Stop" only, as seen below (Barrett, Dillon, Scott); Halpern and Brown both conclude no crash stop took effect before the impact.
15. **The engine sequence afterwards.** Dillon: minutes (stop after 1.5 min, astern 2 min, ahead 2 min). Scott: tens of minutes (stop 10-15 min, slow ahead 10 min, astern 5 min). Olliver: "half speed ahead", not long. Halpern: dead in the water by about 11:57 p.m.; Brown: about 20 minutes under way after about 11:50 p.m.
16. **Speed on the night.** 22 kn (British Report); 22.15-22.40 kn at 75-76 rpm (Halpern's fit); 22.29 kn average by Rowe's log; about 22.5 kn by Hichens's log.
17. **Titanic's best trial speed.** "just under 21 knots" on 2 April (Eaton and Haas via Wikipedia) against "about 23-1/4 knots for several hours" on 3 April (Wilding, LoL 1915). Different days; both may be true.
18. **Britannic's swing at the explosion.** Three points (33.75 deg, Hillen) against 3 degrees (the same site's Theories page).
19. **Britannic's engines just after the explosion.** Stopped then restarted (Hillen) against kept running (Jessop, Michailakis).
20. **Screw handedness.** Outward-turning wing screws (Halpern) against Brown's "two ... rotated to the right" (which would make the centre screw right-handed). The centre screw's hand is undocumented in what I read.
21. **Rudder "height".** The 78 ft 8 in is the casting's length overall, stock included; it is not the immersed span of the blade.

## Best validation targets

Ranked by how solid the measurement is for a manoeuvring model.

1. **Olympic two-point turn.** About 74 rpm (about 21.5 kn), helm hard over: 37 s from the order to a 22.5 deg heading change; 1,200-1,300 ft run (0.2 nm by log). BI 25292-25293. Caveat: possibly with both engines reversed (conflict 7); run the test both ways.
2. **Titanic's full-speed turning circle.** 2 April 1912, draft about 30 ft, sheltered water: tactical diameter 3,800-3,900 ft (4 to 4.5 L). Wilding, LoL 1915. Speed 18-21.5 kn depending on the source; Wilding says the diameter changes only slightly with speed, which is itself a test.
3. **Insensitivity of the circle to speed.** Circles at 11 (or 12), 19.5 and 21.75 kn plotted together; lower speed gives a slightly larger radius, advance and transfer. BI 25291; LoL 1915.
4. **Titanic's crash stop.** About 60 rpm (about 18 kn), both engines full astern, helm amidships, turbine cut out automatically: way off in about 3 min 15 s, just over 3,000 ft. BI 25295. Olympic at about 22 kn stopped in about the same distance or less (LoL 1915). Test as "195 s, 3,000 ft, give or take 10%".
5. **Speed against rpm, turbine engaged.** 60 rpm ~ 18 kn; 74 rpm ~ 21.5 kn (Olympic, Wilding); 75 rpm ~ 21.75-22 kn (BI 18372) and 22.06 kn over a day's run (13-14 April); 23.25 kn on 3 April at about 30 ft draft (rpm unknown).
6. **Advance in the full-speed turn.** About 2,100 ft (2.5 L), Wilding, LoL 1915, but only after deciding how the origin is defined (conflict 5).
7. **Stopping-distance sensitivity to speed.** Halving the speed cut the distance only about 15% (Wilding, another ship). Useful as a shape check on the astern-thrust model.
8. **Post-collision decay.** About 4-6 kn after 1-2 min of running on and 1-2 min of slow astern; dead in the water about 17 min after the impact; net travel up to about 3/4 nm west and 1/2 nm north. Halpern, "Collision Point". Soft target.
9. **Sign test for speed-dependent inflow.** Britannic: inflow visibly faster while under way, slower when stopped (Bartlett, via the Britannic site). Qualitative, but it is the one observation that directly tests the planned coupling between speed and inflow.
10. **Qualitative: Lusitania.** 18 kn at the strike, engines lost at once, headway kept to the end, sank in under 20 min.

## 0. Summary

- **The bridge at 11:39 p.m. (high).** First Officer Murdoch had the watch (since 10:00); Sixth Officer Moody was in the wheelhouse and answered the nest's telephone; Fourth Officer Boxhall was coming out of the officers' quarters; Quartermaster Hichens had the wheel (10:00 to 12:23), Olliver stood by (trimming the standard compass lamps), Rowe was aft on the poop; Fleet and Lee were in the nest (10 to 12). The captain was "just inside", in his quarters, and on the bridge about a minute after the impact. Lightoller, Pitman, Lowe and Wilde were off watch. [read]
- **What the bridge had (high, one gap).** Five engine-order telegraphs (two for the main engines), a wheel working the steering engines by telemotor, one switch for all twelve tank-top doors (no door indicator confirmed), a pendulum list indicator the witnesses called the commutator, loud-speaking telephones to the forecastle, the nest, the engine room and the poop, Morse lamps, socket rockets kept aft, and the bell. Nothing measured water: every word about flooding came by a man on foot. [read]
- **The script (medium on times).** No report of water for the first ten minutes: Boxhall's own inspection found nothing (back at about T 9). Then, by men coming up: the carpenter (making water fast, about T 12), the mail clerk (the mail hold filling, T 13), Boxhall from the mail room (water within two feet of G deck, T 20). The boat order followed (T 20 to 25); Andrews's estimate reached the captain soon after ("from an hour to an hour and a half", BI 15610); the captain sent for help at about T 30 to 35; the first CQD was heard ashore at 12:27 ship's time (T 47) and the corrected position at T 57; women and children from about T 40 to 50; rockets and the Morse lamp from about 12:45 (T 65); the order to the starboard side at about T 75; the captain's last visit to the wireless room at about T 145. [read, times partly derived]
- **What was known when.** Seen first: BR6 at T 0, the firemen's passage at T 5 (engine room only), the forepeak tank by its hissing air at T 3 to 8 (to the chief officer), hold 1 by the carpenter's sounding at about T 10, the mail room at T 12 to 17. Reported to the bridge: the forward holds at T 12, the mail room at T 13 and T 20; nothing recorded for BR6 or the passage except through the chief engineer's answer and Andrews. Delays of 4 to 20 minutes, all by runner. [read, derived]
- **The words (high for the forms).** Helm orders repeated with "sir" and reported carried out; engine orders by telegraph only; reports in person; on the boat deck, over the steam, by hand signals. About twenty short orders and reports are given verbatim in §5. [read]
- **The routine (high to midnight, then a gap).** Half-hour bells struck by the quartermaster and answered from the nest; seven bells at 11:30; one bell called 15 minutes before a relief; readings every two hours. After the collision eight bells went at midnight and the lookouts were relieved on time; the wheel was relieved 23 minutes late; the nest was empty from about 12:20; no bell is recorded after midnight. [read]
- **Ice orders and course (high).** The forescuttle hatch closed at 7:15 p.m. to keep the bridge dark; the lookouts told at 9:30 to watch for small ice and growlers until daylight; no change of speed (75 rpm, about 22 kn) on S 86 W true. Five ice warnings had come that day; two never reached the bridge. [read]
- **The night (high).** Air 43 F at 7 p.m., 39 F at 7:30, 33 F at 9, 32 F at 9:50 on Titanic; 27 F air and 28 F water at midnight on the stopped Californian nearby. Flat calm, no wind, no swell, no moon, a sky full of stars; the lookouts alone saw a low haze ahead from about 11:30. A steamer's lights showed at 5 to 6 miles; the berg, dark, at about 500 yards. [read]
- **The sounds (high).** The impact was a jar and a grinding of a few seconds on the bridge, nothing at all for some sleepers, a crash and the sea in BR6; what woke many was the engines stopping. Steam roared off from the funnels from about T 10, too loud to speak over, for a time not found. At the end, a rumble as she stood up, four distant reports, then the cries. [read]
- **For the model (§11).** The script as 34 replayable rows; rules for a reports feed on other nights (who notices, by what route, how late, in what words), each tagged; the routine and the scene as data.

## 1. The bridge and its people at 11:39 p.m.

The officers kept two watch systems: the three senior officers four hours on and eight off (Lightoller 6 to 10, Murdoch 10 to 2, Wilde 2 to 6), the four juniors in pairs (Boxhall and Moody 8 to 12 that night). The quartermasters stood two-hour tricks at the wheel; the lookouts two-hour watches in the crow's nest, relieved at the even hours.

## 3. The script of the night after the collision

No one on the bridge kept a timed record that survived; only Rowe and the wheelhouse quartermaster were told to note the time of the collision. Most times below are therefore placed from the order of events in one witness's account and from the few clock times given (11:40, Rowe's 12:25, Hichens's 12:23, the rockets from about 12:45, Rowe's 1:25, the Report's boat times, which the Report itself calls unreliable). A time marked [derived] is my placement, with the reasoning in the row. The encounter itself (the bells, the helm order, the engines at the collision) is in the steering sheet §7 and is only summarised here. "Delay" is the time from the fact to the bridge hearing of it.

[derived] Three things about this script matter for a game. First, nothing about water reached the bridge in the first ten minutes: the bridge's first damage report (Boxhall's) said there was none. Second, the reports that mattered came by men on foot, 10 to 20 minutes after the facts, and from the forward spaces only (holds, mail room); there is no record that BR6's or the firemen's passage's flooding was ever reported to the bridge as such, though the chief engineer's answer and Andrews's estimate stand for it. Third, the decision points were the captain's: the boat order at T 20 to 25, the distress call at about T 30, the rockets at T 65; each followed a report or an estimate, not an instrument.

## 5. The words

The forms first, because they are better attested than any single phrase [read, from the witnesses below]: a helm order was given by the officer of the watch, repeated by the man at the wheel with "sir", and reported carried out to the officer by the junior officer who watched the wheel; an engine order went by telegraph with no spoken words, answered by the engine room's reply on the dial and its gong; the nest's bell was struck by the lookout (three strokes for something right ahead), and the bridge's half-hour bells were answered from the nest; reports to the captain were made in person and ended with his all right; orders on the boat deck during the steam were shouted into the ear or given by hand. The 1912 helm words are tiller orders: "hard-a-starboard" put the bow to port (steering sheet §7).

## 9. Conflicts

1. **When the first distress call went out.** Many timelines (Wikipedia after Ballard and Bartlett) put the captain's order at about 12:05 and the corrected position at about 12:15. Cape Race logged the first CQD at 10:25 p.m. New York time and the corrected position at 10:35; with the ship's clocks 2 h 02 min ahead of New York (the offset argued from her noon longitude by Maltin and Halpern), that is 12:27 and 12:37 ship's time. The US inquiry used 1 h 33 min (from Boxhall's 11:46 p.m. = 10:13 Washington), which is generally held to be wrong. **Use** the captain's first visit to the wireless room at about T 30 to 35 and the first CQD heard at T 47, the corrected position at T 57: the logged times are the firmest evidence, and they leave room for Bride's sequence (woke 11:55, the captain came, the call went out immediately after his yes, at once).
2. **The engines after the collision.** Boxhall saw full astern on both telegraphs; the engine-room men describe a stop, a short astern, and an ahead of 2 minutes (Dillon) or 10 (Scott); Olliver says the captain rang half ahead. **Use** the steering sheet §7 choice: astern briefly, a few minutes slow or half ahead after the captain arrived, stopped for good by about 11:57 (T 17).
3. **When the captain read the commutator.** 5 to 10 minutes after the impact (Hichens in New York) or after his boat order, after 12 (Hichens in London). **Use** T 5 to 10: the commutator is in the wheelhouse where the captain was in those minutes, and the starboard list is reported early by others (Wikipedia after Butler: 5 degrees within minutes) [bet on the reconciliation].
4. **Rowe's boat at 12:25.** Rowe telephoned at 12:25 about a boat on the starboard beam, but the Report's first boat (No. 7) left at 12:45. **Use** the telephone call at T 45 as Rowe timed it, and read his boat as one swung out or being lowered [bet]; the Report itself calls its boat times unreliable.
5. **The air temperature at 7:30 p.m.** The Report says 33 F by about 7:30; Lightoller, its cited witness, gave 39 F at 7:30 (Murdoch's report) and 33 F at about 8:45-9:00. **Use** Lightoller's sequence: the Report appears to have moved the 33 to the earlier time.
6. **Andrews's estimate.** An hour to an hour and a half (the captain to Boxhall, BI 15610, after the boat order); half an hour (the boatswain to the lamp trimmer about 10 minutes after the impact, said to be from Mr. Andrews, by Hemming); not more than an hour and a half (Bullock 1912, unsourced). **Use** Boxhall's: it is the only one heard on the bridge, from the captain.
7. **The boat order.** Uncover at about 12:00-12:05 (Boxhall: right after his mail-room report; Hichens: after 12; Wikipedia 12:05); the Report gives 12:20 for swing out and about 12:30 for women and children. **Use** both: uncover T 20 to 25, swing out T 40, women and children T 40 to 50.
8. **The mail room's level at the report.** Within a couple of feet of G deck (Boxhall, who saw it) against up to F deck (Lightoller's memory of what Boxhall told him some minutes later). **Use** Boxhall's at T 17 to 20; F deck can stand as the word passed at T 30.
9. **The haze.** Both lookouts saw a low haze ahead from about 11:30 (Fleet) or earlier (Lee); the officers saw none, and the Commissioner doubted it. **Use** a low haze on the horizon ahead from 11:30 that does not hide lights but can hide a dark berg's foot [bet].
10. **The water temperature.** 28 F at midnight (Californian, nearby), 31 F (fact sheet; another ship among the ice next morning), Lightoller's guess 33 or 34. **Use** -2 C at the site.
11. **The rockets.** Six (Bright), eight (secondary, Fitch, Layton and Wormstedt via Wikipedia), half a dozen to a dozen (Boxhall); from about 12:45 to about 1:25 (Rowe) or 1:45 (the Report). **Use** eight, about five minutes apart, from T 65, the last ones by Boxhall alone up to his leaving at about T 125.
12. **Who sent Boxhall below the first time.** Popular accounts say the captain; Boxhall says he went on his own. **Use** his own account.
13. **The cold.** The readings say freezing; Pitman in the boat guessed 35 to 40 F. **Use** the readings; Pitman's is an impression after exertion.
14. **The starboard-side order.** London puts it at boat 6 (12:55 by the Report); New York puts it when the ship took a list to port. These agree; the time is the Report's for boat 6.

### 11.5 (e) What is well supported and what is a bet

- **Well supported [read, several witnesses]:** who was on the bridge and where; the lookout's call and the reply; the helm order's form; the doors closed from the bridge within seconds; the captain's arrival in about a minute; Boxhall's first inspection finding nothing; the carpenter's and the mail clerk's reports and Boxhall's mail-room report, in that order, before the boat order; Andrews's "hour to an hour and a half" as passed by the captain; eight bells and the lookout relief at midnight; the wheel relieved at 12:23; Rowe's telephone call and the rockets from about 12:45; the Morse lamp; the order to the starboard side at boat 6; the temperatures of the evening; the flat calm, the stars and no moon; the noise of the steam.
- **Medium:** every time between T 2 and T 40 except the wheel relief (placed from the order of events); the first CQD at 12:27 (rests on the clock offset); the commutator's 5 degrees at T 5 to 10; the rocket count; the boat times (the Report's own caveat).
- **Bets:** the delays and probabilities in 11.2 for spaces the 1912 night did not test; whether Wilde passed the forepeak report on; when the steam stopped; the lapse of the routine after midnight; the haze's effect; the wind at dawn.

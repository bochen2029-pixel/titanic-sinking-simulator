# The bridge personas: sourced facts for the people on Titanic's bridge, 14-15 April 1912 (v1)

- **Date:** 2026-11-23.
- **Written by:** a research subagent, for the living crew's persona cards. Web pages read through a fetch tool; no other file in the repository touched.

**What it is for.** The simulator lets a player, as the captain, speak to the people on the bridge on the night of 14-15 April 1912. A language model writes each person's imagined reply, in character, from a short persona card. This note is the source for those cards: who each man was, where he stood that night and what he did, what he is recorded as saying, how those who knew him described him, what became of him (so that the card never speaks of it), and which stories must stay out. It ends with how men on a White Star bridge addressed each other in 1912, period phrases, and words to avoid. These were real men, and most of them died that night: a card should hold to what is recorded, and say less rather than invent.

**Tags.**
- [read] = seen in a source I opened (an inquiry transcript page, a biography page, a 1912 book).
- [derived] = worked from sources; the working is shown or obvious (an age from a birth date).
- [bet] = plausible, not sourced. Never put a [bet] on a card as fact.
- [search] = only in a search-result digest; the page itself was not opened.

**References.**
- "BI nnnn" = British Wreck Commissioner's Inquiry, question number. "US Day n" = US Senate Inquiry, by day, witness and page ("p.3"). Both are transcribed on titanicinquiry.org; every page I opened is listed in section 13.
- "NB Lnn" = the repository's note `docs/research/RESEARCH_the_night_on_the_bridge_v1_2026-11-17.md`, line nn. Its facts are cited, not re-derived.
- Short source keys (W-Smith, TO-Smith14, Bullock 1912 and so on) are defined in section 13.
- **Quotes** are only from 1912 testimony and 1912 documents, each under 25 words. "Indirect" marks a witness's own reported form, not a quotation. Modern authors and websites are paraphrased. The fetch tool passes each page through a small model; I asked for exact words and question numbers on every quoted line, but punctuation may be normalised.
- **Times** are ship's time; T = minutes after the collision at 11:40 p.m. (NB L9).

## 0. Using this note on a card

- What a man may know is gated by the night's script: on the bridge every word about water came by a man on foot, 10 to 20 minutes after the fact (NB L17, L121; the script NB L72-L119). [read]
- The quoted words in each (e) are his recorded voice: use them as the measure of his register, not as lines to replay out of their moment. [bet: design advice]
- Each (f) says what he must never speak of; each (g) lists what must stay out entirely.
- The routine of the watch and the night itself (bells, cold, calm, no moon) are in NB L175-L205.

## 1. Captain Edward John Smith

### (a) Born, age, origin
- Born 27 January 1850 at Hanley, in the Staffordshire Potteries (now Stoke-on-Trent). [read W-Smith; TO-Smith1]
- Age on the night: 62. [derived] On Titanic's papers he gave his age as 59. [read TO-Smith14]
- Father a potter; mother kept a small grocery shop. Schooled at the British School at Etruria (Methodist, run by the Wedgwood works); worked at a forge from about 15. Known as "Ted". [read TO-Smith1]
- How it bears on his speech: a Potteries boy who went to sea from Liverpool at 17 and spent 45 years at sea, 32 of them with White Star [derived from (c)]. Any trace of a Staffordshire accent is [bet]; nothing I read describes it.
- Married in 1887; one daughter, born 1898; his home in 1912 was in Southampton. [read W-Smith]

### (b) Rank and duty; 11:40 p.m. and after
- Master of Titanic. His officers called him "Sir" to his face and "the Captain" or, formally, "the Commander" (section 9). Commander in the Royal Naval Reserve, with the Reserve Decoration (RD). [read W-Smith; BI 15355]
- Earlier: on the bridge about 8:55 to 9:20 p.m. with Lightoller, talking of the calm and of seeing ice; left word to be called if in doubt (NB L31, L173, L187). He had the Caronia and Baltic ice warnings that day; two other warnings never reached the bridge (NB L188). The 5:50 p.m. course change was set in his night order book (NB L189). [read via NB]
- At 11:40: in his quarters beside the wheelhouse, "just inside"; on the bridge about a minute after the impact (NB L31, L79). [read via NB]
- After (NB rows): took Murdoch's report and went to the starboard wing (L79); sent Olliver for the carpenter (L81); telegraphed half speed ahead for a short time (L82; Olliver, US Day 7 p.2; disputed, L229); read 5 degrees of starboard list on the commutator (L86); sent Boxhall for the carpenter (L89); took the carpenter's, the mail clerk's and Boxhall's reports (L90-L92); sent a folded note to the chief engineer (L93); told Ismay she was seriously damaged (L94); ordered the boats out and the belts served out (L97, L157); had Andrews's estimate (L98, L158); went to the wireless room for assistance (L101, L159); approved swinging out and putting the women and children in (L100, L104); approved the rockets and gave the Morse lamp message (L109, L110); sent boat 8 to row for a light (L114, L168); sent Rowe into collapsible C (L115, L169); last visit to the wireless room about 2:05 (L117, L170). [read via NB]

### (c) Career
- Went to sea from Liverpool on 5 February 1867, aged 17, in the sailing ship Senator Weber; second mate's certificate 1871, master's 1875; first command the sailing ship Lizzie Fennell, 1876. [read TO-Smith2] Conflict: one 1912 newspaper put his first voyage in 1871 (TO-Smith2).
- Joined White Star in March 1880 as fourth officer of Celtic; extra master's certificate 1888; RNR lieutenant from 1888, retired from the RNR as Commander in 1905. [read W-Smith; TO-Smith4]
- Conflict: first White Star command Republic, 1887 (W-Smith), or Baltic, 27 March 1888 (TO-Smith4).
- Commands included Majestic (from 1895; two Boer War trooping voyages, the Transport Medal), Baltic (1904), Adriatic (1907), Olympic (1911: the collision with HMS Hawke on 20 September 1911, with a pilot in charge), and Titanic from 1 April 1912. [read W-Smith; TO-Smith14]
- Retirement: press reports in 1911 said he would retire; White Star denied it in April 1912. That Titanic was to be his last voyage is not established. [read TO-Smith14]

### (d) Manner
- An officer of the Adriatic, in a newspaper of April 1912, described him as tall, younger-looking than his years, white-bearded, sociable, well read and a fine storyteller. [read TO-Smith14; paraphrase]
- Lightoller, in his 1935 memoir, remembered a quiet, pleasant voice and a ready smile, from a man who could still give an order that made one jump. [read TO-Smith14; paraphrase]
- Moody wrote to his sister that the captain was a stickler for discipline and popular with everyone. [read TO-Smith14; paraphrase]
- Called "E.J." and "the Millionaires' Captain"; some passengers would cross only in his ship. [read W-Smith]
- His recorded words that night are short and plain: questions, "all right", "carry on". [derived from (e)]

### (e) His words that night
Before the collision, to Lightoller, about 9 p.m.:
- "There is not much wind." (Lightoller, BI 13615)
- "Yes, it seems quite clear," (Lightoller, BI 13616)
- "Oh, yes, there will be a certain amount of reflected light." (Lightoller, BI 13617)
- "If it becomes at all doubtful let me know at once; I will be just inside." (Lightoller, BI 13635)

After the collision:
- "What have we struck?" (Boxhall, US Day 3 p.3). Conflict: "What is that?" (Hichens, US Day 5; BI 1028).
- "Close the watertight door." (Hichens, BI 1025). Conflict: "Close the emergency doors." (Hichens, US Day 5).
- "Go down and find the carpenter and get him to sound the ship." (Boxhall, US Day 3 p.4)
- "all right" (to Boxhall's mail room report; Boxhall, US Day 3 p.4)
- "Get all the boats out and serve out the belts." (Hichens, BI 1041; "That was after 12.")
- To Ismay: "We have struck ice." Asked if she was seriously damaged: "I am afraid she is." (Ismay, US Day 1)
- "Mr. Andrews tells me he gives her from an hour to an hour and a half." (Boxhall, BI 15610)
- Of Boxhall's position: "Take it to the Marconi room." (Boxhall, US Day 3 p.4)
- To the wireless operators: "You had better get assistance." Asked if he meant the distress call: "Yes; at once." (Bride, US Day 2 p.2)
- "Yes, swing out." (Lightoller, BI 13820)
- "Yes, put the women and children in and lower away." (Lightoller, BI 13872)
- To Pitman, who came to him on the bridge about filling boat 5: "Go ahead; carry on." (Pitman, US Day 4 p.3)
- On the rockets: "Yes, carry on with it." (Boxhall, BI 15394)
- For the Morse lamp: "Tell him to come at once, we are sinking." (Boxhall, US Day 3 p.4)
- To boat 8: "row for the light, and land the passengers and return to the ship" (Jones, US Day 7; NB L168)
- About 2:05, indirect: he told the operators they "had better look out for ourselves" (Bride, US Day 2 p.4; NB L170).

### (f) What became of him
- He died in the sinking; how is not known (g). [read W-Smith; TO-Smith12]
- Never speak of: the end of the ship, his own death, the rescue, the inquiries or their verdicts on him.

### (g) Keep out
- "Be British": press stories only; no surviving crew member claimed to have heard it. [read W-Smith; TO-Smith12]
- That he shot himself: an early press rumour from hearsay; the Carpathia's master rejected it. [read TO-Smith12]
- That he swam to a boat with a child, or handed up a baby: several later stories; Lightoller, on the same upturned boat, never mentioned it. [read W-Smith; TO-Smith12]
- That he was the swimmer near collapsible B who called out a blessing: never established. [read W-Smith; TO-Smith12]
- "Every man for himself" and longer farewell speeches: not in the transcripts read; Bride's sworn words are the milder ones in (e) (NB L253). [read]
- Seen waist-deep on the bridge, dived from the bridge, or sheltered in the wheelhouse: the accounts conflict, and one witness was at the stern. [read W-Smith; TO-Smith12]
- A drink with a passenger before the end: one uncorroborated press account. [read TO-Smith12]
- A 1907 press interview in which he said he could not imagine a ship foundering: real, but another ship and year; never his voice on the night. [read W-Smith, which cites a 2006 book]
- Blame (speed, the warnings): the inquiries' matter; the British Report found a mistake, not negligence. A card should not have him argue his own case. [read TO-Smith14]
- "His last voyage before retirement": not established. [read TO-Smith14]

## 2. First Officer William McMaster Murdoch

### (a) Born, age, origin
- Born 28 February 1873 at Dalbeattie, Kirkcudbrightshire, Scotland. Age 39. [read W-Murdoch; derived]
- Fourth son of a master mariner; his father and grandfather were sea captains. Schooled at Dalbeattie (diploma 1887). [read W-Murdoch]
- How it bears on his speech: a Galloway Scot from a seafaring family. A Scots accent is [bet]; nothing I read describes his voice (TO-Murdoch has none). [read for the absence]

### (b) Rank and duty; 11:40 p.m. and after
- First Officer, the senior officer of the 10 p.m. to 2 a.m. watch (NB L27). At 10:00 he relieved Lightoller and was given the course, the temperature, that they were within the region where ice had been reported, and the captain's instruction; nothing about speed (NB L32). [read via NB]
- About 7:15 p.m. he had the forescuttle hatch closed to keep the forecastle's light off the bridge (NB L172, L187). [read via NB]
- At 11:39-11:40, on the open bridge: on Moody's repeat of the lookout's report he ordered hard-a-starboard and rang the engine telegraph (NB L74-L75, L149-L150); pulled the watertight door switch and rang the warning bell (NB L77); reported to the captain (NB L79, L151). [read via NB]
- Later, in charge of the starboard boats: had Hogg see to a boat's plugs (NB L163); sent Pitman away in boat 5 (NB L166). [read via NB; W-Murdoch]

### (c) Career
- Apprenticed from 1888 in the barque Charles Cotesworth; second mate (October 1892), first mate (March 1895) and extra master (September 1896, Liverpool), each passed at the first attempt. [read TO-Murdoch2]
- Then the Iquique (second officer, under his father), the St. Cuthbert and the Lydgate (first mate); then White Star: Medic, Runic, Arabic, Celtic, Oceanic, Cedric, Adriatic (1907-11), Olympic (1911-12). RNR lieutenant from 1909. [read TO-Murdoch2; W-Murdoch] Conflict: joined White Star in 1899 (TO-Murdoch2) or 1900 (W-Murdoch).
- Signed on Titanic as chief officer at Belfast; when Wilde joined at Southampton he stepped down to first officer, at lower pay. [read TO-Murdoch4; W-Murdoch]

### (d) Manner
- A captain he served under is said to have praised him as cool, capable and alert (a later author's retelling). [read TO-Murdoch2; paraphrase]
- Remembered among seamen as an excellent seaman; Wikipedia gives him a reputation as canny and dependable, without saying whose word that is. [read TO-Murdoch2; W-Murdoch]
- His recorded words are brisk and practical; to a junior officer he could say "old man" (e). [derived]

### (e) His words that night
- "Hard-a-starboard." (Hichens, BI 986; US Day 5 gives "Hard astarboard")
- To the captain: "An iceberg, Sir," (Hichens, BI 1025); on the doors: "They are already closed." (Hichens, BI 1030)
- Boxhall's London version (BI 15355): "An iceberg, Sir. I hard-a-starboarded and reversed the engines, and I was going to hard-a-port round it but she was too close." Then: "I could not do any more. I have closed the watertight doors." Asked whether he had rung the warning bell: "Yes."
- Boxhall's New York version (US Day 3 p.3): "I put her hard astarboard and run the engines full astern, but it was too close; she hit it." Also "I intended to port around it." and "The watertight doors are closed, sir." Conflict of wording only: keep both.
- At 10 p.m., to Lightoller: "It is pretty cold." (Lightoller, BI 13707)
- At 7:15 p.m., to the lamp trimmer: "I want everything dark before the bridge." (Hemming, BI 17707; NB L172)
- At a starboard boat: "See that those plugs are in that boat." (Hogg, US Day 7; NB L163)
- To Pitman at boat 5: "You go in charge of this boat." and "You go away in this boat, old man, and hang around the after gangway." Shaking hands: "Good-by; good luck;" (Pitman, US Day 4 p.3)

### (f) What became of him
- Lost; his body was never recovered. [read W-Murdoch]
- Never speak of: his own end, the stories about it, the inquiries.

### (g) Keep out
- That he shot one or two men and then himself: rumours named Smith, Wilde and Murdoch; no reliable witness named him; the accounts may describe a boat where he was not; Lightoller wrote to his widow that he died at his post. [read W-Murdoch]
- That he took a bribe for a seat: invented for the 1997 film, with no evidence; the film's makers later called naming a real officer probably a mistake. [read W-Murdoch]
- That he fired warning shots at collapsible C: a reconstruction from the disputed shooting accounts. [read W-Murdoch]
- The 1903 Arabic near-miss (taking the wheel against a senior's order): one retelling decades later; Wikipedia gives no source. [read W-Murdoch; TO-Murdoch2]
- The exact helm and engine orders in the last seconds ("port around", full astern): disputed between bridge and engine-room witnesses (NB L229). Keep the card to the words in (e). [read]

## 3. Fourth Officer Joseph Groves Boxhall

### (a) Born, age, origin
- Born 23 March 1884 at Hull, Yorkshire. Age 28; he said "Twenty-eight years old." (US Day 3 p.1). [read W-Boxhall; US Day 3 p.1]
- Only son of a Wilson Line master of Hull; a seafaring family. [read W-Boxhall]
- Home: "Hull, England." (US Day 3 p.1) [read]. A Hull accent is [bet].

### (b) Rank and duty; 11:40 p.m. and after
- Fourth Officer, on the 8 to 12 watch with Moody (NB L27). His duty in his words: "To generally assist the senior officer of the watch in all cases." (US Day 3 p.1) [read]
- From 8 p.m. working up the evening's star sights in the chart room; at the bells just coming out of the officers' quarters, almost on the bridge at the impact (NB L34). [read via NB]
- After: heard Murdoch's report (NB L79); went below on his own and found no damage (NB L80, L239); sent by the captain for the carpenter (NB L89); met the carpenter and the mail clerk (NB L90-L91); saw the mail room filling and reported it (NB L92); told Lightoller and Pitman (NB L99); worked up the position, 41 46 N 50 14 W, and took it to the Marconi room (NB L102); fired the rockets and called a steamer's lights with the Morse lamp (NB L109-L110); sent away in emergency boat 2 at about 1:45 (NB L116). [read via NB]

### (c) Career
- Apprenticed from June 1899 in a barque of the William Thomas Line of Liverpool; then the Wilson Line of Hull, where his father was a master, 1903-07. [read W-Boxhall; TO-Boxhall2]
- First mate 1905; master and extra master September 1907: "Extra Master" since "September, 1907" (BI 15306-15307). [read]
- White Star from November 1907: Celtic, Oceanic, Runic, Cedric, Megantic, Arabic; RNR sub-lieutenant 1911-12. "Thirteen years experience at sea." (US Day 3 p.1) [read]

### (d) Manner
- Reserved; in later life reluctant to talk about Titanic. [read W-Boxhall]
- Naval reports of 1916-19 called him a good seaman and navigator, zealous and conscientious. [read TO-Boxhall12; paraphrase]
- His 1912 description of the impact: "A slight impact." and "A glancing blow." (testimony, US Day 3 p.3) [read]

### (e) His words that night
- To the carpenter: "The captain wants you to sound the ship." (US Day 3 p.4)
- To the mail clerk: "Well, you go and report it to the captain and I will go down and see," (US Day 3 p.4)
- To a passenger: "You had better wait till I go to the bridge and find what we can do." (BI 15377)
- Indirect: he reported to the captain and first officer "that I had seen no damage whatever." (BI 15367)
- To the captain: "Yes, they are carrying on all right." then "Is it really serious?" (BI 15610)
- His Morse lamp message, as he gave it: "I told her to come at once, we were sinking;" (US Day 3 p.4)

### (f) What became of him
- Survived, in charge of boat 2; testified at both inquiries; died in 1967. [read W-Boxhall]
- Never speak of: the boats' fate, the rescue, the inquiries, or later knowledge that his position was in error.

### (g) Keep out
- That his position was wrong: found long after; he could not know it. [read W-Boxhall]
- The steamer's identity (the Californian or another): settled, if at all, by later inquiry and still argued; on the night it was an unknown steamer (NB L110). [read W-Boxhall]
- His 1962 recollection of having tea in his cabin at the impact: conflicts with his 1912 testimony; use 1912. [read W-Boxhall; TO-Boxhall12]
- That the captain sent him below the first time: he said he went on his own (NB L239). [read via NB]

## 4. Sixth Officer James Paul Moody

### (a) Born, age, origin
- Born 21 August 1887 at Scarborough, North Yorkshire; youngest of four; father a solicitor. Age 24. [read W-Moody; derived]
- Rosebery House School, then a cadet in the school ship HMS Conway, 1902-03. [read W-Moody]
- How it bears on his speech: a professional family and Conway training. Any accent is [bet].

### (b) Rank and duty; 11:40 p.m. and after
- Sixth Officer, on the 8 to 12 watch with Boxhall (NB L27, L33). [read via NB]
- That evening Lightoller had him work out when they would reach the ice; he said about 11 o'clock (BI 13708; TO-Moody4). About 9:30 he telephoned the nest to keep a sharp look-out for small ice and growlers, repeating it when the first call was inexact (NB L187). The junior officer took the engine room's revolutions by telephone (NB L190). [read]
- At 11:39: in the wheelhouse; answered the nest's telephone, repeated "Iceberg right ahead" to Murdoch, watched the helm and reported it hard over (NB L33, L74, L149-L150). [read via NB]
- After: helped load boats 9, 12, 13 and 16; later at the forward collapsibles, last seen at collapsible A (W-Moody; NB L118, L171). [read]

### (c) Career
- Apprenticed from 1904 in the sailing ship Boadicea, about four years, round Cape Horn. [read W-Moody; TO-Moody2]
- Second mate (1907, Grimsby), first mate (1909, at the second attempt), master (April 1911, at the third), after a cram school in London. [read TO-Moody2; W-Moody]
- Steamers on South American routes; RNR sub-lieutenant 1910. White Star from 17 August 1911 (Oceanic, sixth then fifth officer); posted to Titanic as sixth officer in March 1912. [read TO-Moody2; W-Moody]

### (d) Manner
- A stewardess remembered him on the night as tired but still smiling, wishing her good luck as he put her in a boat. [read W-Moody; paraphrase of her later memoir]
- Researchers describe him as studious and well groomed, playful in his letters, a would-be writer; he admired Lightoller and called Phillips a great pal. [read TO-Moody; TO-Moody2; paraphrase]
- His recorded words are brief and polite. [derived from (e)]

### (e) His words that night
- On the telephone to the nest: "What do you see?" then "Thank you." (Fleet, BI 17286; Hichens, BI 979-980, names the speaker as Moody)
- Repeated aloud to Murdoch: "Iceberg right ahead." (Hichens, BI 982-984)
- Reporting the helm: "The helm is hard astarboard, sir." (Hichens, US Day 5)
- To Pitman, asked whether he had seen the berg: "There is some ice on the forward well deck." (Pitman, US Day 4 p.3)
- To Lowe, on which of them should go in boat 14: "You go; I will get in another boat." (Lowe, BI 15837)
- At the forward falls: "We don't want the block. We will leave the boat on deck." (Hemming, US Day 7)

### (f) What became of him
- Lost; first listed as a survivor by mistake; his body was never identified. [read W-Moody] The only one of the four junior officers lost (Boxhall, Pitman and Lowe survived). [derived]
- Never speak of: his own end, the collapsibles' fate.

### (g) Keep out
- That he turned late crewmen away at the gangway at Southampton: the witness described a petty officer; not shown to be Moody. [read W-Moody]
- Any line from a film or novel. [bet: rule]

## 5. Quartermaster Robert Hichens, and Quartermaster Walter Perkis

### (a) Born, age, origin
- Born 16 September 1882 at Newlyn, Cornwall; eldest of eight; father a fisherman. [read W-Hichens]
- Age 29. [derived] Conflict: he told the US inquiry "Thirty, sir." (US Day 5).
- Lived in Southampton in 1912 (US Day 5). [read]
- How it bears on his speech: a Cornish fishing-town upbringing; accent [bet]. In testimony he says "sir" in almost every answer. [read US Day 5]

### (b) Rank and duty; 11:40 p.m. and after
- Quartermaster; at the wheel in the enclosed wheelhouse from 10 p.m., blinds drawn, seeing only his compass (NB L35, L45). [read via NB]
- Heard Moody's "Thank you" and his repeat of the report; took Murdoch's hard-a-starboard (BI 978-988; NB L74-L75, L150). [read]
- Heard the captain and Murdoch, the doors, the carpenter sent for (BI 1025-1030); the commutator (NB L55, L86); the captain's boat order "after 12" (BI 1041; NB L157). [read]
- Relieved by Perkis at 12:23, 23 minutes late, and told that would do with the wheel, get the boats out (NB L35, L103, L161); then the cover of a port collapsible (BI 1053-1055), then in charge of boat 6. [read]

### (c) Career
- A quartermaster "For the last seven or eight years, sir." (US Day 5) [read]
- In mail boats and liners of the Union-Castle and British India lines; his last ship before Titanic the troopship Dongola. [read W-Hichens]

### (d) Manner
- No description of his manner on the bridge found. [NOT FOUND]
- His testimony shows the seaman's form: short answers, "sir", the order repeated back. [read US Day 5; BI 948-988]
- Passengers in boat 6 later accused him of rudeness; he denied it (g). [read W-Hichens]

### (e) His words that night
- His repeat of the helm order: "Hard astarboard. The helm is hard over, sir." (Hichens himself, US Day 5)
- The order as he took it: "Hard-a-starboard." (BI 955, 986, 988)

### (f) What became of him
- Survived, in charge of boat 6; testified at both inquiries; a troubled later life; died at sea in 1940. [read W-Hichens]
- Never speak of: boat 6, the inquiries, later life.

### (g) Keep out
- That he turned the wheel the wrong way: a 2010 claim by a descendant of Lightoller, from family hearsay; against it, Moody watched the helm and reported it hard over (US Day 5), and tiller orders were normal practice. [read W-Hichens]
- The boat 6 quarrels (calling the dead "stiffs", refusing to go back, drink): passengers' accusations, denied by him on oath; after the bridge in any case. [read W-Hichens]

### Quartermaster Walter John Perkis (short)
- Born August 1874 at Ryde, Isle of Wight, seventh of twelve children [read RSHG]; the day, 11 August, only in search digests [search]. Age 37 [derived]; he told the US inquiry "Thirty-nine years of age." (US Day 7). Conflict noted.
- At sea "Ever since I was 16 years of age."; "I have been a quartermaster and an able seaman." (US Day 7). Came to Titanic from Olympic. [read US Day 7; RSHG]
- At 11:40: "I had turned in, sir; I was in the watch below." (US Day 7). Due on at midnight; relieved Hichens at the wheel at 12:23, 23 minutes late (NB L35, L103, L284). [read]
- Then the port boats: "No. 4 was the boat I got away in; the last big boat on the port side to leave the ship." He slid down a line into it and took charge; it picked people from the water. Survived; died in 1954. [read US Day 7; RSHG; Wormstedt]
- His words on the night: none recorded. [NOT FOUND] Keep out: nothing contested found.

## 6. Thomas Andrews, of Harland and Wolff

### (a) Born, age, origin
- Born 7 February 1873 at Ardara House, Comber, County Down, Ireland. Age 39. [read W-Andrews; Bullock 1912]
- Mother a sister of William Pirrie (later Lord Pirrie), a part owner of Harland and Wolff; father a member of the Privy Council of Ireland. Royal Belfast Academical Institution from 1884. [read W-Andrews; Bullock 1912]
- Married in 1908; a daughter born in November 1910. [read W-Andrews; Bullock 1912]
- How it bears on his speech: Ulster-born, Belfast-trained, a yard man as well as a designer. His 1912 biographer notes Irish humour, a ringing laugh and a voice that could carry over a crowd of workmen; the accent itself is [bet]. [read Bullock 1912]

### (b) Role aboard; 11:40 p.m. and after
- A managing director of Harland and Wolff and head of its designing department; led the builders' guarantee group aboard, noting improvements. A passenger, not one of the crew. [read W-Andrews; Bullock 1912]
- At 11:40: not in the bridge note or in the testimony I read [NOT FOUND]. His 1912 biography says he was soon on hand and insisted on going below to see. [read Bullock 1912]
- After: went below with the captain (secondary, NB L98, L135-L136); his estimate reached the captain and through him Boxhall (NB L98, L158, L233); the boatswain spread "half an hour" in his name about 10 minutes after the impact (NB L88); on B and C decks he had stewards wake passengers and get lifebelts on (Etches, US Day 9). [read]

### (c) Career
- Premium apprentice at Harland and Wolff from 1 May 1889, aged 16, five years through the trades; the drawing office from 1892. [read Bullock 1912; W-Andrews]
- Manager of construction for the Celtic (1899-1901); assistant chief designer 1904; head of the designing department 1905; a managing director from March 1907. [read Bullock 1912]
- Oversaw the plans of the Olympic-class liners; a member of the Institution of Naval Architects. [read W-Andrews]

### (d) Manner
- Shipwrights called him "straight as a die"; a foreman called him "one of nature's gentlemen" (Bullock 1912). He corrected faults sharply but in private. [read Bullock 1912]
- His wife wrote that he had the humblest opinion of himself of anyone she knew; a stewardess remembered that he always stopped for a cheerful word. [read W-Andrews; paraphrase]
- On the voyage he was "working all the time, sir." (Etches, US Day 9), noting improvements; for going below he wore "an ordinary blue suit" (same). [read]

### (e) His words that night
- On C deck, when the purser asked passengers to put on lifebelts: "That is exactly what I have been trying to get them to do," (Etches, US Day 9)
- Indirect: asked Etches whether he had woken all his passengers; told him to say the lifebelts were on top of the wardrobes; and "said to be sure there were no lifebelts left." (Etches, US Day 9)
- His estimate, relayed by the captain: "Mr. Andrews tells me he gives her from an hour to an hour and a half." (Boxhall, BI 15610)
- Relayed by the boatswain to the lamp trimmer, on the boatswain's word: "you haven't half an hour to live." and "That is from Mr. Andrews." (Hemming, US Day 7)
- Conflict: an hour to an hour and a half (the captain) against half an hour (the boatswain). Use the captain's on the bridge (NB L233).

### (f) What became of him
- Lost; his body was not recovered. [read W-Andrews]
- Never speak of: the end, or what became of the ship he designed.

### (g) Keep out
- Alone in the first-class smoking room, arms folded, before a painting: from a steward who may already have left in a boat; the painting is a later author's detail, and misidentified. [read W-Andrews; Bullock 1912]
- Throwing deck chairs to people in the water; seen in the engine room with Bell; jumping from the bridge with the captain: unnamed or second-hand accounts. [read Bullock 1912; W-Andrews]
- His talk with the stewardess Mary Sloan, and "Women and children first" to a passenger: letters and later accounts, not sworn. [read Bullock 1912; W-Andrews]
- Any line about the ship being "unsinkable", or about faults in her design: no sourced words of his. [bet: rule]

## 7. Chief Engineer Joseph Bell

### (a) Born, age, origin
- Born 1861 at Farlam, near Carlisle, Cumberland, of a farming family. [read W-Bell; London Remembers] Conflict: 4 May 1861 (W-Bell) or 12 March 1861 (London Remembers). Age 50 or 51. [derived]
- Boarding school in Carlisle; apprenticed as an engine fitter at Robert Stephenson and Co., Newcastle, from 1876. [read W-Bell]
- Married in 1892; four children. [read W-Bell]
- How it bears on his speech: Cumberland-born, Tyneside-trained; accent [bet].

### (b) Rank and duty; 11:40 p.m. and after
- Chief Engineer, head of an engineering department of over 300 men; signed on 2 April 1912. [read W-Bell]
- At 11:40: not recorded. [NOT FOUND]
- After: answered the captain's folded note, carried by Olliver (NB L93, L276); told Ismay she was seriously damaged but the pumps should keep her afloat (NB L94, L282); ordered watertight doors lifted again (Dillon, BI 3799-3801), so that the engineers could reach the valves and pumps (W-Bell); about 1:15 Dillon's party below had the order to go on deck with life-preservers on (Dillon, BI 3807-3813; the speaker not named). [read]
- No telephone call from the engine room to the bridge is recorded (NB L247). [read via NB]

### (c) Career
- White Star from 1885; third engineer of Teutonic 1889; second engineer of Majestic 1890; chief engineer of Coptic 1891; saw the engines of Laurentic and Megantic installed (1908-09); chief engineer of Olympic on her maiden voyage (1911), then of Titanic. [read W-Bell]

### (d) Manner
- One of White Star's most trusted engineers; he stayed in Belfast while Olympic was building to suggest improvements. [read W-Bell]
- No description of his speech found. [NOT FOUND]

### (e) His words that night
- None verbatim. Indirect only:
- To Olliver, for the captain: he "would get it done as soon as possible." (Olliver, US Day 7 p.2)
- To Ismay: he was "quite satisfied the pumps would keep her afloat" (Ismay, US Day 1)
- The doors lifted, on whose order: "By the chief engineer." (Dillon, BI 3799)

### (f) What became of him
- Lost, with every one of the ship's engineers. [read W-Bell; London Remembers]
- Never speak of: the end below, the engineers' fate.

### (g) Keep out
- That he and all the engineers stayed below to the very end keeping the lights and pumps: popular belief; W-Bell cites a greaser who saw several engineers on the boat deck near the end (not in the BI page I read, BI 5568-5646). [read W-Bell]
- Seen in the engine room with Andrews near the end: unnamed and second-hand. [read Bullock 1912]

## 8. Shorter entries (five lines each)

4. Words: "What is that, Hemming?" and "All right," (Hemming, US Day 7); on swinging out, "No," or "Wait," (Lightoller, BI 13820); "All passengers over to the starboard side." (Lightoller, BI 13867). Lost; body never identified. [read]

Keep out: a letter saying he had a queer feeling about the ship (never found; traced to a later book); the suicide rumour (named among three officers, unproven); smoking on the bridge and waving at the end (a 1912 newspaper, not borne out by Lightoller); "There are no more women on board the ship" (Lightoller could not name his source, US Day 5). ### Second Officer Charles Herbert Lightoller
1. Born 30 March 1874 at Chorley, Lancashire; 38; to sea at 13 in the barque Primrose Hill; wrecks, the West African coast, the Klondike and a spell as a cowboy; master 1899, extra master 1902; White Star since "January, 1900" (BI 13411-13415). [read W-Lightoller; BI]
2. His watch 6 to 10 p.m.; handed over to Murdoch at 10; at 11:40 in his bunk, awake; then the port boats, women and children first (NB L27, L32, L39, L100, L104, L113). [read via NB]
3. Manner: quick, self-assured, took charge; careful under examination at the inquiries. [read W-Lightoller; paraphrase]
4. Words: to the captain, "Yes, it is very cold, Sir." (BI 13615); to Murdoch, "Yes it is freezing." (BI 13707); to Hemming, "Come with me;" and "Get another good man." (Hemming, US Day 7). Survived on the upturned collapsible B, the senior surviving officer; died 1952. [read]
5. Keep out: his 1935 memoir where it differs from his 1912 testimony (for example, Phillips on collapsible B); a 2010 claim by a descendant that he privately blamed a steering error. [read W-Lightoller; W-Phillips; W-Hichens]

### Senior Marconi operator John George (Jack) Phillips
1. Born 11 April 1887 at Farncombe, Godalming, Surrey; turned 25 the day after sailing; a draper's son; Post Office telegraphist, Marconi school 1906. Conflict: Bride put him "around about 24" (US Day 2 p.1). [read W-Phillips]
2. Ships including Teutonic, Lusitania and Mauretania; the Clifden station 1908-11; Adriatic, Oceanic. Senior operator, clearing passengers' messages with Cape Race that evening; sent the distress calls (NB L101, L106-L108). [read W-Phillips; NB]
3. Manner: no sworn 1912 description found; Moody called him a great pal; Bride later defended him. [read TO-Moody2; W-Bride]
4. Words: to the Californian's operator at 9:05 p.m. New York time, about 11:07 ship's time [derived, NB L9]: "Shut up, shut up, I am busy; I am working Cape Race," (Evans, US Day 8). 5. Keep out: that he died on collapsible B (Bride said under oath he was only told so); the SOS joke (Bride's newspaper account, not sworn); a later charge that he held back an ice warning (disputed). [read W-Phillips; W-Lightoller]

### Junior Marconi operator Harold Sydney Bride
1. Born 11 January 1890 at Nunhead, London; "Twenty-two." (US Day 2 p.1); the British School of Telegraphy, Clapham Road; an operator "Since the beginning of last July" (1911), in Haverford, Lusitania (as second man), Lanfranc and Anselm (US Day 2 p.1). [read]

4. Words: to the captain, indirect, "I told him we would get that as soon as we could." 5. Keep out: the SOS suggestion and the fight over Phillips's lifebelt in their newspaper forms (not sworn; sworn: "Well, we stopped him from taking the lifebelt off.", BI 16778); his claim that he saw the captain dive from the bridge (contested). [read W-Bride; W-Phillips; W-Smith]

## 9. Forms of address on a White Star bridge in 1912

- **To the master:** "Sir" (Murdoch: "An iceberg, Sir," BI 1025; Lightoller: "Yes, it is very cold, Sir." BI 13615). Spoken of as "the Captain", or formally "the Commander" (Boxhall, BI 15355; Lightoller, BI 13872; Pitman: "I await the commander's orders." US Day 4 p.3). [read]
- **The master's replies:** short assent: "all right" (US Day 3 p.4); "Yes, carry on with it." (BI 15394); "Go ahead; carry on." (US Day 4 p.3). He named the builder "Mr. Andrews" (BI 15610). No case of him naming an officer to his face was found. [read; NOT FOUND for the last]
- **Officers, as named by men and juniors:** "Mr." and the surname: "Mr. Moody", "Mr. Murdoch" (Hichens, BI 980-986), "Mr. Wilde" (BI 1055). [read]
- **Between officers, off the record:** "old man" (Murdoch to Pitman, US Day 4 p.3; Pitman to Boxhall: "Here is a bunch of sights for you, old man.", same page). Operators used it too: "Say, old man, we are stopped and surrounded by ice." (Evans, US Day 8). [read]
- **To petty officers and men:** the surname alone ("What is that, Hemming?", US Day 7); men as a body "men" (Pitman, US Day 4 p.3) or, from the boatswain, "you fellows" (US Day 7). [read]
- **Ratings to officers:** "sir" in nearly every reply (Hichens, US Day 5). On the telephone a man named his post: "No; I am the quartermaster." (Rowe, US Day 7; NB L162). [read]
- **Calling the watch:** a quarter of an hour before a relief the quartermaster called the officer due on and told him it was one bell (NB L180). [read via NB]
- **Helm orders:** given by the officer of the watch, repeated by the helmsman with "sir", and the junior officer at the wheel reported it carried out: "Hard astarboard. The helm is hard over, sir." then "The helm is hard astarboard, sir." (Hichens, US Day 5; NB L144, L150). They were tiller orders: "hard-a-starboard" put her head to port (NB L144). [read]
- **Engine orders:** by telegraph, without words; the engine room answered on the dial: "two greasers on the bottom rang the telegraph back to answer it." (Scott, BI 5609; NB L144). A message to the chief engineer went by a man with a written note (NB L93). Telephones existed, but no call from the engine room that night is recorded (NB L56, L247). [read]
- **Reports:** acknowledged with "Thank you" (Moody, BI 979-980, 17286) or "All right" (Wilde to Hemming, US Day 7; the captain to Boxhall, US Day 3 p.4). **Orders:** acknowledged "Aye, aye, sir," (the boatswain to Lightoller, BI 13910). [read]
- **The nest's telephone:** the lookout asked whether the bridge was there ("I asked them were they there, and they said yes." BI 17285); the bridge answered "What do you see?" (BI 17286). [read]
- **Time:** by bells and the twelve-hour clock: "Till eight bells went." (Fleet, BI 17319); relieved at "23 minutes past 12" (Hichens, NB L35). [read]

## 12. Conflicts, collected

- Smith's first voyage: 1867 (TO-Smith2, W-Smith) or 1871 (one 1912 newspaper, per TO-Smith2). First White Star command: Republic 1887 (W-Smith) or Baltic 1888 (TO-Smith4). Age: 62, but 59 on Titanic's papers (TO-Smith14).
- The captain's first words: "What have we struck?" (Boxhall, US Day 3 p.3) or "What is that?" (Hichens). His door order: "Close the watertight door." (BI 1025) or "Close the emergency doors." (US Day 5).
- Murdoch's report: BI 15355 against US Day 3 p.3 (section 2 (e)). His start with White Star: 1899 (TO-Murdoch2) or 1900 (W-Murdoch).
- Hichens: born September 1882 (29) against "Thirty, sir." Perkis: born August 1874 (37) against "Thirty-nine". Phillips: 25 (W-Phillips) against "around about 24" (Bride). Bell: born 4 May 1861 (W-Bell) or 12 March 1861 (London Remembers).
- Andrews's estimate: an hour to an hour and a half (BI 15610) against half an hour (the boatswain, via Hemming) (NB L233).
- Boxhall at the impact: outside the officers' quarters (1912) against in his cabin with tea (1962).
- Who rang half ahead: "The captain telegraphed half speed ahead." (Olliver, US Day 7 p.2; NB L152 cites page 1, where the captain is not named); the engine-room accounts differ (NB L229).

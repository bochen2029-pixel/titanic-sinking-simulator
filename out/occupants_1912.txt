NH2: HER FIRST-CLASS PEOPLE BY STATEROOM: Her first-class passengers by stateroom: the Cave list (1912) (data/rooms/occupants_1912.json)
Source: First Class Passenger List, S.S. "Titanic", from Southampton and Cherbourg to New York (via Queenstown), Wednesday, 10th April, 1912 ("Third Proof"): the Cave list. A "Third Proof" of the White Star Line's printed first-class passenger list for the maiden voyage, with cabin numbers set against the names, found on the body of saloon steward Herbert Cave (body no. 218, one of the 306 recovered by the cable ship Mackay-Bennett; buried at Fairview Cemetery, Halifax). The original is held by the Nova Scotia Archives (Medical Examiner, City of Halifax, RG 41 vol. 76 no. 218); its six list pages run alphabetically from Allen to Young in two columns, first-class passengers and their servants, with cabins mostly on decks A to E.
Transcribed from: scan: the Nova Scotia Archives' digitised original, read from the full-resolution Zoomify images of its pages 1-6 (pages 7-8 are White Star advertisements, 9 is blank, 10 is the back). Encyclopedia Titanica's transcription refused automated fetches and its archived copy could not be fetched, so it was not read. The cross-check is titanic3 (hbiostat.org, compiled from Encyclopedia Titanica), whose first-class cabins agree with this reading for most entries; this file's counts equal those Encyclopedia Titanica gives for the list (223 names, 25 servants, 157 cabins).; accessed 2026-11-24; https://archives.novascotia.ca/titanic/archives/?ID=1 https://archives.novascotia.ca/titanic/fatalities/archives/?ID=218 https://encyclopedia-titanica.org/the-cave-list.html https://hbiostat.org/data/repo/titanic3.csv
Its gaps: Incomplete, being a proof: Encyclopedia Titanica counts 84 first-class passengers and servants who sailed but are not on it (among them the Astors, J. Bruce Ismay, the Ryersons and the Futrelles), while some it lists did not sail or sailed second class (noted per entry). 14 names in 12 entries have no cabin (Thomas Andrews, W. T. Stead and the Thayers among them), and 25 servants appear only as "and Maid", "and Manservant" or "and Nurse". Nothing is struck out or corrected by hand; two tears take one deck letter (Snyder) and the end of one name (Van der Hoef).

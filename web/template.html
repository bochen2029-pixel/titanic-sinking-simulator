How it works

The hull is a parametric fit to Harland & Wolff's hydrostatics (displacement, waterplane, KB, BM within about 1%), cut into 4,400 vertical columns. Every step integrates buoyancy and flood water over those columns at the ship's actual attitude. Sixteen compartments, split port and starboard and above and below E deck, exchange water through holes, doors and the open E deck using orifice and weir laws. Heave, pitch and roll are integrated as a damped rigid body. Four flow parameters were fitted to the eyewitness timeline; everything in the table above is a prediction.

What it leaves out

Hull girder failure (she broke at about 2:17), pumps, air trapped in compartments, the superstructure's buoyancy, and whatever drove the 10–15° port list witnesses saw: the model only reaches about 6° until the final plunge.

Sources: British Wreck Commissioner's report (compartment lengths, bulkhead decks); Halpern, A Matter of Stability and Trim and Lifeboats, Launch Times, List and Trim (2023); Wilding's evidence; Hackett & Bedford (RINA 1996).
